# 10. Is dating and marriage worth it

This section splits "is marriage worth it" into money, time, health, legal risk and relationship-quality accounts, and keeps them separate. Each account uses only figures with a traceable source, limited to official statistics or meta-analyses (pooling several studies' data together and recalculating). This section makes no moral judgement and draws no conclusion for you. The health accounts are counted by mortality risk, the time accounts by minutes per day, the legal accounts by what the statute says. These three kinds of accounting are never converted into each other. The account-splitting method, a fill-in-the-blank checklist and common mistakes are in [docs/结婚划不划算.md](../docs/结婚划不划算.md).

The entries in this section fall into the blocks below by theme, with entry numbers in brackets.

**Meeting people and dating**: meet more people (§10.1), stop once the other person has said no (§10.2), judge interest by behaviour (§10.3), take turns answering questions that go from shallow to deep (§10.5), long distance is not worse than being in the same city (§10.6).

**Whether the relationship is good**: look at your own experience of the relationship (§10.4), argue about the issue and not the person (§10.17), go to couples therapy together (§10.20).

**The accounts on whether marriage is worth it**: start with the registration figures (§10.7), discount the health benefit for observational data (§10.8), count the time account as unpaid labour (§10.9), work out the exit cost (§10.15), keep a separate account for marrying to please your elders (§10.16).

**Getting the certificate and the premarital check-up**: getting the certificate only needs your ID card (§10.13), have one premarital check-up (§10.14).

**Money and debt**: start the money account with the law's default rules (§10.10), write down whether money your parents put toward a house is a loan or a gift (§10.11), a large debt one spouse borrows alone (§10.12).

**Same-sex partners and sexual orientation**: same-sex partners should set up power of attorney and a will (§10.18), do not rely on concealing your sexual orientation to get married (§10.19).

### 1. Meet more people instead of fixating on one: whether two people click cannot be predicted before you meet
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The time goes into meeting a few more people instead of going over the same one again and again. The hard part is holding back from fixating on one person.
- In plain words: Two speed-dating studies had people fill in more than 100 questions about what they look for in a partner before they ever met. The questionnaires could only work out who generally finds it easy to like people, and who is generally popular. They could not work out whether two specific people would click. Whether you click only shows up once you actually meet. So meeting a few more people pays off better than betting everything on one.
- Benefit: In two speed-dating studies, participants first filled in more than 100 self-report scales (questionnaires where you rate yourself) related to mate preference before meeting anyone. The researchers used a random forest model (an algorithm for finding patterns) to work it out. "How easy a person is to like in general" could be explained 4%–18% by these questionnaires; variance here means the differences between one person and another, and the questionnaires explained only a small part of it. "How popular a person is in general" could be explained 7%–27%. But "whether these two specific people click" could not be worked out at all, no matter how the pre-meeting traits and preferences were combined. The same researchers had earlier run a speed-dating experiment in which participants reported their ideal-partner preferences beforehand, such as valuing looks over earning prospects. That preference did not predict their actual interest in a specific person at the event.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478-1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580>；Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245-264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: The two studies only show that it cannot be worked out before you meet, not that trying harder is pointless. Whether you click can only be known once you meet, so it pays off better to spend time meeting more people, each one an actual meeting, rather than betting it all on one. The participants in both studies were university students in Europe and the United States. The dating environment in China is different, so discount these figures when applying them to yourself.

### 2. Stop once the other person has clearly said no: continuing to pursue them is a policing offence, not "sincerity"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time. The hard part is holding back: no more messages, no more waiting around for them.
- In plain words: If the other person has clearly said no and you keep messaging them or waiting for them, that has been a policing offence since January 2026. You can be held for up to 5 days, or fined up to 1000 yuan. A more serious case gets 5 to 10 days' detention. The police can also order you not to contact the other person, and breaking that order means another 5 to 10 days' detention.
- Benefit: The Law on Penalties for Administration of Public Security, in effect from 1 January 2026, lists harassment and stalking as their own item. The text covers "repeatedly sending obscene, insulting or threatening messages, or using methods such as pestering, harassment or stalking, that disrupt another person's normal life". This is punished with detention of up to 5 days, or a fine of up to 1000 yuan. A more serious case gets 5 to 10 days' detention, which can carry an additional fine of up to 1000 yuan. This item carries one more layer: with the approval of the head of the public security organ, the person can be ordered not to contact the victim for a set period. Breaking a no-contact order means another 5 to 10 days' detention
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2025 修订). 治安管理处罚法（第五十条第一款第五项、第二款）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>（最高人民检察院转载）
- Notes: No study supports the idea that "pursuing someone relentlessly will eventually move them", but detention and a no-contact order are written in black and white. A record of this can also affect a civil-service exam, a public-sector hiring process, a political vetting check, and eligibility for some professions. The line between pursuing someone and harassing them is not about what you think you mean. It is about whether the other person has clearly said no.

### 3. Judge interest by behaviour, not by "signals": who initiates, who makes the plans, who follows up
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes little time. The hard part is changing a habit: stop reading expressions and tone, and record facts instead.
- In plain words: 52 pairs of strangers, men and women, talked for about ten minutes and then each judged whether the other person had been flirting. The judgement "they were not flirting" turned out fairly accurate, but "they were flirting" did not. A second study with 261 people judging videos reached the same conclusion. So do not guess from expression and tone. Watch behaviour instead: who initiates contact, who arranges the next meeting, and whether they follow up when you do not push things forward.
- Benefit: Two studies measured people's accuracy at recognising flirting. The first had 52 opposite-sex strangers talk for 10 to 12 minutes in pairs. Afterwards each reported whether they themselves had been flirting, and also whether they thought the other person had been. The result was that "interactions where no flirting happened were judged more accurately, while interactions where flirting did happen were judged less accurately". The second study used 26 one-minute video clips, judged by 261 observers, and reached the same conclusion. People can fairly reliably tell "they were not flirting", but find it hard to tell "they were flirting".
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939-958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972>；Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70-77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727>（争议方）
- Notes: Disputed. Another study argues it is not that men overestimate women's sexual interest, but that women under-report their own intent on questionnaires. Which side judges more accurately is still unsettled. But both sides agree on one thing: reasoning backwards from a subtle cue like an expression or a tone of voice to guess someone's intent is not reliable. Visible behaviour is far more reliable: whether the other person initiates contact, arranges the next meeting, and follows up when you do not push things forward.

### 4. Whether a relationship is good mainly comes down to your own experience of it, not the other person's qualities
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes little time. The hard part is changing your viewpoint: put away the checklist of what you want in a partner, and look at how things actually go instead.
- In plain words: What best explains whether a relationship is good is your own experience of it, not the other person's qualities, for example whether you feel they are committed, whether you feel appreciated, and how often you argue. Your own experience predicts this two to four times better than what your partner reports. But whether this relationship gets better or worse going forward could not be worked out by any questionnaire.
- Benefit: A machine-learning analysis pooled 43 longitudinal paired datasets from 29 laboratories; longitudinal means tracking the same couples over time. The items that best predicted relationship quality all came from a person's own sense of the relationship: feeling the partner's commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, feeling the partner's own satisfaction, and conflict. Among items about the individual, the strongest were life satisfaction, negative affect, depression, avoidant attachment and anxious attachment. The relationship-related items explained up to 45% of the variance when first surveyed; variance here means the differences in relationship quality between one couple and another, and 45% is the share these items can account for. By the end of the study that had dropped to 18% at most. And the predictive power of "items a person reports about themselves" was two to four times that of "items the partner reports". Adding individual differences and the partner's own reports on top of a person's self-reported sense of the relationship added no further predictive power at all.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061-19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: The same study also found that whether the relationship got better or worse during the study period could basically not be worked out from any set of self-rated indicators in the questionnaire. So, to judge whether the relationship in front of you is good, asking about your own experience is more accurate than checking off the other person's list of qualities, but do not expect it to predict how things will go later. See §10.17 (arguing about the issue, not the person) for the account that links relationship quality to physical health.

### 5. To build closeness, take turns answering a set of questions that go from shallow to deep: do not learn a script
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. It takes 45 minutes.
- In plain words: Two strangers took turns answering 36 questions that went from shallow to deep, and after 45 minutes they felt far closer than people who had only chatted casually for the same length of time. What made it work was taking turns telling the truth and going deeper layer by layer, not any script. This only shows they felt closer at that moment, not that it can grow into a long-term relationship.
- Benefit: Strangers were paired up and took turns answering three sets of increasingly deep questions, 36 in total. The questions ran from "who would you most like to have dinner with" all the way to "when did you last cry in front of someone else". The closeness felt after 45 minutes was significantly higher than a control group that chatted casually for the same length of time. It was also higher than the average rating university students in the study gave their closest real-life relationship.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363-377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: This was only run once, on university students, and it only measured closeness over a short time, not whether it can grow into a long-term relationship. The closeness in the experiment came from two people taking turns, each going deeper layer by layer with real information about themselves, not from any script or routine, and not from putting on a performance. So in conversation you can try "I'll say something real, and ask you something just as deep", with both sides taking turns.

### 6. Long distance is not worse than being in the same city: the real hurdle is the first three months after moving in together
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: You spend money on travel and time on the trips there and back. You see each other less than a couple in the same city would. The hard part is holding out over the long run without seeing each other.
- In plain words: A long-distance relationship is actually more stable while the couple is apart, because seeing each other less often makes it easy to idealise the other person as better than they really are. The real hurdle comes after moving in together. In the tracking data, about half of long-distance couples made it to living in the same city, and the other half had already ended while still apart. Of the half that reunited, a third broke up within 3 months.
- Benefit: Two studies reached the same conclusion. Long-distance relationships are more stable while apart than relationships in the same city. The reason is that idealising the partner as better than they really are is markedly stronger in long-distance relationships; the studies measured idealised distortion, romantic love, memory of the relationship, and perceived agreement. The less often they saw each other, the more idealised the view. But this stability only held while the two were still apart. Once they moved to the same city, long-distance couples broke up more easily. The study that tracked couples from long distance to the same city gave this split: about half of long-distance couples made it to living together, and the other half had already ended while still apart. Of the half that reunited, a third broke up within 3 months of reuniting. The longer the gap between visits and the stronger the idealisation during the separation, the less stable things were after reuniting.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901-919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472>；Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37-54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578>；Dargie E 等 (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181-202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367>（备注里那项异地研究）
- Notes: The participants were American university students in dating relationships, not marriages, and the data is fairly old, so do not apply the absolute proportions directly. Another study surveyed 717 people in long-distance relationships and 425 in same-city ones. The difference in relationship quality between the two types was small; what a relationship is like mainly depends on the two individuals and the relationship itself. So when deciding whether to go long distance, do not treat "long distance always ends in a break-up" as a reason. What you should prepare for instead is the months right after reuniting, when personal space shrinks, you learn far more about each other, and conflict and jealousy both rise.

### 7. Check the registration figures before you listen to your elders: what marriage and divorce rates actually count
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Reading the marriage-registration section of the Ministry of Civil Affairs' annual bulletin takes a few minutes.
- In plain words: In 2024, 6.106 million couples registered to marry nationwide, down a fifth from the year before, and 3.513 million couples divorced. The claim online that "the divorce rate is over half" comes from dividing one of these numbers by the other, giving about 57.5%. But the people divorcing and the people marrying that year are not the same group, so this cannot be read as "half of marriages end in divorce". The bulletin's own marriage rate of 4.3‰ and divorce rate of 2.5‰ are calculated against the whole national population.
- Benefit: In 2024, 6.106 million couples completed marriage registration nationwide according to the law, down 20.5% from the previous year, giving a marriage rate of 4.3‰. The same year, 3.513 million couples completed divorce procedures according to the law, of which 2.622 million were registered divorces handled by civil affairs departments and 891 thousand were court judgments or mediated divorces, giving a divorce rate of 2.5‰. The bulletin notes that the marriage (divorce) rate = the number of couples marrying (divorcing) that year / the average total population that year × 1000‰. This is a "crude rate": the denominator is the whole population, not the married population.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民政部 (2025). 2024 年民政事业发展统计公报（五（二）1 婚姻登记服务、注释 5），网页版与 PDF 版. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html>、<https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>
- Notes: The commonly seen claim online that "the divorce rate is over 50%" comes from dividing the year's number of divorcing couples by the year's number of marrying couples: 3.513 / 6.106 ≈ 57.5%. This is called the "divorce-to-marriage ratio". The two numbers being divided are not the same group of people, and the bulletin does not even contain this indicator, so it cannot be read as "half of marriages will end in divorce". To work out "the probability that a given marriage eventually ends in divorce" would require tracking the same group of married people over their whole lives, and no official data doing this was found for this entry.

### 8. Count the health benefit in, but discount it for being observational data
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time. Just remember one thing: these numbers describe married people having lower mortality, which means the two things occur together, not that marriage is what causes you to live longer.
- In plain words: People who are married have about a 12% lower chance of dying over the same period than people who are not, and people who have never married have about a 24% higher chance than people who are married. But this is only two things occurring together, not marriage itself having that effect. People who are already healthy, higher-earning and more socially connected are both more likely to marry and more likely to live longer. These figures are mostly from Europe and the United States, so for China they can only be a rough guide to the scale.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis focused on older people pooled multiple studies and recalculated them, covering 53 comparisons and over 250 thousand people. Married people, compared with unmarried people, had an all-cause mortality RR of 0.88, 95% CI 0.85–0.91 (RR is the risk ratio, CI is the credible range), about a 12% lower chance of dying over the same period. In the same study, widowed people had an RR of 1.11, divorced or separated people 1.16, and never-married people 1.11, about 11%, 16% and 11% higher respectively. There was no difference between men and women. The most conservative estimate was RR 0.94, only about 6% lower. A separate meta-analysis compared unmarried (never married) people with married people, using 95 papers and over 500 million people. The result was HR 1.24 (1.19–1.30), about a 24% higher chance of dying over the same period. The gap narrowed with age. A third meta-analysis split by sex, covering 21 studies and over 7.89 million people: for both men and women, being unmarried occurred together with higher all-cause, cancer and cardiovascular mortality. The gap in cardiovascular and all-cause mortality was larger on the men's side. Unmarried men, compared with unmarried women, had 31% more stroke deaths and 9% more all-cause deaths.
- Evidence: A (disputed)
- Sources: Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Soc Sci Med 64:77–94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031>；Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. Am J Epidemiol 174(4):379–389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111>；Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Glob Health Res Policy 5:4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes: Disputed because the people who marry are already a selected group: people who are healthy, higher-earning and more socially connected are both more likely to marry and more likely to live longer. All three meta-analyses used observational data from existing populations, which only tracked and recorded people, with no control group. The authors themselves note possible publication bias or methodological limits. Roelfs also found that the more rigorous the study, the smaller the gap came out. These figures are mainly from European and American populations, so for China they can only be a rough guide to the scale.

### 9. Count the time account as "unpaid labour", and settle the division of it before you register
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Spend an hour or two before the wedding settling a clear division of unpaid work: housework, caring for the old and young, and whose job gives way to whose. Then act on the division you agreed. The hard part is putting it on the table instead of letting it slide.
- In plain words: In the national time-use survey, unpaid work such as housework and caring for and keeping family company averaged 1 hour 52 minutes a day for men and 3 hours 29 minutes for women. The survey did not split people by whether they were married, so there is no official figure for "how much more housework comes with being married"; this gender gap can only be used as an upper bound. The division of labour can be settled differently before you register.
- Benefit: The 2024 national time-use survey. Unpaid work means activities such as housework and caring for and keeping family company that carry no pay. People who did this kind of activity on the survey day spent 2 hours 45 minutes a day on it on average, 1 hour 52 minutes for men and 3 hours 29 minutes for women. The share of people who did any such activity on a given day was 67.5% for men and 83.9% for women. Household chores averaged 1 hour 17 minutes a day overall, or 1 hour 59 minutes counting only people who did any, 28 minutes less than in 2018. Caring for and keeping family company averaged 30 minutes a day overall, or 1 hour 46 minutes counting only people who did any. The 2018 figures were: household chores 45 minutes for men and 2 hours 6 minutes for women, caring for and keeping children company 17 minutes for men and 53 minutes for women.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国家统计局 (2024). 第三次全国时间利用调查公报（第二号、第三号）. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html>、<https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html>；国家统计局 (2019). 2018 年全国时间利用调查公报. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>
- Notes: Both surveys only broke results down by sex, urban/rural and age, not by marital status. So there is no official figure for "how much more housework comes with marriage"; the gender gap can only serve as an upper bound. The figures are national averages, and the two of you can settle on a completely different division.

### 10. Start the money account with the law's default rules, then decide whether you want a written agreement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Read through the property provisions in the marriage and family book of the Civil Code. If you want one, the two of you can sign a written property agreement; the law only requires it to be in writing, not notarised. The hard part is raising the subject of money ahead of time.
- In plain words: Without a written agreement, the law's default is that wages, bonuses, business and investment income, and inherited or gifted property earned during the marriage belong to both of you jointly. Only property from before the marriage, or property a will or gift contract names as going to one person alone, counts as personal property. The two of you can sign a written agreement to keep things separate instead, and once signed it is legally binding. Whoever has taken on more of the childcare or eldercare can ask for compensation when the marriage ends.
- Benefit: The default rule, absent any other agreement, is: wages, bonuses and payment for labour, income from running a business and from investment, income from intellectual property, and inherited or gifted property obtained during the marriage belong to both spouses jointly (Article 1062). Property from before the marriage, compensation for personal injury, property a will or gift contract explicitly assigns to one spouse alone, and personal items for one spouse's own use, are personal property (Article 1063). The two parties can agree in writing that property from before and during the marriage belongs to each of them separately, to both of them jointly, or partly separately and partly jointly, and this agreement is legally binding on both of them (Article 1065). The spouse who has taken on greater duties raising children, caring for elderly family, or assisting the other spouse's work has the right to request compensation when the marriage ends (Article 1088).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人民代表大会 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典（2021 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第一千零六十二、一千零六十三、一千零六十五、一千零八十八条）. 国家法律法规数据库 <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B8&id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>；条文全文见最高人民法院公报转载 <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>
- Notes: There is no official statistic on bride price, weddings or a marital home, so this entry gives no figures for them. How much the Article 1088 compensation comes to is for the two people to negotiate first; if they cannot agree, the court decides, and there is no fixed formula. A written property agreement works both ways: it protects the higher-earning spouse, and it protects the spouse who has done more of the unpaid housework and caregiving.

### 11. If your parents put money toward a house, write down whether it is a loan or a gift at the moment they transfer it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. On the day the money is transferred, write a one-page statement saying who the money is being given or lent to, signed by both the parents providing it and the child. If it is written as a loan, have both spouses sign, and put the same statement in the transfer memo.
- In plain words: Money a parent puts toward a house before the marriage defaults to being a gift to their own child only. Money put in after the marriage, with no agreement, counts as joint marital property. From February 2025, if one spouse's parents paid the whole amount after the marriage and the gift did not specify it was for their own child only, the court can award the house to the contributing parent's child in a divorce, then decide separately whether to compensate the other spouse. To make it count as a loan, write it down clearly on the day and have both spouses sign.
- Benefit: Money a parent contributes before the marriage toward a house for both parties is treated as a gift to their own child alone, unless the parent explicitly states it is a gift to both. Money contributed after the marriage follows any agreement that exists; absent an agreement, or if it is unclear, it is treated under the principle of joint marital property. From 1 February 2025 there is a further rule. Where one spouse's parent paid the full amount during the marriage and the gift contract did not state it was a gift to their own child only, the court may, in dividing property on divorce, award the house to the contributing parent's child. After awarding it, the court then weighs several factors to decide whether to compensate the other spouse, and how much: how long the couple lived together and whether they had children together, fault in the divorce, each spouse's contribution to the family, and the market value of the house at the time of divorce. Where one spouse's parents contributed only part of the money, or both sets of parents contributed, the award is based on the source and proportion of each contribution, with the house going mainly to one side and a reasonable compensation paid (nationwide).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 最高人民法院 (2020). 关于适用《民法典》婚姻家庭编的解释（一）（法释〔2020〕22 号，第二十九条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282071.html>；最高人民法院 (2025). 关于适用《民法典》婚姻家庭编的解释（二）（法释〔2025〕1 号，第八条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/452771.html>；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千零六十二条第一款第四项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: Writing it down clearly helps both sides. The parents providing the money no longer need to hope that a divorce will retroactively produce an IOU to protect what they put in. Once the second judicial interpretation took effect, who actually paid for the house is itself the basis for dividing it; whose name is on the deed no longer decides ownership. The spouse receiving the contribution also does not have to wait until a divorce to find out what the money counts as. If it is meant to be a loan, treat it as one in fact: both sides agree in writing that it is a loan, agree on how it will be repaid, state this on the transfer, and keep repayment records if needed. Missing any of these means explaining it all over again in court.

### 12. A large debt one spouse borrows alone does not automatically become yours, if you never signed it and never acknowledged it afterwards
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Do not sign as a "co-borrower" or "guarantor" on your spouse's IOU or guarantee letter. Do not write things like "we will pay this back" in chat either, since that can be treated as you acknowledging the debt afterwards. The hard part is holding back from responding when someone is pressing you.
- In plain words: If your spouse borrowed money behind your back, and it clearly exceeds the household's everyday spending, and you never signed for it and never acknowledged it afterwards, it does not automatically become your debt. The person owed the money has to prove it was used for shared living expenses or a shared business. But for money that genuinely went into a house you now live in together or jointly own, the other side can usually prove that link.
- Benefit: Debt borrowed in the following situations counts as joint marital debt. First, debt both spouses signed for together. Second, debt one spouse acknowledged afterwards, meaning they later accepted it and both spouses now acknowledge it. Third, debt one spouse borrowed in their own name during the marriage for the family's everyday needs. Debt one spouse borrowed in their own name that clearly exceeds the family's everyday spending is not joint marital debt. The exception is where the creditor (the person owed the money) can prove the money was used for the couple's shared life or a shared business, or that both spouses have acknowledged it. For money used to buy a house that clearly exceeds everyday family needs, the burden of proof is on the party claiming the debt; the creditor has to produce the evidence themselves. But where this money genuinely became a house the couple lives in together or co-owns, the creditor can usually prove it reaches the standard of "used for the couple's shared life" (nationwide, from 2021).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千零六十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2020 年第二次修正). 关于审理民间借贷案件适用法律若干问题的规定（第十五、十六条）. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/94b6623974526df7d2430a3c73f050.html>（最高人民法院公报）
- Notes: When dividing property in a divorce, if the other side's parents suddenly produce a loan agreement signed only by your spouse, it has to be looked at in two steps. The first step is whether the loan actually happened at all. If you say the money was never handed over and can give a reasonable account of why, the court weighs several things together: the amount of the loan, how the money was transferred, both parties' financial means, and then how the two usually did business, whether their assets changed accordingly, and what witnesses say. If the other side only has a transfer record, they still have to prove the loan was genuine. The second step, once the loan is established, is whether it counts as joint debt. The general rule for IOUs and guarantee letters is at §8.18.

### 15. Work out the exit cost: a mutual-consent divorce has a 30-day cooling-off period, a contested divorce has statutory conditions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Before the wedding, spend a little time reading through what it takes to exit if you ever need to, and how long it takes. The hard part is thinking about splitting up before you have even married.
- In plain words: A mutual-consent divorce is not finished the moment you sign. Within 30 days of filing, either party can withdraw it. After that period, both of you still have to show up in person within another 30 days to collect the divorce certificate, and failing to show up counts as withdrawing it. If the other party does not agree, you can only sue. The court mediates first, and only rules the divorce through if there are grounds such as domestic violence, bigamy, or having lived apart for two years, and mediation fails.
- Benefit: A mutual-consent divorce requires a written divorce agreement and both parties applying to the marriage registration authority in person (Article 1076). Within 30 days of the registration authority receiving the application, either party may withdraw it. After the 30 days, both parties must personally apply to collect the divorce certificate within another 30 days; failing to do so counts as withdrawing the application (Article 1077). Where one party does not agree, the case goes to litigation, and the court must mediate first. Where there is bigamy or cohabiting with someone else, domestic violence or abuse or abandonment of a family member, a gambling or drug habit the person repeatedly fails to correct, or the couple has lived apart for two years or more due to a breakdown in the relationship, and mediation fails, the court must grant the divorce. Where a divorce was refused and the couple then lives apart for another year, a second suit must be granted (Article 1079). In 2024, 3.513 million couples divorced nationwide, of which 891 thousand went through a court judgment or mediation.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人民代表大会 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典（第一千零七十六、一千零七十七、一千零七十九条）. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>；民政部 (2020). 关于贯彻落实《中华人民共和国民法典》中有关婚姻登记规定的通知（民发〔2020〕116 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-12/04/content_5567010.htm>；民政部 (2025). 2024 年民政事业发展统计公报. 题录同第 7 条（结婚率、离婚率各是什么口径）
- Notes: The cooling-off period only applies to mutual-consent divorce. A contested divorce has no cooling-off period, but it takes longer overall. Court fees and legal fees vary by region, so this entry gives no figure for them. A high exit cost does not mean you should not marry, but it is worth knowing before you do.

### 16. Keep a separate account for "marrying to please your elders": do not mix it in with your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Write it out on paper in two columns. One column is which benefits of this marriage are your own, which the earlier entries in this section let you work out. The other column is which benefits belong to your elders: their peace of mind, their sense of face, and them stopping the pressure to marry. The hard part is keeping the elders' column separate, not folding it into your own.
- In plain words: Your elders' benefit, meaning their peace of mind, their sense of face, and them stopping the pressure, is real. But it does not automatically turn into your own health benefit or relationship quality. The time account, money account and exit cost are all borne by you alone. Writing out which benefits go to you and which go to your elders, in two separate columns, lets you see for yourself whether this account adds up.
- Benefit: Your elders' benefit is real. But it does not automatically convert into your own health benefit (§10.8) or relationship quality (§10.4, which depends on your own experience). The time account (§10.9), the money account (§10.10–10.12) and the exit cost (§10.15) are all borne by the person who marries. Once you keep these accounts separate, you can see for yourself whether a marriage done "as a task" is worth it or not.

- Sources: 作者经验，方法类建议；数据部分见本节前面各条
- Notes: You can also decide to weight your elders' column very heavily; how you balance it is up to you. A fill-in-the-blank checklist is in [docs/结婚划不划算.md](../docs/结婚划不划算.md).

### 17. Treat the quality of a relationship as a health account: argue about the issue, not the person, and do not insult or sneer at each other
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. When you argue, stick to the issue in front of you. Do not insult, do not sneer, do not drag up old grievances. The hard part is holding your tongue while angry.
- In plain words: People in a good marriage are somewhat healthier and have a somewhat lower risk of dying. The link is not large, about the same size as the link between diet and health. In one experiment, couples who stayed hostile while arguing had experimentally made wounds that healed at only six tenths the speed of calmer couples.
- Benefit: Pooling 126 studies and over 72 thousand people together (a meta-analysis): the higher the marital quality, the better the physical health, with correlation coefficients r between .07 and .21. A correlation coefficient of 0 means no relationship at all, and 1 means moving perfectly together. The correlation with lower mortality risk was r = .11. The correlation with a smaller cardiovascular response while arguing was r = -.13. The authors say all these effects are small, about the size of the link between a health habit like diet and health. In a separate crossover experiment, 42 healthy couples were each admitted twice, for 24 hours each time. Once they had a mutually supportive conversation, and once they discussed something they disagreed about. After arguing, experimentally made blister wounds healed more slowly. Couples who showed more hostility on both occasions had wounds that healed at only 60% the speed of low-hostility couples. The morning after an argument, their blood inflammation markers (IL-6, tumour necrosis factor) also rose more.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Robles TF, Slatcher RB, Trombello JM, McGinn MM (2014). Marital quality and health: a meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031859>；Kiecolt-Glaser JK 等 (2005). Hostile marital interactions, proinflammatory cytokine production, and wound healing. Archives of General Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.62.12.1377>
- Notes: Graded B for this reason: most of the pooled studies only tracked and recorded people, with no control group, so it cannot be told apart whether a worse relationship makes people sicker, or worse health makes a relationship worse. In some studies the link held even after adjusting for age and income. Studies that directly compared men and women mostly found no difference between them. This entry found no meta-analysis directly comparing the mortality of "a bad marriage" against "not being married", so "a bad marriage is worse for you than staying unmarried" can only be treated as an inference. The wound-healing experiment had only 42 couples. "Argue about the issue, not the person, and do not insult or sneer" is a practice the author drew out of the "hostile behaviour" measured in the experiment, and no study has directly compared it against anything else. If the fighting is severe and will not let up, go to couples therapy together, see §10.20 (couples therapy). If the fighting turns physical, or there is ongoing insult or threat, that is no longer a communication problem, it is domestic violence, see §8.43 (if you have been subjected to domestic violence). To think through whether to leave, see the exit-cost entry in this section (§10.15).

### 18. While you are both still able to, same-sex partners should set up power of attorney, a living will and a will: in law you are not close relatives, and without these documents you have no right to consent on the other's behalf and no right to inherit
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: A living will and a will are done at a notary office, and notary fees vary by region, starting from a few hundred yuan. A handwritten will costs nothing. Altogether it takes a couple of half-days. The hard part is the two of you sitting down to talk about illness and death.
- In plain words: In law, a close relative means a spouse, a parent, a child, a sibling and similar people. A partner does not count. Without these documents in place beforehand, if one of you falls seriously ill, the other has no right to decide on their behalf, and if one of you dies, the other inherits nothing at all. Three documents each cover one stage: power of attorney while you are alive and capable, a living will once you lose the capacity to decide, and a will for after you die.
- Benefit: Article 1045 of the Civil Code lists close relatives as spouse, parents, children, siblings, grandparents, maternal grandparents, grandchildren and maternal grandchildren. Article 1127's statutory heirs are, in the first order, spouse, children and parents, and in the second order, siblings, grandparents and maternal grandparents. A partner falls into neither. Article 1219 covers surgery, special examinations and special treatment. Where it is not possible or not appropriate to explain something to the patient directly, medical staff must explain it to the patient's "close relative" and obtain their consent. There are three routes to fill this gap. First, Article 33 allows an adult to agree in writing beforehand with "another individual willing to act as guardian". If this person later loses full or partial capacity, that person becomes their guardian, and the guardian acts as their legal representative. Second, Article 165 states that a written power of attorney must state the agent's name, the matters and scope of the authority, and the period, and must be signed or sealed by the person themselves. The authorisation ends the moment the person dies. Third, Article 1133 allows a person to make a will leaving their personal property to an individual who is not a statutory heir. An article published on the Hubei Provincial Department of Justice's website by the Yichang Municipal Bureau of Justice states that same-sex partners can have a living-will agreement notarised, naming each other as their future guardian (nationwide).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第二十三、三十三、一百六十五、一百七十三、一千零四十五、一千一百二十四、一千一百二十七、一千一百三十三至一千一百四十、一千一百四十二、一千二百一十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>（国家法律法规数据库）；宜昌市司法局 (2021). 意定监护公证：通向彩虹的公证之路. 湖北省司法厅网站「实践荟萃」栏目. <https://sft.hubei.gov.cn/fbjd/sjhc/202101/t20210108_3208602.shtml>
- Notes: A partner receives an inheritance in the capacity of a "legatee", not an heir. Once they learn of the bequest, they must clearly state they accept it within 60 days; not responding counts as declining it. The partner themselves, and anyone with a stake connected to them, cannot act as a witness to the will. Where several wills exist, the last one made governs, see §17.2 (a later will overrides an earlier one). Whether a living-will guardian can sign a surgical consent form directly varies by hospital, so ask the hospital you usually use in advance. If family members refuse to recognise these documents, you will have to go to court: a first-instance ordinary proceeding takes at least six months, and you pay your own legal fees. So do these documents at a notary office wherever possible. For a house bought together or savings kept together, write a separate agreement stating the actual contributions. How to arrange a living will is covered at §17.1 (appoint a future guardian in writing while still of sound mind). The beneficiaries here are yourself and your partner.

### 19. Do not rely on concealing your sexual orientation to marry, and do not treat a marriage of convenience as an easy way out: in law it is a real marriage, and the only way out is divorce
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The hard part is standing up to family pressure to marry, which can go on for many years.
- In plain words: Marrying while concealing your sexual orientation does not make the marriage invalid or voidable in law; the only way out is divorce. A marriage of convenience is also a real marriage: money earned and debt taken on afterwards both count under marital rules. At divorce, the party who was deceived can ask for a larger share of the property and compensation for emotional harm, but whether a court will grant it is still unsettled.
- Benefit: Article 1051 of the Civil Code lists only three situations that make a marriage invalid: bigamy, a prohibited degree of family relationship, and not having reached the legal marrying age. Articles 1052 and 1053 list only two situations that make a marriage voidable: being coerced into marrying, and concealing a serious illness before marriage. Article 17 of Interpretation (I) of the marriage and family book states that where a party requests that a marriage be declared invalid on grounds outside these three, the court must dismiss the claim. So the only way out is divorce. A marriage of convenience that has been registered is also a lawful marriage. Article 1062 states that wages, bonuses, payment for labour and similar income earned during the marriage are joint marital property. Article 1064 states that debt both spouses signed for together, or debt one spouse borrowed in their own name for the family's everyday needs, counts as joint marital debt. Divorce damages are covered by Article 1091, limited to bigamy, cohabiting with someone else, domestic violence, abuse or abandonment of a family member, and "any other serious fault". Article 2 of Interpretation (I) limits "cohabiting with someone else" to cohabiting with "a person of the opposite sex outside the marriage". So a same-sex relationship can only be argued under "any other serious fault". Compensation includes compensation for emotional harm, and the deceived party has to raise it together with the divorce suit; if the divorce is not granted, the compensation claim is not supported either. No publicly available court judgment of this kind has been found since the Civil Code took effect. Under the earlier law, an article published in 2013 on the Jiangsu Provincial Higher People's Court's website discussed a case where a wife sued for divorce after her husband concealed being gay. The article set out two views. One held that homosexuality was not among the statutory faults at the time, so neither a larger share of property nor emotional-harm compensation should be granted. The author sided with the other view: treat the deceived party as "the party without fault", favour her in dividing the property, and also award emotional-harm compensation (nationwide).

The Civil Code added "any other serious fault", which gives the deceived party one opening to argue the point that the earlier law did not have. The statutory text itself is grade A. Where both parties in a marriage of convenience each have a same-sex partner of their own, that partner is not protected by marriage law with respect to the other party, so property needs a separate written agreement, see §10.18 (same-sex partners setting up a living will and a will). If a mutual-consent divorce agreement states that compensation is waived, a later suit for it will not be supported. The main beneficiary here is the spouse who would be deceived, and after that, you yourself: once the concealment is discovered, the divorce, the division of property and any child custody dispute will all land at once.

### 20. If a relationship is stuck in repeated fighting and stonewalling, go to couples therapy together: waiting it out on its own mostly does not work
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs money. It is charged per session, and the amount varies by region and by hospital, so this entry gives no figure. In the studies, couples typically went once a week, and one trial ran for about 8 months in total. The hard part is that both people have to go, and keep going until it is finished.
- In plain words: Couples stuck in a bad pattern who completed couples therapy together were clearly more satisfied with the relationship afterwards, with roughly seven in ten showing improvement. Couples who were simply waitlisted and left to wait showed basically no change. But the effect fades: by the fifth year, about half were still clearly better off, while about a quarter had already separated or divorced.
- Benefit: A 2020 meta-analysis pooled 58 studies and 2092 couples. After completing couples therapy, the change in relationship satisfaction was g = 1.12 (credible range 0.92–1.31), which the authors describe as a large effect. g is an effect size, see the glossary in the README. Couples assigned to a waiting list showed g = 0.12 (−0.04 to 0.29) over the same period, no clear improvement. Couples who started off worse improved more. A 2012 review states that couples therapy brought improvement to 70% of couples who received it. A separate randomised trial recruited 134 couples with long-standing, severe discord, and ran about 8 months of two kinds of behavioural couples therapy. At the end of treatment, the change in satisfaction was d = 0.90 and 0.71 for the two approaches. By year 5, 50.0% and 45.9% of couples in the two approaches respectively still showed clear improvement, which the authors call "clinically significant improvement". At the same time, 25.7% and 27.9% respectively had already separated or divorced.
- Evidence: A (disputed)
- Sources: Roddy MK, Walsh LM, Rothman K, Hatch SG, Doss BD (2020). Meta-analysis of couple therapy: effects across outcomes, designs, timeframes, and other moderators. - Notes: Disputed over how long the effect lasts. A separate meta-analysis that only pooled randomised trials found g = 0.60 at the end of treatment, down to 0.44 after six months. By 12 months, only behavioural couples therapy still had data, at g = 0.06, almost none of the gain held. Another review that pooled 20 meta-analyses states that a clinically significant effect appeared in 40% to 50% of people. "Seven in ten improved" and "four to five in ten clearly improved" use different standards, so do not compare them directly. The 5-year trial had no untreated control group, so it cannot say whether divorce became more or less common as a result. The 2020 pooled analysis covered only opposite-sex couples, and this entry found no domestic randomised trial either, so discount these figures when applying them to yourself. Where to go in China: the Mental Health Law requires psychotherapy to be done at a medical institution, and counsellors without that qualification are not allowed to provide psychotherapy. You can ask a hospital's psychiatry or psychology department whether they offer couples therapy. If you look outside a hospital, check the provider's qualifications and training first. Do not do this together where there is domestic violence. The 2016 meta-analysis states explicitly that its conclusion applies only to mild-to-moderate cases where both parties act out during conflict, and does not cover violence characterised by controlling the other person or causing serious harm. People in the field also worry about retaliation after a session. If you have been hit, see §8.43 (if you have been subjected to domestic violence) first.
