# 18. Is raising a child worth it

This section counts two accounts, money and time. The same as section 10's account of marriage, this only splits the account open for you to see, and does not reach a conclusion for you. If you are already pregnant and need to go through the steps one by one, see section 27.

### 1. First count in what you can claim: the state childcare subsidy is 3600 yuan per child per year, paid until age 3
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Apply where the child's household registration is. One trip finishes it
- In plain words: A child born on or after 1 January 2025 (month 1), and not yet 3 years old, can receive this money. One of the parents applies where the child's household registration is. Each child gets 3600 yuan a year, and it is paid until age 3. This money is not subject to individual income tax. When you apply for assistance such as the minimum subsistence allowance, it is not counted in your household's income.
- Benefit: The plan issued by the General Office of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China and the General Office of the State Council provides: infants and young children born from 1 January 2025 (month 1) onward, and under 3 years old, are paid "3600 yuan per child per year". One of the child's parents, or another guardian, goes to claim it. This subsidy is "exempt from individual income tax". When assistance such as the minimum subsistence allowance is assessed, it is "not counted in household or personal income"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 中共中央办公厅、国务院办公厅 (2025). 育儿补贴制度实施方案. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12206/202508/content_7035435.html>
- Notes: This is the national minimum line. Some provinces and cities have their own local subsidy on top, and you can claim it stacked with this sum. Three years added together is 10800 yuan. Inside the spending of raising a child this is only a very small piece. Do not treat it as the basis for whether to have a child

### 2. Maternity leave is 98 days, and the maternity allowance is paid by the maternity insurance fund at the employer's previous-year average monthly wage of its staff
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The employer files for it. You do not have to go yourself
- In plain words: Giving birth, you can take at least 98 days of maternity leave, of which 15 days can be taken before the birth. A difficult birth adds another 15 days. For each extra child, add another 15 days. The money paid for this stretch is called the maternity allowance, and it comes from the maternity insurance fund. If the employer did not put you on maternity insurance, the employer pays it itself.
- Benefit: The number of days of maternity leave is set by the State Council. "A female employee who gives birth enjoys 98 days of maternity leave, of which 15 days may be taken before the birth. For a difficult birth, add 15 days of maternity leave. For a multiple birth, for every 1 extra baby, add 15 days of maternity leave." The money received during maternity leave is called the maternity allowance. If you have already joined maternity insurance, it is "paid by the maternity insurance fund at the standard of the employer's previous-year average monthly wage of its staff". If you are not insured, the employer pays you at the wage standard you had before maternity leave
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2012). 女职工劳动保护特别规定（国令第 619 号，第七条、第八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Notes: 98 days is the minimum the state sets. Provincial population and family-planning regulations generally add another 60 days or more of birth-reward leave, and added together it is usually 158 days or more. Who pays the wage during the reward leave differs from province to province

### 3. Know this: wages must not be cut, and you must not be dismissed, because of pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money
- In plain words: During pregnancy, birth and breastfeeding, if the employer cuts your wage or dismisses you, that is against the law, and the fine is 1000 to 5000 yuan per person. If the child is under one year old, you have 1 hour a day for breastfeeding, and the employer also cannot assign you overtime or a night shift. How to claim, see section 19.
- Benefit: The State Council provides: "An employer must not, because a female employee is pregnant, giving birth or breastfeeding, lower her wage, dismiss her, or end the labour or employment contract with her." During these three stretches the employer cannot cut your pay, and cannot dismiss you or end the contract. If you are breastfeeding a baby under 1 year old, you have 1 hour of breastfeeding time each day. The employer also must not assign you extended working hours, and must not assign a night shift. If the employer breaks the rules above, the fine is 1000 yuan or more and 5000 yuan or less per person
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2012). 女职工劳动保护特别规定（国令第 619 号，第五条、第九条、第十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Notes: If you really are dismissed against the law, the path for a claim is in section 19. The focus of the evidence is the written notice of a transfer or a pay cut, and the chat records. Keep both

### 4. Count the time account by whose time is taken up, not by how hard it feels
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. What it costs is time. Before the child is 3, the whole day is basically taken up. The hard part is that it is like this every day, and gritting your teeth for a few days will not get you through it
- In plain words: Turn the hours spent watching the child each day, and the number of times you are woken at night, into time. Then multiply by the hourly wage of the person who does this work. The number that comes out is usually far larger than the money for formula and nappies. So the split of the work is best agreed before the birth.
- Benefit: Turn the time spent watching the child each day, and the number of times you are woken at night, into hours. Then multiply by the hourly wage of the person who does this work. The number you get is usually far larger than the money for formula and nappies. Watching the child, cooking, and getting up at night are work nobody pays for, and they are still entered in the cost this way. The method is the same as section 10's way of counting housework. Agree the split first, and then decide whether to have a child
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature; the measure see section 10
- Notes: The number does not have to be very exact. What is useful is the two people, before the birth, talking through the split against the same sheet, so you do not start arguing after the birth

### 5. Split the money account into three stretches: age 0 to 3, compulsory education, and after compulsory education
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no fixed figure for how much it costs. Cities differ, and ways of caring differ, and the gap is wide. The hard part is settling down and splitting the three stretches apart, instead of naming one figure by feel
- In plain words: The money from age 0 to 3 is spent mainly on how the child is cared for: you do it, the grandparents do it, or you hire someone. From primary school to junior middle school, it is spent mainly on housing and on classes outside school. After that, it is spent mainly on the path of going up to the next school. The three stretches are spent in completely different ways. Mixing them together only produces a frightening total.
- Benefit: The large part from age 0 to 3 is the way of care: you do it, the grandparents do it, or you hire someone. The large part in compulsory education (primary school to junior middle school) is housing and spending outside school. The large part after that is the path of going up to the next school. Where the money of these three stretches goes is completely different. Counting them mixed together only produces a frightening total
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature
- Notes: First count in the money you can certainly get, the childcare subsidy, the maternity allowance and medical-insurance reimbursement, and then see how much is still short. Do not decide from a figure circulating online of "how many tens of thousands it takes to raise a child". That kind of figure has an unclear measure. You do not know what it counted, and what it left out

### 6. Write a separate account for having a child for the elders, for the marriage, and for old-age support
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. What it costs is one serious conversation. The hard part is saying it openly, and not going around it
- In plain words: Other people hoping you will have a child can count as one factor you consider. But the time and the money are entered on your own account. "Raise a child to provide for old age" needs an account of its own, because what it bets on is another person's means and willingness twenty years later. Write down the consequences of not having a child as well, and set the two sides side by side.
- Benefit: The approach is the same as section 10. Other people's expectations can count as one factor you consider. But the cost is entered on your own account, not on theirs. "Raise a child to provide for old age" especially needs an account of its own, because what it assumes is another person's means and willingness twenty years later
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature; the same kind of analysis see section 10
- Notes: Write down "the consequences of not having a child" as well, and set them side by side with "the consequences of having a child"
