# 26. Making a website or a platform: licences, filing and the server

You want to make a website or an app. This section answers four things. What kind of business it counts as in law. Which licence you must get. Where the server sits. Who is responsible for what users post, and for the money taken in. The account is personal freedom and money. The order is this. First the things that can put you in prison. Then the licences you must get first. Then the things a platform must do every day. The last entry is experience in choosing a server. That entry can only give a judgement. It cannot give a number. If you are a hired technician, the red lines are in section 11. How to register a company and how to file tax, see section 12. This section does not repeat them. A comparison table of the licences, and a decision table for choosing a server, are in [docs/做平台要办哪些证.md](../docs/做平台要办哪些证.md).

### 1. A platform should not collect the money itself and then pass it to the seller. Let the money settle directly through a licensed payment institution
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. You spend some time connecting a product of a licensed payment institution. A licensed payment institution is a company that has obtained a payment licence from the central bank. Use its split-settlement or escrow-transaction function. The buyer's money settles directly to the seller. It does not enter the platform's own account.
- In plain words: If the buyer's money first enters your account, and you then transfer it to the seller, you are doing payment business. This business needs approval from the People's Bank of China, and you must really put up 1 hundred million yuan of capital. Doing it without approval gets you shut down. The money you earned is taken. You are also fined. The boss and the person who handled it are fined separately. Serious cases also get a criminal sentence. There is only one safe way. The money does not enter your account.
- Benefit: The payment business the regulation talks about is "transferring monetary funds according to an electronic payment instruction submitted by the payee or the payer". In plain terms, that is transferring money for someone else on another person's instruction. To do this business, you "shall be approved by the People's Bank of China and obtain a payment-business licence". The minimum registered capital is 1 hundred million yuan, and it is paid-in monetary capital. This 1 hundred million yuan must really be paid into the company account. "Whoever, without lawful approval, sets up a non-bank payment institution on their own, or engages in or covertly engages in payment business, shall be banned by the People's Bank of China according to law, and the unlawful gains confiscated." Doing this without approval means a direct shutdown, and the money earned is taken. If the unlawful gains are 50 ten thousand yuan or more, you are fined another 1 to 5 times the unlawful gains. If there are no unlawful gains, or they are under 50 ten thousand yuan, the fine is 50 ten thousand to 200 ten thousand yuan. The legal representative and the person directly in charge are fined another 10 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan. The Criminal Law also counts "unlawfully engaging in fund payment and settlement business" as the crime of illegal business operation. Serious circumstances get a sentence of under 5 years. Especially serious circumstances get 5 years or more. What counts as "serious circumstances" is lined out in an interpretation of the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate. The line is an illegal-business amount of 500 ten thousand yuan or more, or unlawful gains of 10 ten thousand yuan or more (nationwide, from May 2024 (month 5))
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 非银行支付机构监督管理条例（国务院令第 768 号，第二、六、八、二十七、四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11086/202401/content_6924970.html> ; 中国人民银行 (2024). 非银行支付机构监督管理条例实施细则（中国人民银行令〔2024〕第 4 号，第六十八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11546/202408/content_6970979.html> ; 全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第二百二十五条第三项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; 最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2019). 关于办理非法从事资金支付结算业务、非法买卖外汇刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（第一、三、四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml>
- Notes: Designing the money so it does not enter your account is the only safe way. The market calls this kind of collect-and-pay "erqing", but official documents do not have this word. The implementing rules say "covertly engaging in payment business". That means transferring money for someone else on a user's instruction without approval. The interpretation of the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate lists only three situations: making up a fake transaction to cash out, using a unit account to cash out, and using a cheque to cash out. It does not name a platform collecting and paying on. So you cannot say that a platform collecting and paying on "certainly constitutes a crime".

### 2. Most websites that charge money need a licence. Selling your own goods and not letting others open a shop does not. Matching transactions is another kind of licence. A free site only needs filing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Filing costs no money. A value-added telecommunications business licence costs money. It governs "whether a website that charges users money may open". To get this licence you first need a company. If you operate only inside one province, the registered capital is not less than 100 ten thousand yuan. If you operate across provinces, it is not less than 1000 ten thousand yuan. The review period is 60 days. The licence is valid for 5 years.
- In plain words: A website that charges users money mostly needs a value-added telecommunications business licence first. An individual cannot get this licence. Selling goods only on your own website or app, and not letting others open a shop on it, does not need it. Only opening a mini-program or an online shop on WeChat or Alipay does not need it either. Charging money and opening a site without the licence you should have gets the income confiscated and a fine. Serious cases get the site closed.
- Benefit: The regulation's own words are "the state applies a licensing system to commercial internet information services; it applies a filing system to non-commercial internet information services. Without a licence, or without completing filing, you may not engage in internet information services." A site that charges money must get a licence first. A site that does not charge money must file first. Without both, you cannot open the site. What counts as commercial? The regulation says it is "providing information, or web-page making and similar services, to internet users for a fee through the internet". In short, charging users money. The kind that matches transactions is called online data processing and transaction processing. The trade shortens it to EDI. The regulation defines it as "using all kinds of data and transaction or affairs processing application platforms connected to the public communications network or the internet, and through the public communications network or the internet providing users with online data processing and transaction or affairs processing". It also "includes transaction processing, electronic data interchange, and network or electronic-equipment data processing". That means the website processes data online for users, or matches and processes a deal for the two sides. Operating without the licence, or operating beyond the scope, means the unlawful gains are confiscated, and you are fined another 3 to 5 times the unlawful gains. Operating beyond the scope means you have the licence, but what you do goes past what the licence writes. If there are no unlawful gains, or the unlawful gains are under 5 ten thousand yuan, the fine is 10 ten thousand to 100 ten thousand yuan. If the circumstances are serious, you are ordered to close the website (nationwide). Charging money does not always mean you must get the licence. A question-and-answer issued by the Guangdong Communications Administration in October 2025 (month 10) says that, following a requirement of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the three kinds below do not need this licence. The first: an enterprise, on its own website or app, "directly sells, in a self-operated way, goods or services of itself or of other enterprises", and no other unit or individual opens a shop on it. The second: an enterprise, on its own website or app, only publishes its own information, and does not provide other people with a platform for publishing information. The third: an enterprise does business only through mini-programs, official accounts, video accounts and online shops on platforms such as WeChat and Alipay, "and has no other independently operated platform". The other way round, once you let others open a shop, or let users publish information here, you are not inside these three kinds.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 广东省通信管理局 (2025). 关于办理增值电信业务经营许可证的常见问题解答（2025 年 10 月 17 日，第一项）. <https://gdca.miit.gov.cn/bsfw/bszn/jyxk/tzgg/art/2025/art_6bcbfa4f041247868c0de08e765b33e9.html> ; 国务院 (2000). 互联网信息服务管理办法（国务院令第 292 号，2024 年 12 月 6 日第二次修订，第三、四、七、十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> ; 国务院 (2024). 关于修改和废止部分行政法规的决定（国务院令第 797 号，第十八项）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11786/202501/content_6997034.html> ; 国务院 (2000). 电信条例（2016 年第二次修订，第七、九、十三、六十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5139478.htm> ; 工业和信息化部 (2017). 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第六、九、十一、十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm>
- Notes: Whether what you want to do is the EDI kind, match it yourself against the definition in the Classification Catalogue of Telecommunications Services. No official sentence says in so many words that "an e-commerce platform with matching and fund handling must get an EDI licence". The Ministry's guide for handling the matter only says "apply for the corresponding telecommunications business licence according to the business definition". So whether to get it can only be judged by you against the definition. One more point. An individual cannot get this licence. The applicant must be a company set up according to law. The three situations where self-operation does not need a licence come from the question-and-answer of the Guangdong Communications Administration. It writes that this follows a requirement of the Ministry, but the same public text was not found for other provinces. If you are unsure, first telephone the communications administration of the place. The same question-and-answer also reminds you that getting the licence does not need an intermediary. You only submit materials on the Ministry's "telecommunications-business market comprehensive management information system". The authority does not charge a fee.

### 3. Doing livestreaming needs a network-culture business licence. The licence for putting audiovisual programmes online is basically out of reach for a private company
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The network-culture business licence is commonly called the wangwen licence. It governs "whether you may make money from network performances such as livestreaming and game commentary". The provincial culture department approves it. It gives a result within 20 days after accepting the application. It is valid for 3 years. Go to renew it 30 days before it expires. The audiovisual-programme licence governs "whether you may put programmes online". It requires the applicant to be a wholly state-owned unit or a state-controlled unit.
- In plain words: Doing livestreaming or game commentary needs a network-culture business licence issued by the provincial culture department. The business scope on the licence must write network performance. A result comes within 20 days after acceptance. The licence covers 3 years. Videos you have broadcast must be kept by you for a full 60 days. Opening without the licence gets you ordered to stop, and a warning, and then a fine of under 3 ten thousand yuan. If you do not stop, you go on a blacklist.
- Benefit: A unit that does network performance must get the licence first. The regulation's own words are "it shall, under the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture, apply to the provincial culture department for a Network Culture Business Licence, and the business scope of the licence shall expressly include network performance". Playing a game for people to watch, or explaining it to people, is also handled under this set of rules. The regulation's own words are "manage it by reference to these measures". For all video materials of network performance, "the storage time shall not be less than 60 days". Videos you have broadcast must be kept by you for a full 60 days. Doing commercial internet-culture activity without approval means you are ordered to stop, given a warning, and fined under 3 ten thousand yuan. If you are told to stop and still do not stop, you are put on the culture-market blacklist. Putting programmes online is another licence. You "shall obtain an Information Network Transmission of Audiovisual Programmes Licence". The applicant must "have the status of a legal person, and be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled unit". It must be a unit wholly owned by the state or controlled by the state. A private company basically cannot get it. Doing it without this licence means a warning and an order to correct, and a further fine of under 3 ten thousand yuan is allowed. If the circumstances are serious, the penalty follows the regulation on radio and television administration (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 文化部 (2016). 网络表演经营活动管理办法（文市发〔2016〕33 号，第二、四、六、十三、二十条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/scgl/202012/t20201206_918194.html> ; 文化部 (2011). 互联网文化管理暂行规定（文化部令第 51 号，2017 年经文化部令第 57 号修订，第七、八、九、十六、二十一、二十八条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/bmgz/202012/t20201204_905340.html> ; 广电总局、信息产业部 (2007). 互联网视听节目服务管理规定（第 56 号令，2015 年修订，第二、七、八、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2022-08/23/content_5722664.htm> ; 国家网信办等七部门 (2021). 关于加强网络直播规范管理工作的指导意见（国信办发文〔2021〕3 号，第 9 项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586472.htm>
- Notes: Which business matches which licence is written most clearly in that seven-department document. A platform that makes money from livestreaming needs the wangwen licence plus ICP filing. ICP filing is the procedure of registering the website with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. Putting programmes online needs the audiovisual licence, or completing registration. Publishing news needs an internet news information service licence. Also, the text of the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture forwarded on the Chinese Government website in 2025 is the unrevised original of 2011. Citations should follow the current text of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism.

### 4. A server placed inside China needs filing. The access provider must itself hold a value-added telecommunications licence
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Filing costs no money. It is submitted together with the server at the provider. It usually takes a few days to a few weeks. Checking once whether the provider has the qualification takes ten minutes.
- In plain words: If the server is inside China, you must file before the site opens. Filing costs no money. You submit it with the server at the provider. It usually takes a few days to a few weeks. Without filing you are ordered to correct within a time limit, and then fined 1 ten thousand yuan. If you do not correct, the site is closed. Not hanging the filing number on the home page is a separate fine of 5000 yuan to 5 ten thousand yuan.
- Benefit: The regulation's own words are "without filing, you may not engage in non-commercial internet information services inside the People's Republic of China". A website that does not charge users money, as long as it is placed inside China, must also file first. Without filing, you are ordered to correct within a time limit, and then fined 1 ten thousand yuan. If you refuse to correct, the website is closed. The provider that connects your server to the network is also regulated. It "may not provide internet access service for an organisation or individual that has not filed, for them to engage in non-commercial internet information services". A provider that collects money for you is the same. The regulation says this kind of provider "may not provide access, collection of fees on behalf, or similar services for a unit or individual that has not lawfully obtained a business licence or completed filing for non-commercial internet information services". That means a regular provider may not connect a site that has neither a licence nor filing. Further, server-room hosting and cloud hosts are called internet data centre business in the regulation. Broadband access is called internet access service business. These two themselves need a licence. The provider must have the licence first. A website that does not mark the filing number on the home page is ordered to correct, and then fined 5000 yuan to 5 ten thousand yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 信息产业部 (2005). 非经营性互联网信息服务备案管理办法（信产部令第 33 号，2024 年经工信部令第 68 号修订，第五、十三、十八、二十二条）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html> ; 工业和信息化部 (2017). 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第二十、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm> ; 工业和信息化部 (2015). 电信业务分类目录（2015 年版，B11、B14、B21、B25）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tg/art/2020/art_e98406cd89844f7e92ea1bcf3b5301e0.html>
- Notes: Before you talk price, first check whether the provider has a licence. To check a licence, use the Ministry's telecommunications-business market comprehensive management information system, tsm.miit.gov.cn, and search by the company's full name or the licence number. To check a website's filing, use beian.miit.gov.cn. A "server room" without the corresponding licence means that, on the day something goes wrong, you do not even know whom to complain to. Putting the machine outside China can indeed get around filing. But if the users are inside China and the money is inside China, not one of the duties written in §26.5–26.10 of this section is reduced, and you add another layer of cost for data leaving the country.

### 5. If you let users come on and sell things, the platform must verify and register them, report the information, and keep it for three years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. You must verify sellers' qualifications in the back office, and build a registration file. You report tax-related information once each quarter. Transaction data is kept for three years. The hard part is that this is year-round work. It is not finished by doing it once.
- In plain words: If you let other people sell things on your platform, you first verify the seller's identity, address, contact details and licence, and then register and file them. After the file is built, you look back and verify again at least once every six months. Who the seller is must be reported to market regulation. The tax situation is reported to the tax authority each quarter. Goods and transaction records are kept for three years. Miss one item, and you are first ordered to correct. If you do not correct, the fine is 2 ten thousand to 10 ten thousand yuan.
- Benefit: The law has a stretch of requirements for the platform. The original words are "it shall require an operator applying to enter the platform to sell goods or provide services to submit true information on identity, address, contact details, administrative licence and so on, verify and register it, build a registration file, and verify and update it regularly". Before a seller enters, they must hand over identity, address, contact details, and the licence they should have. You verify it once, register and file it, and after a period you look back and verify again. How long is "regularly"? A departmental rule says it more finely: "verify and update at least once every six months". You must also report the identity information of operators on the platform to the market-regulation department. The tax side must also be reported. The original words are "in accordance with the laws and administrative regulations on tax collection and administration, report to the tax department the identity information of operators on the platform and the information related to tax payment". So who the seller is goes to market regulation. The seller's identity and tax situation go to the tax authority. Information on goods and services, and transaction information, "shall be kept for not less than three years from the day the transaction is completed". Counted from the day a transaction is finished, the data is kept for at least three years. If any item above is not done, you are ordered to correct within a time limit. If you do not correct when the time is over, the fine is 2 ten thousand to 10 ten thousand yuan. If the circumstances are serious, you are ordered to suspend business for rectification, and fined another 10 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan. Tax-related information shall be reported "within the month after the quarter ends". It must be reported in the next month after each quarter ends. Failing to report on time has the same penalty (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 电子商务法（第十、二十七、二十八、三十一、八十条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; 市场监管总局 (2021). 网络交易监督管理办法（市场监管总局令第 37 号，第八、二十四、二十五、三十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5602020.htm> ; 国务院 (2025). 互联网平台企业涉税信息报送规定（国务院令第 810 号，第二、三、四、十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12146/202507/content_7030983.html> ; 国家税务总局 (2025). 关于互联网平台企业报送涉税信息有关事项的公告（税务总局公告 2025 年第 15 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202506/content_7029727.htm>
- Notes: The seller themselves must also go and register as a market entity. The law lists several kinds that do not need to: "an individual selling self-produced agricultural and sideline products or household handicrafts, an individual using their own skill to do convenience labour that lawfully needs no licence, and sporadic small-amount transactions". "Sporadic and small-amount" has a concrete figure. Article 8 of the measures on the supervision and administration of online transactions sets it at an annual transaction amount of 10 ten thousand yuan. The platform also has two more things to do: mark registered sellers and unregistered sellers separately, and do not delete consumers' reviews.

### 6. You must manage what users post: a review mechanism, a report entry, and stopping transmission at once and reporting when you find something unlawful
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: You must spend money hiring people to review. This is the platform's largest long-term expense. The report entry, and the record of every disposal, must leave a trace. The hard part is that someone must watch it every day. Stop for one day and something can go wrong.
- In plain words: If something goes wrong with what users post, the platform is responsible. You must build this set of rules: registration, review of posts, review of comments, a daily patrol, and how to dispose of an incident. Put a report entry in an obvious place on the page. When you see unlawful information, stop its spread at once, keep the record, and report it to the competent department. If you do not, the fine is 5 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan. Serious cases are more.
- Benefit: The regulation says the platform "shall fulfil the principal responsibility for managing information content". What users post is managed by the platform itself. In concrete terms it must "improve the systems for user registration, account management, review of published information, review of follow-up comments, ecological management of pages, real-time patrol, emergency disposal, and disposal of online rumours and black-industry-chain information". From registration, posts and comments, through to daily patrol and disposal of an incident, every link must have a written rule. It also "shall set a convenient complaint and report entry in a conspicuous position". The report button must sit where a user can see it and reach it at a glance. When unlawful information is found, you "shall at once take disposal measures according to law, keep the relevant records, and report to the relevant competent department". The Cybersecurity Law says it more specifically. The original words are "on discovering information whose publication or transmission is prohibited by laws or administrative regulations, you shall at once stop transmitting that information, take disposal measures such as removing it, prevent the information from spreading, keep the relevant records, and report to the relevant competent department". If this is not done, you are ordered to correct, given a warning, and circulated in a notice, and a further fine of 5 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan is allowed. If you refuse to correct, or the circumstances are serious, the fine is 50 ten thousand to 200 ten thousand yuan. You may also be ordered to suspend business for rectification, to close the website or the application, or to have the licence or the business licence revoked. The person directly in charge is fined another 5 ten thousand to 20 ten thousand yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国家互联网信息办公室 (2019). 网络信息内容生态治理规定（网信办令第 5 号，第八、九、十、十六、三十七条）. <http://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm> ; 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第四十九、六十九条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国务院 (2000). 互联网信息服务管理办法（第十五、十六、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>
- Notes: You do not need to invent the review rules. Copy them. Which nine kinds of content may not be posted is listed clearly in article 15 of the measures on the administration of internet information services. Livestreaming has three more things. You must "build a platform for reviewing livestream content". You must be able to cut off a livestream that is on air at any moment. The statute calls this "instant blocking". You must also keep the livestream content and the logs for 60 days. Video materials of network performance are likewise not less than 60 days.

### 7. If you provide information publishing or instant messaging, you must require users to provide true identity information
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: You must connect a real-name verification channel. This kind of channel charges per use. On the product, design it as "real name in the back office, voluntary on the front". The back office stores the true name. The page shows a nickname.
- In plain words: If your website can post, or can chat, you must ask the user for true identity information when they open the service. If they do not give it, you cannot let them use it. The way to do it is to store the true name in the back office. What nickname the page shows is decided by the user. Verification with a mobile number is enough. If you do not do real-name checks, you are first ordered to correct. If you do not correct, the fine is 5 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan, and the site may also be closed.
- Benefit: The law's own words are "when a network operator handles network access or domain-name registration for a user, handles network-entry procedures for a fixed telephone, a mobile telephone and the like, or provides the user with services such as information publishing or instant messaging, when it signs an agreement with the user or confirms that it will provide the service, it shall require the user to provide true identity information. If the user does not provide true identity information, the network operator shall not provide the related service." If you did not ask for a real name, or the user did not give a true identity and you still let them use it, you are ordered to correct. If you refuse to correct, or the circumstances are serious, the fine is 5 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan. You may also be ordered to suspend the related business, to suspend business for rectification, to close the website or the application, or to have the licence or the business licence revoked. The person directly in charge is fined another 1 ten thousand to 10 ten thousand yuan. Livestream services follow the principle of "real name in the back office, voluntary on the front", and do "authentication of true identity information based on a mobile telephone number or a similar method". That means the platform's back office stores the user's true name. What nickname the page shows is decided by the user. Verification relies on a method such as a mobile number. Livestream selling is stricter. It is "authentication of true identity information based on identity-document information, the unified social credit code, and the like". The unified social credit code is the string of numbers on the business licence (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十六、六十四条；2016 年文本为第二十四、六十一条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2016). 互联网直播服务管理规定（第十二、十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-11/04/c_1119847629.htm> ; 国家网信办等七部门 (2021). 网络直播营销管理办法（试行）（国信办发文〔2021〕5 号，第八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/23/content_5601682.htm>
- Notes: The article numbers have moved. The Cybersecurity Law was amended in 2025, and takes effect on 1 January 2026 (month 1). The real-name article moved from article twenty-four to article twenty-six. The penalty moved from article sixty-one to article sixty-four. The wording of the text did not change. If the materials in your hands still cite the old article numbers, change them.

### 8. Do not open a livestream account for a person under 16 years of age. Handle tips by age bands
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Add one step at registration and one step at payment. First judge the age. Then confirm that a guardian has agreed.
- In plain words: A person under 16 years of age cannot register a livestream-publisher account with you. A person who has reached 16 must have their identity verified, and must have the agreement of a parent or another guardian. Tips are split into three bands. Under 8 cannot tip. From 8 to 16 needs a guardian's agreement. Over 16 needs a guardian's agreement or a check of proof of income. If a child tips behind the guardian's back, and the guardian comes to ask for the money, the court will order it returned.
- Benefit: The law's own words are "a provider of network livestream services shall not provide account-registration service for a network-livestream publisher to a minor who has not reached the age of sixteen; when providing account-registration service for a network-livestream publisher to a minor who has reached the age of sixteen, it shall authenticate their identity information, and obtain the agreement of their parents or another guardian". The regulation also requires you to "build a mechanism for dynamically verifying the true identity information of network-livestream publishers". The identity of the person who goes on air must be something you can check again at any time. You must also "reasonably limit the amount of a single spend, and the cumulative amount spent in a single day, by minors of different age stages when they use the service". Set a per-transaction and a per-day spending cap for minors by age. Tips are now split into three bands. "Do not provide tipping service to a minor aged eight or under. Providing tipping service to a minor from eight to sixteen shall have the guardian's agreement. Providing tipping service to a minor over sixteen shall have the guardian's agreement, or a check of their proof of income." There is one more rule. If a person with limited capacity for civil conduct tips without the guardian's agreement, and the guardian comes to ask for the money, the court shall support it. The money can be got back. A person with limited capacity for civil conduct means a minor who, in law, cannot yet fully decide for themselves (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2020). 未成年人保护法（第七十四、七十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2020-10/22/c_1604928959588622.htm> ; 国务院 (2023). 未成年人网络保护条例（国务院令第 766 号，第三十一、四十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10806/202311/content_6913813.html> ; 中央网信办秘书局 (2026). 关于加强网络直播打赏规范管理的通知（第 7 项）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/13/c_1777815804150225.htm> ; 最高人民法院 (2020). 关于依法妥善审理涉新冠肺炎疫情民事案件若干问题的指导意见（二）（法发〔2020〕17 号，第九条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/230181.html>
- Notes: If a refund dispute really starts, handle it in the direction most favourable to the minor. The wording of this matter has changed. The 2022 opinion of four departments, and the 2020 notice of the National Radio and Television Administration, both said that minors may not tip at all. The new notice of April 2026 (month 4) changed it to bands at 8 and 16. It did not write that the earlier two documents are repealed. The authorities have also not said how the two fit together. So the sentence "minors are forbidden to tip, with no exception" is not accurate now.

### 9. When you receive a notice of infringement, handle it in time. If nothing follows within 15 days after you forward the statement, restore the item
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Build one fixed process: receive the notice, forward it to the seller, receive the seller's defence, and publish the result. Keep a file of every step.
- In plain words: If someone sends a notice of infringement, and attaches preliminary evidence, you must in time delete the item, block it, or disconnect the link, and at the same time forward the notice to the seller. If you drag and do not handle it, you must pay, together with the seller, the extra part of the loss. If the seller defends, you forward the defence to the other side, and wait 15 days. If the other side neither complains nor sues, put the item back.
- Benefit: A right holder "has the right to notify the e-commerce platform operator to take necessary measures such as deletion, blocking, disconnecting the link, and ending the transaction and the service. The notice shall include preliminary evidence that infringement is constituted." A right holder is the owner of a right such as a trademark, a copyright or a patent. Sending one sentence does not count. Preliminary evidence must be attached. After the platform receives the notice, it "shall in time take the necessary measures, and forward that notice to the operator on the platform; if it does not take the necessary measures in time, it bears joint liability with the operator on the platform for the enlarged part of the harm". If you drag and do not handle it, you must pay, together with the seller, the extra part of the loss. After the seller defends, "within fifteen days after the forwarded statement reaches the intellectual-property right holder, if no notice is received that the right holder has already complained or sued, the measures taken shall be ended in time". That means you forward the seller's defence to the right holder, and wait 15 days. If the other side neither complains nor sues, restore what was deleted or taken down. If the platform knows, or ought to know, that there is infringement and does not handle it, it bears joint liability with the infringer. The intellectual-property department may also order you to correct within a time limit. If you do not correct when the time is over, the fine is 5 ten thousand to 50 ten thousand yuan. If the circumstances are serious, the fine is 50 ten thousand to 200 ten thousand yuan. The other way round, "whoever maliciously sends a wrong notice and causes loss to an operator on the platform bears liability for compensation at double" (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 电子商务法（第四十二、四十三、四十四、四十五、八十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千一百九十五、一千一百九十六、一千一百九十七条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: If you are not an e-commerce platform, and you are only an ordinary website, follow the notice-and-takedown rule of the Civil Code. The difference is how long you wait after the other side defends. The E-Commerce Law gives a clear 15 days. The Civil Code only writes "a reasonable period". Also, publishing the notice you received, the seller's defence, and your result is an express requirement of article forty-four of the E-Commerce Law. If the platform itself organises the uploading of pirated copies, or knows of the infringement and still leaves it, the rule "delete it when you receive the notice" does not protect it. See §9.24 (fan-translating comics, reposting films and series).

### 10. Do not casually put users' information outside China. Sending it out has statutory conditions and a headcount threshold
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The simplest way is to keep user data inside China. If you really must send it outside China, use a standard contract or do a certification.
- In plain words: Sending information of users inside China to a machine outside China is called sending it out of the country. Putting the server outside China does not mean the data has not left the country. To send it out, you must take one of four paths. A security assessment by the cyberspace department. Certification by a professional institution. Signing under the standard contract. Or another provision of law. You must also tell the user who outside China receives the information, and what they will do with it, and ask the user once, specifically about this.
- Benefit: Handing information of users inside China to a company outside China, or storing it on a machine outside China, is called providing personal information outside China in law. You "shall have" one of four conditions. One is passing a security assessment organised by the cyberspace department. Two is personal-information protection certification by a professional institution. Three is signing with the overseas recipient under the standard contract drawn up by the cyberspace department. Four is another condition provided by laws or administrative regulations. At the same time you must tell the user these items: the name and contact details of the overseas recipient, the purpose of processing, the method of processing, the kinds of personal information, and how the user exercises their rights. And you must "obtain the individual's separate consent", asking the user once, specifically about this matter. The concrete threshold is banded by the cumulative number of people. An ordinary enterprise is called, in the regulation, a data handler other than an operator of critical information infrastructure. This kind of enterprise has a line below which it is exempt. The original words are "from 1 January of that year (month 1), the cumulative provision outside China of personal information of under 10 ten thousand people (not including sensitive personal information)". If you do not reach this line, declaring an assessment, concluding a standard contract, and doing certification are all exempt. From 10 ten thousand people up to under 100 ten thousand people, you shall conclude a standard contract or pass certification. From 100 ten thousand people up, or if sensitive personal information reaches 1 ten thousand people or more, you shall declare a security assessment (nationwide, from March 2024 (month 3))
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法（第三十八、三十九、四十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2024). 促进和规范数据跨境流动规定（网信办令第 16 号，第三、四、五、七、八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11366/202405/content_6954192.html> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2022). 数据出境安全评估办法（网信办令第 11 号，第四条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-07/07/c_1658811536396503.htm>
- Notes: Putting the server outside China does not mean "the data has not left the country". A user registers inside China, and you send their information to a machine outside China. That is sending it out. There is one kind that does not have to take the three paths of security assessment, certification and a standard contract. Cross-border shopping, cross-border delivery, and booking air tickets and hotels are things that really must be sent "in order to conclude or perform a contract to which the individual is a party". Also watch how the number of people is counted. It is counted "cumulatively from 1 January of that year (month 1)". It is not twelve months counted back from today.

### 11. When you choose a server, first see whether you can bear downtime, then see whether anyone will operate it, and only then compare prices
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A cloud host from a mainstream cloud vendor is several hundred to several thousand yuan a month. A VPS from a small provider starts at several tens of yuan a month. A VPS is a small virtual server. Colocation of a dedicated server starts at several thousand yuan a year, and you buy the hardware yourself.
- In plain words: The difference among the three bands is not the price. It is what you have in hand on the day something goes wrong. A large vendor has a filing channel. Its qualification is public. When it breaks there is a notice and a compensation clause, and you can find a person. A small provider is half the price. But when it oversells, runs off, or is cut off by the upstream, you do not even know whom to look for. A static site with no user data is served by a VPS of several tens of yuan.
- Benefit: The difference among the three bands is not the price. It is what you have in hand on the day something goes wrong. A mainstream cloud vendor has a filing channel. Its qualification is public. When there is a fault there is a notice. The contract has a compensation clause. When there is a problem you can find a person. A small provider's price can be half as low. But when it oversells, runs off, or is cut off by the upstream, you have no one to complain to. So before you talk price, first go to the Ministry's system and check whether it has a value-added telecommunications business licence. Colocation of a dedicated server pays only when both conditions hold: the bandwidth or the hardware cost is clearly cheaper, and you yourself have someone to do the operation. How to choose: a static site with no user data is served by the cheapest VPS. If there is user registration and there are transactions, choose a cloud host that can scale elastically and can do snapshot backup. Keep the backup in at least two places.
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature；the statutory requirements for the provider's qualification and filing, see §26.4 (filing when the server is inside China)，keeping logs for 6 months and the graded-protection duty, see §11.16 (do the ICP filing first, and keep logs for 6 months)
- Notes: The above is a judgement from experience. There is no literature. The only thing that can really be checked is the provider's licence. If you want to save money, save it on configuration and bandwidth. Do not save it on the qualification.
