# 16. How to live after you get a chronic disease

The measure is total mortality and money. After a chronic disease is found, you often still have decades to live. This section counts how to manage those decades.

### 1. Take the medicine through as the doctor ordered, and do not stop because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money, and it saves the money you would later spend treating complications. Taking the medicine each day takes only a few minutes. The hard part is taking it every single day without a gap.
- In plain words: People who take the medicine through as the doctor said have about half the chance of dying in the same period as people who take it on and off. This number needs a discount. People who take even a dummy pill all the way through also die less often, so part of the benefit comes from "people who can keep taking medicine already pay more attention to their health". But the harm of stopping is certain, especially for blood-pressure drugs, anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs.
- Benefit: 21 studies and 46847 people added together (a meta-analysis): compared with people who take medicine on and off, people who take it through as ordered have about a 44% lower chance of dying in the same period. The original value is an odds ratio of 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63 (credible range). Looking only at treatments that really work, about 45% lower (0.55, 0.49–0.62)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Notes: Disputed. This is also the number in this book that most needs a discount. In the same pooled analysis, people who took even a placebo (a dummy drug) all the way through also had a lower death rate (0.56, 0.43–0.74). This shows a fair part of the benefit comes from "people who can keep taking medicine already pay more attention to their health". The authors call this mixed-in factor the healthy adherer effect. But the harm of stopping the drug itself is clear, especially blood-pressure drugs, anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs

### 2. First get the outpatient chronic and special disease recognition, then file for out-of-area care, so hypertension, diabetes, radiotherapy and chemotherapy, dialysis and anti-rejection can be settled directly away from home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Before you set out, file once for out-of-area care on the medical insurance app. You can do it on the phone.
- In plain words: Hypertension, diabetes, outpatient radiotherapy and chemotherapy for a malignant tumour, dialysis for uraemia, and anti-rejection treatment after an organ transplant: for these five, outpatient care away from home can also swipe medical insurance directly. You do not pay yourself first and then claim it back where you are insured. But you must first get the outpatient chronic and special disease recognition where you are insured, then file for out-of-area care. It works only after both are done.
- Benefit: The National Healthcare Security Administration's document says this step has already been reached. The original words are 「基本实现高血压、糖尿病、恶性肿瘤门诊放化疗、尿毒症透析、器官移植术后抗排异治疗等 5 种门诊慢特病费用跨省直接结算县域可及」. "县域可及" means you can get it done at county level. On top of these 5 kinds, the document adds another 5 kinds
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅 (2024). 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Notes: The condition is that both things are done: first get the outpatient chronic and special disease recognition where you are insured, then file for out-of-area care. How far the added 5 diseases have been opened differs from place to place. Go by what the local medical insurance department has published

### 3. Do follow-up checks at the interval the doctor gave, and write each reading in the same notebook
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Each check costs tens to hundreds of yuan. A trip to the hospital plus waiting for the result takes some time. The hard part is that you have to go again after a gap, and it is easy to keep putting it off.
- In plain words: A chronic disease is read from the change over a stretch of time, not from one reading. Write the date, the reading, the medicine you were taking then, and the dose, in the same notebook. When you change hospitals, take it out. You do not have to repeat the checks and spend the money again.
- Benefit: What a chronic disease needs is how the numbers change over a stretch of time. One reading is not enough. Writing them in one place has another benefit: when you change hospitals you do not repeat the checks, and you do not spend the money again
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature
- Notes: Each time, write at least four things: the date, the reading, the medicine you were taking then, and the dose. Taking a photo of the lab sheet on your phone is also fine, but put them all in one place. Do not leave one sheet here and another there

### 4. Do not stop regular treatment in order to try a folk remedy or a health product
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money, and it also saves the money spent on health products. It takes no time. The hard part is holding out when other people keep urging you to try.
- In plain words: The harm of stopping the drug is certain. The benefit of a folk remedy or a health product is not certain. That is trading a certain loss for an uncertain benefit. If you want to try a supplement, tell the doctor in charge first. Some supplements change the level of an anticoagulant or an anti-rejection drug in the blood.
- Benefit: The harm of stopping the drug is certain. The benefit of a folk remedy is not certain. Common kinds that do not work are listed as a batch in section 6 of this book
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature; related evidence see section 6
- Notes: If you want to try a supplement, tell the doctor in charge first. Some supplements interact with prescription drugs, for example by changing the level of an anticoagulant or an anti-rejection drug in the blood

### 5. For a stable chronic disease, a community hospital can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medicine at one time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Go to the community health service centre and ask whether they can write a long-term prescription. It is one sentence.
- In plain words: A person whose condition is stable and whose medicine has not been changed can get enough medicine for 12 weeks at one time at a community health service centre. That saves the half-day of going back and forth to a big hospital every month to queue and register. In the same insured place, the reimbursement rate for a stay in a primary hospital is usually about ten percentage points higher than the hospital one level up.
- Benefit: You do not have to go back and forth to a big hospital every month to queue for medicine. In the same insured place (also called a pooling region), the inpatient reimbursement rate at a primary hospital is in principle about 10 percentage points higher than the hospital one level up
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」；the same document 「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」, and it requires higher-level hospitals to open, at the primary level, outpatient clinics for common diseases and chronic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease；the same document 「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」, 「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Notes: "Meets the conditions" is judged by the doctor. It is generally a person whose diagnosis is already clear and whose drug plan is also stable. The drug is still that drug. What you save is the half-day of one trip a month, and the registration fee. If the primary level does not have the kind of drug you need, you can ask whether there is a shortage register and delivery. The same document also requires tertiary hospitals to gradually cut ordinary outpatient visits for this kind of chronic disease, and to move the same experts' ordinary clinics to the community. If you still treat the big hospital as the place you go to get medicine, getting a registration slot will get harder and harder.

### 6. Before you sign with a family doctor in the community, ask clearly which parts go through medical insurance and which you pay yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Ask one more question before you sign. It takes a minute.
- In plain words: The basic service package you sign for goes through medical insurance as the rules say. The money for a personalised service package is all paid by you. When someone sells an "upgrade package", first ask whether it is the basic package or a personalised package, whether it has been filed, and how much it costs for a year.
- Benefit: The basic service package is paid by medical insurance as the rules say. The money for a personalised service package is all paid by you, and this kind of package also has to be filed with the county health department
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Notes: The value of signing itself is that someone follows this disease of yours over the long term. It is not in how many items are bundled. When someone sells an "upgrade package", ask three things first: is this the basic package or a personalised package, has it been filed, and how much is it for a year.

### 7. Once diabetes is diagnosed, get the back of the eye checked once, then at the interval the doctor gives; check the feet once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One dilated check of the back of the eye costs tens to hundreds of yuan. A foot check is usually included in the outpatient visit, so it does not cost extra. The hard part is remembering to go when neither the eyes nor the feet feel wrong.
- In plain words: When diabetes damages the back of the eye or damages the feet, there is almost no feeling before it gets so bad you cannot see, or before the foot starts to rot. If it is found early, there is still treatment. Type 2 diabetes: get a dilated check of the back of the eye at the time of diagnosis. Type 1: start checking 5 years after the disease begins. If one or two checks found nothing and blood sugar is also on target, you can stretch it to once every one or two years. Check the feet once a year.
- Benefit: Damage from diabetes to the back of the eye is called retinopathy. Damage to the foot is called diabetic foot. Both have almost no feeling before something goes wrong. If found early, there is still treatment. Once you cannot see, or the foot has started to rot, it is late
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 美国糖尿病学会 (2026). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care：「People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis.」「Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes.」「If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered.」「Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.」<https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Notes: Type 2 diabetes needs the check at the time of diagnosis. Type 1 starts the check only 5 years after onset. The difference is that type 2 has often already been quietly high for several years. If one or two checks found nothing and blood sugar is also on target, you can stretch it to once every 1 to 2 years. A person whose feeling in the feet has grown dull should look at the sole once a day themselves. A mirror is fine too.

### 8. If you have had a kidney stone, drink water up to 2.5–3 litres a day, and cut salt to within 6 grams
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Make drinking water at any time into a habit. On days when you sweat a lot, drink still more. The hard part is remembering every day.
- In plain words: A person who grew a calcium stone for the first time, if they do nothing, about a quarter will grow another within five years. A person who drinks enough water every day has a recurrence within five years of only a bit over one in ten, less by more than half. The amount in the British guideline is 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day, and salt not over 6 grams.
- Benefit: A controlled trial that randomly split people into two groups, lasting 5 years, in 199 patients who had a calcium stone for the first time and for whom no cause could be found (also called idiopathic calcium stones). One group drank at least 2 litres of water a day. The other group had no intervention. Recurrence within 5 years was 12/99 (12.1%) in the group that drank more water, and 27/100 (27.0%) in the group with no intervention, P=0.008. A smaller P value means the difference between the two groups is less likely to be chance. The average time until recurrence was 38.7±13.2 months versus 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. The 24-hour urine volume measured at the start was clearly lower in stone patients than in people without stones: men 1,057±238 mL versus 1,401±562 mL (P<0.0001), women 990±230 mL versus 1,239±440 mL (P<0.001). NICE guideline NG118, recommendation 1.8.1, advises adults to drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day, and children and adolescents 1 to 2 litres by age. Add a little fresh lemon juice to the water. Do not drink carbonated drinks. Adults should keep salt to no more than 6 grams a day
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.8.1 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Notes: Recurrence is about 55% lower, and it costs no money, so by this book's ranking rule this is the highest value-for-cost entry in this section. What you watch is the 24-hour urine volume. The target is 2 to 2.5 litres or more, not how much you drank. A person who sweats a lot, or who works in high heat, has to drink more. That trial was in patients with a first attack of idiopathic calcium stones. Stone composition is not only one kind. Uric acid stones, infection stones and cystine stones each have their own response. A person who has had a recurrence is worth one stone-composition analysis and one 24-hour urine test, and then you decide whether to add a drug. Do not give up calcium in order to prevent stones, see §6.21 (do not give up calcium). When an attack brings severe cramping pain in the waist and belly, go to a hospital. It is not something you flush out by drinking more water. Blood in the urine that does not hurt has other things that need checking, see §1.27 (blood you can see in the urine)

### 9. Once gout is diagnosed, take a uric-acid-lowering drug long term, press blood uric acid below 360 µmol/L, and keep it there
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Allopurinol costs a few yuan to a few tens of yuan a month. At the start, check blood uric acid every few weeks to adjust the dose. After it is stable, check every few months. For the first 3 to 6 months you also take a drug that prevents attacks at the same time. The hard part is keeping on with it even when it does not hurt.
- In plain words: Gout needs a long-term uric-acid-lowering drug, pressing uric acid below 360 µmol/L. You cannot only take a painkiller when it hurts. Among patients whose dose a nurse helped adjust from the lab results, 95% reached the target after two years, and only 30% of those who saw a doctor as usual did. You still cannot stop the drug after you reach the target. Cutting foods cannot get you down to this line, and quitting alcohol only lowers it by 1.6 mg/dL.
- Benefit: A controlled trial that randomly split people into two groups, in 517 adults who had had an attack in the past 12 months. One group was led by a nurse, who explained to the patient what the disease is, then adjusted uric acid to target from the readings. The other group saw a general practitioner as usual. At 2 years, the share whose blood uric acid had fallen to <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL) was 95% versus 30%. The chance of reaching target was 3.18 times the usual-care group (RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18, and this interval is the credible range of the number), P<0.0001. In year 2, the secondary measures of attack frequency, tophi and quality of life were also better than the usual-care group. Each extra year of life counted with quality of life folded in (a quality-adjusted life year) cost £5,066. The same trial also did a follow-up study. Questionnaires went to 438 people, and 82% came back. Both groups reported for themselves how many attacks they had in the past 12 months. The median in the group that reached target was 0 times (the middle half of people fell in 0–0 times). The usual-care group was 1 time (0–3), P<0.001. The range in the brackets is called the interquartile range. The share still taking a uric-acid-lowering drug was 1.19 times higher in the target group than in the usual-care group (adjusted RR 1.19, 1.09–1.30). The American College of Rheumatology 2020 gout guideline strongly recommends starting uric-acid-lowering treatment for three kinds of people: those with a tophus under the skin, those in whom imaging shows gout has bitten a defect into the bone, and those with ≥2 attacks a year. The method is to keep monitoring blood uric acid and adjust the dose from the result until it reaches target, with a target of <6 mg/dL. Allopurinol is preferred, and it is also preferred for people with stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease, with a starting dose ≤100 mg a day. When you start, also give an anti-inflammatory drug to prevent attacks, for at least 3 to 6 months. The same guideline also cites a set of case observations. These patients stopped the drug after long-term control had been good, and after stopping, blood uric acid still stayed <7 mg/dL. Among them, only 13% (27/211) had not a single attack during 5 years of follow-up. On diet, people who limited alcohol or quit it had blood uric acid 1.6 mg/dL lower than people who did not limit it, and one serving of beer raised blood uric acid by 0.16 mg/dL. A healthy diet, a Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet had even smaller effects. Which uric-acid-lowering drug to prefer is disputed. The American College of Rheumatology guideline strongly recommends allopurinol as the first choice, because when the dose is high enough it works, is tolerated, is safe and is cheap. It also conditionally suggests: if a person taking febuxostat has a history of cardiovascular disease, or has a new cardiovascular event, switch off it when another drug is available. This recommendation matches the black-box warning the FDA added for febuxostat. One basis is the CARES trial: 6190 gout patients who also had cardiovascular disease were randomly given febuxostat or allopurinol. The risk of death from any cause in the febuxostat group was 1.22 times the allopurinol group (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, about 22% higher). Cardiovascular death was 1.34 times (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). The European FAST trial reached a different conclusion. It enrolled 6128 patients over 60 who had cardiovascular risk factors, and major cardiovascular events on febuxostat were not more than on allopurinol (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03). Deaths were 7.2% in the febuxostat group and 8.6% in the allopurinol group. For people taking a drug that promotes uric acid leaving through the kidney, the American College of Rheumatology conditionally advises against alkalising the urine, because there is no evidence it works and the certainty of the evidence is very low. It also requires telling these people to drink more water. People already known to have kidney stones, or stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease, should not use this kind of drug
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>；Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333>；FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. Arthritis Care & Research, 72(6), 744-760. <https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.24180>；White WB, Saag KG, Becker MA, et al.; CARES Investigators (2018). Cardiovascular Safety of Febuxostat or Allopurinol in Patients with Gout. New England Journal of Medicine, 378(13), 1200-1210. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1710895>；Mackenzie IS, Ford I, Nuki G, et al.; FAST Study Group (2020). Long-term cardiovascular safety of febuxostat compared with allopurinol in patients with gout (FAST): a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial. Lancet, 396(10264), 1745-1757. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32234-0>；中华医学会内分泌学分会 (2020). 中国高尿酸血症与痛风诊疗指南(2019). 中华内分泌代谢杂志, 36(1), 1-13. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1000-6699.2020.01.001>
- Notes: Disputed: which drug to use to lower uric acid differs across countries' guidelines. The US guideline prefers allopurinol, and a person with cardiovascular disease should switch off febuxostat if they can, but the European FAST trial did not see febuxostat as more dangerous. In China you can still buy benzbromarone. It is a drug that promotes uric acid leaving the body. The Chinese Medical Association's guideline (the 2019 edition and the 2024 update) gives its own recommendations on drug choice and on alkalising the urine. Whether to add sodium bicarbonate to alkalise the urine while taking benzbromarone: the US guideline holds there is no evidence. Follow your doctor's view, and drinking more water cannot be skipped. **Before a Han Chinese person uses allopurinol, it is worth testing HLA-B\*5801 first.** It is an inborn gene type that governs the immune response. You test once, and the result does not change for life. People who carry this gene are 7.4% among Han Chinese, Koreans and Thais, and only 0.7% among white people and people of Hispanic origin. After Asian people and people of African descent use allopurinol, the risk of allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome is 3 times that of white people. When this illness is severe, the skin peels over a large area, and it can kill. The American College of Rheumatology 2020 gout guideline suggests, for these two groups, that a test before the drug can be considered. **When you first start lowering uric acid, an attack is instead more easily triggered**, so the guideline explicitly requires an anti-inflammatory preventive drug at the same time for the first 3 to 6 months. "I took the uric-acid-lowering drug and it hurt more, so I stopped" is the most common way this fails. When it hurts, what you should do is add an anti-inflammatory as the doctor ordered, not stop the uric-acid-lowering drug. The target value is written differently in different places: <6 mg/dL is about 360 µmol/L, and Chinese lab reports mostly use µmol/L. High uric acid found on a check-up when you have never had an attack is a different matter, see §6.19 (high uric acid on a check-up but you have never had pain). Sugary drinks and alcohol, see §2.7 and §2.19 (do not drink sugary drinks, drink less or do not drink)
