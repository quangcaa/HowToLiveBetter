# 30. Children after they start school

Section 20 is about the child's first year after birth. Section 18 counts how much money and how much time raising a child takes. The stretch from 3 to 18 is written in this section. The entry on screens by age starts from age 0, because the official bands are 0–3, 3–6, and primary and secondary school. Splitting them makes the entry harder to use. This section answers one question. When "do not let schoolwork slip" and the body collide, which things cannot wait. These things have a time window. Once it has passed, you cannot make it up. Whether schoolwork should be that intense, this section does not discuss. It only gives the windows and the numbers.

The first eight entries, §30.12 (dilated refraction), §30.13 (pit and fissure sealant) and §30.15 to §30.18 count death, and harm that really stays in the body and the mind. Among them, §30.16 and §30.17 are about how the family manages the child: do not hit and do not scold, and go to a parenting class that teaches concrete methods. §30.18 is about not sending the child into a closed school that claims to break internet addiction. §30.9 (do not buy a product that "cures myopia") counts money. §30.10 (the written rules on sleep, homework, sport and ranking), §30.11 (suspending school while the student status is kept) and §30.14 (a child who loves games) count time. Drowning, traffic, helmets and the HPV vaccine, see section 1. Infants, see section 20. A child being cheated, and topping up a game, see section 5. Red lines a minor himself should not step on, see section 9. How to recognise an emergency and how to call a car, see section 13. A child after a parent has died, see section 29. None of these is repeated in this section.

### 1. If a child says there is sudden severe pain, and it keeps getting worse, go to hospital at once. Do not let him hold on until school ends
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Take half a day off and take the child to the emergency department.
- In plain words: Some illnesses are counted by the hour. Sudden severe pain in one side of a boy's scrotum is testicular torsion. That is the vessel the testicle hangs on twisting shut, so the blood supply is cut off. Treated within 6 hours, the share of testicles saved is 97.2%. Dragged out to 19 to 24 hours, only 42.5% is left. This pain cannot wait until school ends, and it cannot wait until the exam is over.
- Benefit: By the rules, 30 groups of case reports and 2116 patients with testicular torsion were gathered and looked at together. Of them, 1283 were counted in bands of 6 hours. Counted from when the pain started, the share of testicles saved was 97.2% at 0 to 6 hours. It was 79.3% at 7 to 12 hours. It was 61.3% at 13 to 18 hours. It was 42.5% at 19 to 24 hours. It was 24.4% at 25 to 48 hours. Past 48 hours it was 7.4%. Merged into long stretches, it was 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% at 13 to 24 hours, and 18.1% past 24 hours. The authors' conclusion is that the time in which it can still be saved can be longer than the 6 to 8 hours usually spoken of. So even if it has already hurt for a long time, it still has to be treated. Do not skip it because "it is late anyway"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Notes: Tell a boy in advance: if it suddenly hurts badly down there, say so at once. There is no need to be embarrassed. Testicular torsion is common in boys at puberty. Quite a few hold on because it is hard to speak of. Also counted by the hour are acute appendicitis (dragged out, it can perforate), diabetic ketoacidosis, and meningitis. This section has not checked, item by item, "how many hours of delay ruins how much" for these. The signal they share is this. It starts suddenly, it keeps getting worse, and it is not like any time before. How to recognise an emergency and how to call a car, see section 13.

### 2. Do not put off a treatment that should be done in order to "wait until the exams are over". Some windows follow bone age, not the exam timetable
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. What you have to decide is only whether to do it early or late. Leave and follow-up visits take time. The cost of the treatment itself is counted separately. The hard part is that the brace has to be worn at least 18 hours a day, and the child has to keep putting up with it.
- In plain words: Scoliosis is the spine bending to the side. A brace is a hard-shell vest. Worn on the body, it holds the spine. Worn while the bone is still growing, 75% of the children did not bend as far as needing an operation. Among those who were only watched and did not wear one, it was only 42%. The time you can wear it follows the bone finishing its growth. It does not follow the senior-high or college entrance exams. Waiting until the exams are over often leaves only an operation.
- Benefit: A trial done by several hospitals together found 242 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis who met the conditions for a brace. Of them, 116 were assigned by lot, and 126 chose for themselves whether to wear one. Success and failure were defined in advance. A curve that grew past 50 degrees counted as treatment failure. Bone finished growing and still not at that degree counted as treatment success. It was counted by which group they were first assigned to, whether or not they later really wore it enough. The success rate in the lot-assigned brace group was 75%. The lot-assigned observation-only group was 42% (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16). The chance of success was about 4 times that of observation only. Putting the lot-assigned and the self-chosen groups together, it was 72% against 48% (after evening out the difference the two groups already had, OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46, still about 1.9 times). The more hours worn each day, the higher the success rate (P&lt;0.001, this link is unlikely to be chance). The trial was stopped early because the brace really worked
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: Scoliosis is the example here because it has a trial that assigned people by lot, it has a clear time window, and it is exactly one of the key items on the student health check (see §30.7). The same kind of thing includes squint, amblyopia, tooth decay, and an operation such as for congenital heart disease that can pick its time. What they share is that the later you do it, the worse the effect, or the larger the price. Ask the specialist for the specific window. If you are afraid of missing class, during compulsory education the child can suspend school, for 1 year at most, and the student status is kept. See §30.11.

### 3. If a child is bullied, report it to the school the same day and ask for it to be handled in writing. If it involves hitting, taking money or spreading rumours, call the police directly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Find the class teacher, and also the school's organisation for handling student bullying. If you call the police, call 110. The hard part is standing up to advice of the kind "do not make a big thing of it".
- In plain words: A child bullied by peers is about 2.2 times as likely to have a suicidal thought as one who is not bullied, and about 2.6 times as likely to have had a suicidal act. Once the school receives a report it has to investigate at once. If it finds bullying, it has to give a punishment. A serious case may not be hidden. It must be reported to the public-security organ. So report it the same day, ask for the finding procedure, and leave a written record.
- Benefit: 34 studies and 284,375 children and adolescents were counted together. Being bullied by peers was related to having a suicidal thought, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37). The chance of a thought appearing was about 2.2 times that of not being bullied. Another 9 studies and 70,102 people linked being bullied with a suicide attempt, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34), about 2.6 times. Sex, age and the quality of the studies themselves differed, and the result was the same. The link between cyberbullying and suicidal thoughts was stronger than for offline bullying. Article 21 of the Provisions on the Protection of Minors in Schools lists five kinds of act: beating, kicking, slapping, grabbing and biting, pushing and bumping, pulling; insulting, mocking, and giving an insulting nickname; snatching, forcing something away, or deliberately destroying property; maliciously shutting someone out and isolating them; inventing facts to defame, or spreading rumours, through the network. The same article also provides that where the side that has the advantage in age, body or numbers does these things on purpose or out of malice, and causes personal injury, property loss or mental harm, it can be found to be bullying. Article 22 requires staff to ask in time and report if they find a student's mood is not normal, or the body is injured. Article 23 requires the school, once it receives a report, to investigate at once, submit it to the bullying-handling organisation for a finding and a disposition, and notify the parents to take part. Where it is found to be bullying, an educational punishment or a disciplinary sanction has to be given. Serious bullying that breaks public-security administration, or that is suspected of a crime, may not be concealed, and shall be reported in time to the public-security organ and the education department (nationwide, in force from 1 September 2021, month 9)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Notes: If the child has an injury he cannot explain, suddenly will not go to school, or keeps losing things, first ask along the signals in article 22. This analysis that puts many studies together only followed and recorded. It had no grouped comparison. So it cannot prove that bullying alone causes suicide. The direction and the size of the risk are clear. The Plan on Strengthening Comprehensive Handling of Bullying among Primary and Secondary Pupils separately requires a strict distinction between bullying and pupils fooling around with each other. So the school has a duty to make a finding. It cannot finish with one sentence, "children are just playing".

### 4. Let the child spend a full 2 hours outdoors every day. This is at present the only way to prevent myopia that a randomised trial supports
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. But time has to be cleared every day, and most of it has to be squeezed out of homework and online classes. The hard part is doing it every day, not a few times and then stopping.
- In plain words: First-grade primary pupils who had 40 extra minutes of outdoor class a day had new myopia fall, three years later, from about four tenths to about three tenths. The amount the health commission gives is no less than 2 hours outdoors in the daytime every day, or 14 hours added up over a week. An overcast day still counts. Standing outside during the break also counts.
- Benefit: First-grade pupils in 12 primary schools in Guangzhou were assigned by lot with the school as the unit (952 people in the intervention schools, 951 in the control schools, mean age 6.6). The intervention was one extra 40-minute outdoor activity class on each school day, and parents were encouraged to take the child out more after school. Over 3 years, the share with new myopia was 30.4% in the intervention group, 259 cases among 853 who qualified, and 39.5% in the control group, 287 among 726. The difference between the two groups was -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P&lt;0.001). The real gap was roughly between 4 and 14 percentage points fewer. The deepening of the degree over 3 years (change in spherical equivalent) was -1.42 D against -1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group deepened a little less. D is the unit for measuring the degree. Growth of the axial length (the length of the eyeball from front to back) showed no difference between the two groups (P=0.07). The Ten Items of Core Knowledge on Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents requires children and adolescents to have no less than 2 hours of outdoor activity in the daytime every day, or 14 hours added up in a week. It notes that even on an overcast day, outdoor activity still has a protective effect, and it requires avoiding the period of high temperature and strong sun after noon
- Evidence: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: This trial ran for only 3 years, and the children who took part all started from age 6. A 9.1 percentage-point gap in the share with myopia can keep only some people from becoming myopic. It cannot guarantee that someone will not become myopic. The same ten items of core knowledge also give a few methods. Reading and writing should keep "one chi, one fist, one cun". After 20 minutes of close-up eye use, look into the distance for more than 20 seconds. For primary and secondary pupils who use an electronic screen not for study, one time is no more than 15 minutes, and a day adds up to no more than 1 hour. "Staying outdoors" and "having a PE class" are not the same thing. Standing outside during the break also counts.

### 5. No screen from 0 to 3. From 3 to 6, try not to give one. For primary and secondary pupils, non-study use is no more than 1 hour a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The hard part is taking the screen out of the child's hands, and also the parent himself looking at it less in front of the child.
- In plain words: The national health commission requires no screen from 0 to 3, trying not to let a child of 3 to 6 touch one, and, for primary and secondary pupils using one not for study, no more than 15 minutes at a time and no more than 1 hour in a day. The reason given is that using the eyes at close range for a long time makes myopia come early. The evidence linking screens and myopia is not this hard. What has really been tried and shown to work is 2 hours outdoors every day.
- Benefit: The original wording of item 4 of the Ten Items of Core Knowledge on Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents is: "infants and young children aged 0–3 do not use screen-type electronic products such as phones, tablets and computers; young children aged 3–6 should as far as possible avoid contact with and use of screen-type electronic products such as phones, tablets and computers; for primary and secondary pupils, a single use of an electronic screen for a non-study purpose should not exceed 15 minutes, and the total time in a day should not exceed 1 hour". The same item writes that "using electronic screen-type products at close range for a long time easily uses up a child's reserve of farsightedness, and is an important reason myopia starts early and is common in children and adolescents". The 2018 implementation plan of the Ministry of Education and other departments, eight departments in all, gives parents the same requirement, and adds one sentence: "the younger the age, the shorter the continuous time on electronic products should be". The research evidence is much weaker. One systematic review took in 33 studies, and 11 of them entered the pooled calculation. The people ranged from 3 months to 33 years. Counting phones and tablets only, the odds ratio for myopia was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Counted together with computers it was 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors' conclusion only goes as far as "may be related", and they write that not one of the 33 studies had a reliable measurement of screen time
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; 教育部等八部门 (2018). 综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: It is graded B because what the officials give is a rule in bands, not a number measured by a trial. The lower bound of the odds ratio from the research sits right on 1.00, and the authors only dare to say "may be related". So treat it as a house rule that is easy to carry out. Do not treat it as "if he does not look at a screen he will not become myopic". What a lot-assigned trial really supports is §30.4 (2 hours outdoors). Eyes should also be checked before school age. The same document writes that ages 1–3, 4–6, and after age 7 should all have regular refraction screening, to see how much farsighted reserve is left. How to check, see §30.12 (dilated refraction). The link between screens and a child's language ability has also been pooled. 42 studies and 18905 people: the longer the screen time, the weaker the language ability (r=-0.14, 95% CI -0.18 to -0.10). But watching together, and watching programmes with educational content, went the other way and was positively related (r=0.16 and 0.13). This is a link, not a cause. It only shows that using a screen in place of talking with the child does not pay.

### 6. Treat myopia as damage that stays: the deeper the degree, the more retinal detachment, macular disease and glaucoma there will be later
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. There is nothing to do in this entry itself. What there is to do is in §30.4 and §30.5 (2 hours outdoors, and screens managed by age).
- In plain words: Myopia does not get better by itself, and the degree cannot be brought back down. It is also not only a matter of "wearing a pair of glasses". Compared with people who are not myopic, the chance of myopic macular disease is more than ten times higher in mild myopia, and several hundred times higher above 600 degrees. Retinal detachment is about 3 times higher in mild myopia, and about 13 times higher above 600 degrees. So the earlier a child becomes myopic, and the faster it rises, the larger the risk he will carry later.
- Benefit: One systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies from before June 2019 (month 6), in three bands by degree: mild (-0.5 to -3.00 D), moderate (-3.00 to -6.00 D), and high (≤-6.00 D, above 600 degrees). Compared with people who are not myopic, the odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration were, in order, 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48), and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34). For retinal detachment they were, in order, 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract they were, in order, 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28), and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma, mild was 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91), and moderate and high together were 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52). The odds ratios for vision impairment appearing above age 60 were, for the three bands in order, 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten Items of Core Knowledge on Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents says the same thing: above 600 degrees is high myopia. In this group, the rates of blinding eye diseases such as cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic macular disease, retinal detachment and myopic optic neuropathy are clearly higher than in other people, and "myopia can be prevented and controlled, and it cannot be reversed"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: What was pooled in were all observational studies, and the odds ratios are also unusually large. The credible range for high myopia and macular degeneration runs from 230 to 3104, which means the studies underneath differ a lot. The numbers can only be read for their size. They cannot be used as exact values. Most of these complications only show up in middle and old age. Myopia itself is not a disability. After correction with glasses, the great majority of people have normal vision. But myopia is not finished once a pair of glasses is fitted. It is worth the effort to delay it and control it. How to follow up after it is confirmed, see §30.12 (dilated refraction). Do not buy a product that claims it can cure it, see §30.9 (curing myopia).

### 7. Read the report from that yearly student health check yourself, and take any abnormal item to a hospital the same year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. During compulsory education the check is paid from the school's public funds. Parents do not pay extra.
- In plain words: Primary and secondary schools organise one student health check every year. The key points on the report are overweight, obesity, undernutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and tooth decay. These are the ones most easily let slide. Do not glance at height and weight and then put the form away. If any of these is abnormal, take the child to a specialist the same year and get it clear.
- Benefit: The Measures for the Health Examination of Primary and Secondary Pupils (2021 edition) provide that primary and secondary schools organise 1 health examination of pupils at school every year. The surgical check includes the head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin and lymph nodes. The eye check includes the appearance of the eye, distance vision, and refraction (the degree of myopia or hyperopia). The report given to the person should include the results found, the overall assessment, and health-guidance advice. Among these, overweight, obesity, undernutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision and tooth decay (cavities) must be treated as the focus of the guidance. The education department shall open a file on a pupil who has a health problem, and follow it up (write it down, and follow it again later). During compulsory education, the cost of the pupil health examination is paid from the school's public funds. Parents do not pay extra (nationwide, issued in September 2021, month 9)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: It is graded B because this is a document on how schools are to do it, not a study of the effect. What it guarantees is "a check every year, a check of these items, and a follow-up when something abnormal is found". It does not guarantee that someone will take charge once it is found. The check only screens first. It is not a confirmed diagnosis. Poor vision means going to ophthalmology for a dilated refraction (§30.12). Abnormal spinal curvature means going to orthopaedics or spinal surgery (§30.2, do not put off treatment in order to wait until the exams are over). Both of these steps have to be taken by the parent, taking the child himself.

### 8. Give a child aged 12 to 18 one depression screening, and do not treat the school's psychological test as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: From 0 to a few tens of yuan, filling in a screening questionnaire at a community clinic or a hospital outpatient clinic. The two hotlines 12355 and 12356 cost nothing to call.
- In plain words: The American prevention guideline suggests screening children aged 12 to 18 for major depression. For age 11 and under, the evidence is not enough. Suicide is the second leading cause of death for children aged 10 to 19. The school's psychological test each year is only a general survey. It cannot be treated as a diagnosis. If the child says he feels bad, or eating, sleep, interest or marks suddenly change, go straight to psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: In 2022 the US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (a grade B recommendation). Grade B means the confidence is moderate and the benefit, once counted, is also moderate, and it is worth doing. For screening children aged 11 and under for major depression, the evidence is insufficient (an I statement, meaning the evidence is not enough to give advice). For a special screening of children and adolescents for suicide risk, the evidence on both benefit and harm is insufficient (an I statement). The document points out that suicide is the second leading cause of death in people aged 10 to 19. The document also points out that major depression in children and adolescents is closely related to many later problems. These include depression coming back in adulthood, other mental disorders, and a higher risk of suicidal thoughts, suicide attempts and death by suicide. The Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental-Health Work for Primary and Secondary Pupils requires a three-level service system of the class teacher, full-time and part-time mental-health teachers, and a psychiatrist. Each pupil receives a psychological test each school year, in general no more than 1 time. The document also requires pushing forward referral to medical care for mental illness, and a mechanism for returning to school after recovery. It requires using the 12355 youth service desk and the 12356 psychological-aid hotline (nationwide, issued in October 2025, month 10)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: It is graded B because what the original gives is a rating of how strong the recommendation is, not an effect that can be turned into a number. It is also an American recommendation. China has no matching official screening advice. A positive screening result only means another assessment is needed. It is not a confirmed diagnosis. What to do when there is a suicidal thought, see §1.25. What the family does, see section 29.

### 9. Do not buy products and services that claim they can "cure myopia" or "lower the degree"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The other way round, it also saves this sum.
- In plain words: The market-regulation administration says plainly that "under present medical technology, myopia cannot be cured". Claims such as "restore", "lower the degree", "cure myopia" and "repair the degree" are to be investigated and dealt with strictly. See words of this kind and rule them out at once. There is no need to compare prices. An eye-protection device counts as a medical device. The e-commerce platform has a duty to check whether the seller is qualified to sell it.
- Benefit: The notice of a special clean-up by the general office of the market-regulation administration, from November 2021 (month 11) to March 2022 (month 3), states clearly: "under present medical technology, myopia cannot be cured". The notice requires investigating and dealing with, strictly and according to law, the use of misleading wording such as "rehabilitation", "restore", "lower the degree", "cure myopia", "the nemesis of myopia" and "repair the degree" to market products for preventing and controlling myopia in children and adolescents. The notice requires a heavier crackdown. What it governs is marketing and publicity that borrows the name of traditional Chinese medicine, or that pretends to use the theory and technique of traditional Chinese medicine, to deceive consumers. The notice also requires a hard crackdown on two kinds of false and unlawful advertising: using a patient's name or image for a before-and-after comparison of the effect of treatment, and making a guaranteed promise about the safety or efficacy of treatment. The notice requires e-commerce platforms to strictly carry out the duty to review business qualifications for medical devices such as eye-protection devices, and for related special goods (to check clearly whether the seller is qualified to sell) (nationwide)
- Evidence: B

Three months later, the child's behaviour problems were also fewer (d = -0.40). The Family Education Promotion Law requires parents to learn family-education knowledge of their own accord. Residents' committees and villagers' committees may run community parent schools. Governments at provincial level and above are to open online parent schools and service hotlines for the public good. A family-education guidance institution may not organise training for profit (nationwide, in force from 1 January 2022)

### 18. Do not send a child into a closed "internet-addiction school" or "training camp": the law forbids using methods that harm body and mind to break internet addiction, and children have already been locked up until they died
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money, and it also saves a sum of tuition. The hard part is, when the child is at his most difficult, the parent holding back from handing him to an outsider for "closed discipline".
- In plain words: The law forbids, in so many words, using a method that harms a child's body and mind to break internet addiction. A training school in Hefei handcuffed a boy who had just been sent there inside a confinement room, and about 44 hours later he was dead. Yuzhang Academy in Nanchang locked students in confinement. Several people in charge were sentenced. The parents asked for the tuition to be paid back, and the court did not support that.
- Benefit: Article 68 of the Law on the Protection of Minors provides that no organisation or individual may intervene in a minor's addiction to the network by a method that harms the minor's physical or mental health. Both of the cases below have a court judgment. The first was in Hefei, Anhui. In August 2017 (month 8), the parents of an 18-year-old boy signed an agreement with a training school to send him to break an internet addiction. People from the school took him away by force, locked him in a confinement room, and handcuffed both his hands to the window bars. They limited his eating, drinking and rest, and they also hit him. About 44 hours later, the boy died. The appraisal conclusion was that high temperature, a restricted posture, a lack of food and drink, and external injury caused death from a disorder of water and electrolytes. On 31 October 2018 (month 10), the Hefei Intermediate Court gave a first-instance judgment. The person in charge of the school committed the crimes of intentional injury and unlawful detention, and was sentenced to 16 years. Four instructors were sentenced to from 1 year to 8 years and 6 months. On 28 December 2018 (month 12), the Anhui High Court at second instance upheld the original judgment. The second-instance ruling writes that this school had not been approved by the department concerned, and that none of the defendants had a teacher's qualification. The second was in Nanchang, Jiangxi. Yuzhang Academy opened in 2013 and was cancelled and closed in November 2017 (month 11). While it was running, the school took some students into a "room for release from vexation" and locked them in confinement, with someone assigned to watch, each time for three to ten days. On 28 April 2023 (month 4), the Anyuan District Court in Pingxiang, at a retried first instance, announced the judgment. Five defendants constituted the crime of unlawful detention. Four were sentenced to from two years and ten months down to eleven months, and were prohibited for a set period from work related to education. One was exempted from criminal punishment. The parents asked for the tuition and the return travel costs to be paid back, and the court did not support that (nationwide / Anhui / Jiangxi)
