# 2. Do not die slowly

This section takes in only two kinds of slow risk: one kind moves the overall death rate a lot, and the other has hard evidence. Entries are ordered by value for money, from high to low. A few are marked as "randomised trials", which means people were split into two groups at random and compared. That kind of result is the most trustworthy. The other figures all come from studies that only tracked and recorded people, with no groups. The risk ratios such as HR and RR from those studies mix in two kinds of interference: these people were different to start with, and some were already ill before they did the thing. So these figures only show the direction and the rough size. They do not mean that doing it will lower the risk by that much.

The entries in this section fall into the blocks below by theme, with entry numbers in brackets.

**Smoking, alcohol, betel**: quit smoking the earlier the better (§2.1), no smoking at home or in the car (§2.2), get the medicine first when you quit (§2.3), set a quit day (§2.4), go to a quit clinic or call 12320 (§2.5), consider an e-cigarette only if you still cannot quit (§2.6), do not chew betel (§2.8), drink less or not at all (§2.19), do not quit cold on your own if you shake as soon as you stop (§2.20), count how much you drank in a week (§2.21).

**What to eat and drink less of**: do not drink sugary drinks (§2.7), eat less processed meat (§2.18), eat less ultra-processed food (§2.28), do not drink scalding tea and soup (§2.30).

**What you can swap in and eat more of**: switch to a low-sodium salt (§2.9), a small handful of nuts a day (§2.22), swap some red meat for fish and poultry (§2.23), swap some white rice and white flour for whole grains (§2.24), drink tea more than three times a week (§2.25), drink three to four cups of coffee a day (§2.26), eat a full 5 portions of fruit and vegetables (§2.27), eat chilli more than four times a week (§2.33), drink one or two portions of milk a day (§2.34), you do not need to give up eggs (§2.35).

**Exercise and weight**: walk to 7000–8000 steps a day (§2.11), play racket sports (§2.14), pile up short bursts of hard effort (§2.15), do strength training every week (§2.16), do not sit for too long at a stretch (§2.17), keep BMI at 20–25 (§2.32).

**Sleep and night shifts**: sleep about 7 hours a night (§2.13), keep naps within half an hour (§2.37), make up the sleep the next night after a late night (§2.38), move off night shifts early if you can (§2.39).

**Oil and fumes in the kitchen**: do not burn coal or wood for cooking and heating (§2.29), do not buy loose home-pressed peanut oil (§2.40), use vegetable oil instead of lard and butter (§2.41), turn the range hood on when you stir-fry (§2.42).

**Teeth, medicine and other habits**: brush properly and clean between the teeth (§2.10), take medicine on schedule as the doctor said (§2.12), get some sun when you go out in the day (§2.31), take a bath if you can (§2.36), get hormone treatment assessed for hot flushes around menopause (§2.43).

### 1. Quit smoking, the earlier the better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and you save money instead. A pack a day is about 20–30 yuan, and that money stays with you once you quit. The hard part is getting through withdrawal, usually a few weeks to a few months.
- In plain words: People who smoke live more than ten years less, on average, than people who do not. Quit before 40, and you wipe out about nine tenths of the extra death risk that comes from going on smoking. The earlier you quit, the more you get back. If you keep smoking after a fracture, the chance the bone fails to knit is about 2.3 times that of someone who does not smoke, so do not smoke before the bone has knitted.
- Benefit: A US study that tracked a group of people (recording only, no groups) found this. People still smoking now had a life expectancy more than 10 years shorter than people who never smoked. Quitting before 40 can remove about 90% of the death risk that comes from continuing to smoke. Quitting at 25–34 adds about 10 years of life, at 35–44 about 9 years, and at 45–54 about 6 years (the original reports this in years of life). A similar study in China: in the 2010s, the death risk of urban male smokers was 1.65 times that of non-smokers (RR 1.65), and for rural men it was 1.22 times. After a full 10 years of quitting on your own, the extra risk from smoking nearly disappears. The same holds after a fracture. Counted together, 19 studies that only recorded and did not split groups found that smokers had about 2.3 times the chance of a fracture failing to heal (the bone not knitting) that non-smokers had (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.76–3.06, other factors already taken off). The same tilt shows up in shin fractures and open fractures. In a Swedish randomised trial, of 50 people who joined a 6-week quit programme after emergency fracture surgery, 20% had at least one complication after surgery. Of the 55 who did not join, it was 38%. For surgery booked ahead, you can start quitting 4 to 8 weeks before the operation, with someone coaching you each week, and with nicotine replacement. Done that way, complications after surgery were about 58% lower (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.27–0.65, 2 trials, 210 people).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Jha P 等 (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>；Chen Z 等 (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>；Oberg M 等 (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>（the two second-hand smoke figures）；Scolaro JA 等 (2014). Cigarette smoking increases complications following fracture: a systematic review. J Bone Joint Surg Am. <https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.M.00081>；Nåsell H 等 (2010). Effect of smoking cessation intervention on results of acute fracture surgery: a randomized controlled trial. J Bone Joint Surg Am. <https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.I.00627>；Thomsen T 等 (2014). Interventions for preoperative smoking cessation. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002294.pub4>（the fracture and surgery figures）
- Notes: Breathing other people's smoke kills too: in 2004 about 60.3 ten thousand people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1% of all deaths worldwide that year. Even if you do not smoke, stay out of other people's smoke, and above all do not let children breathe it. How to quit is in the next four entries: §2.3 (quit-smoking medicine), §2.4 (set a quit day), §2.5 (go to a quit clinic), §2.6 (e-cigarettes). The figure for bones failing to knit comes from studies that only recorded and did not split groups. Smokers may also differ from non-smokers in other ways, and those cannot all be stripped out. The quit trial after a fracture had only 105 people, and a different way of counting makes the difference no longer clear.

### 2. Do not smoke at home or in the car, and do not let guests smoke at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time. The hard part is speaking up, and asking family and guests not to smoke at home.
- In plain words: About 60 ten thousand people a year worldwide die from breathing other people's smoke, and nearly three tenths of them are children. People who breathe second-hand smoke for a long time have about three tenths higher risk of high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke. What you breathe at home hurts more than what you breathe outside.
- Benefit: In 2004, 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1.0% of deaths worldwide, and 28% of them were children. Counted together, 57 studies: the chance of high blood pressure in people who breathed second-hand smoke was about 1.28 times that of people who did not (odds ratio). Heart disease was 1.39 times, heart attack 1.50 times, and stroke 1.36 times. The risk from breathing second-hand smoke at home is higher than from breathing it outside the home
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet：「603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men」，「61% of DALYs were in children」. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>；(2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research：57 项研究，「hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)」「Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: Deal with the home first, because you spend a long time there, and taken together several studies show that second-hand smoke breathed at home does more harm than smoke breathed outside the home. Of the health loss from second-hand smoke, 61% falls on children. Quitting yourself is in §2.1 of this section.

### 3. Do not quit by willpower alone; get the medicine first: the success rate can more than double
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Nicotine patches and chewing gum are over-the-counter drugs you can buy at a pharmacy counter. A course is 8 to 12 weeks, a few hundred yuan to over a thousand yuan. Varenicline and bupropion are prescription drugs. You get them at a quit clinic or in respiratory medicine. That money is roughly offset by the cigarettes you do not buy over the same stretch.
- In plain words: Most people cannot quit by willpower alone. The share who quit on varenicline is more than twice the share on a placebo. People who use nicotine patches, chewing gum and the like are about five tenths higher than people who use no medicine. Using a patch and chewing gum together is more than two tenths higher again.
- Benefit: Counted together, 41 randomised trials and 17395 people: the quit rate in the varenicline group was 2.32 times that of the placebo group (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15–2.51, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in; high-certainty evidence). Varenicline was also higher than bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and higher than one nicotine replacement used alone (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). Nicotine replacement against no medicine: 133 trials, 64640 people, success about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49–1.61). A patch plus a fast-acting form (chewing gum, lozenge) was about 27% higher than one form alone (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17–1.37, 16 trials, 12169 people, high-certainty evidence).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8>；Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Nicotine replacement therapy versus control for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD000146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000146.pub5>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>；上海市卫生健康委员会 (2021). 选对药物，让戒烟轻松一点：「市场上可见的戒烟药物主要有三种，即尼古丁替代疗法药物、安非他酮、伐尼克兰」，「尼古丁替代疗法药物属于非处方药（OTC），可通过药店柜台购买；而安非他酮、伐尼克兰属于处方药，须到医院戒烟门诊或呼吸内科就诊，凭医师处方经药师调配后才能得到」. <https://wsjkw.sh.gov.cn/jtyx/20211119/df50681e01ba49f896d54f771d8176ae.html>
- Notes: The quit medicines on sale in China are the three kinds above, and only nicotine replacement is over the counter. Common reactions to varenicline are nausea, vivid dreams and poor sleep. If you have a psychiatric history, tell the doctor. A course of nicotine replacement is usually 8 to 12 weeks. Do not stop it suddenly yourself. Cut the dose as the doctor says. The medicine only covers the discomfort of those few weeks of withdrawal. It does not cover the moments when you "want a cigarette", so use it together with §2.4 (set a quit day) and §2.5 (go to a quit clinic).

### 4. Set a quit day, and stop all at once on that day; do not cut down first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You pick a day on the calendar and tell family and colleagues ahead of time.
- In plain words: Many people plan to smoke a few fewer a day and taper to zero. By month six, 22% of the people who set a day and stopped at once had not smoked again, against only 15.5% of those who tapered. So set a day, and stop all at once on that day.
- Benefit: In Britain, 697 smokers were split at random into two groups. One group stopped all at once on the quit day. The other cut the amount by 75% in the two weeks before the quit day. Both groups had behavioural support from a nurse, and both used nicotine replacement around the quit day. At week 4, 49.0% of the stop-at-once group (95% CI 43.8–54.2, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in) had not relapsed, against 39.2% of the taper group (34.0–44.4), relative risk ratio 0.80 (0.66–0.93). At month 6, the stop-at-once group was 22.0% (18.0–26.6) and the taper group 15.5% (12.0–19.7), relative risk ratio 0.71 (0.46–0.91). People who already preferred to taper also had a lower success rate at week 4 than people who preferred to stop at once (38.3% against 52.2%).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308（the part on starting nicotine replacement early）. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes: Disputed. Cochrane used to have a review that compared these two methods on purpose. Its conclusion was that they were about the same (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.79–1.13, 10 trials, 3760 people), but that review was withdrawn in 2019 and is no longer updated. So the best evidence now is the randomised trial above, and the direction supports stopping at once. Also, in that trial both groups started nicotine replacement before the quit day, and that practice itself has evidence: starting early beats starting only on the quit day, with success about 25% higher (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08–1.44, 9 trials, 4395 people, moderate-certainty evidence). So a reasonable order is to set the day first, put the patch on two weeks ahead, and stop all at once when the day comes.

### 5. Go to a quit clinic, or call 12320 to ask whether your area has a quit service
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One registration is a few yuan to a few tens of yuan. Behavioural support is usually 4 to 8 face-to-face talks, half an hour to an hour each, and the total is mostly within 5 hours. The hotline costs nothing.
- In plain words: Medicine and "someone asking you on a schedule" are two different things, and they work best together. People who take medicine and also have someone following them quit at close to twice the rate of people who only hear one short piece of advice. The phone counts too: a quit line that calls you back can raise the success rate by another two or three tenths.
- Benefit: Counted together, 52 trials and 19488 people: the quit rate in the medicine-plus-behavioural-support group was 1.83 times that of usual care or a short piece of advice (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.68–1.98, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in; high-quality evidence). In the 43 trials that recruited inside medical settings it was 1.97 times (1.79–2.18). Telephone counselling on its own: among people who had already called a quit line, those who got several callbacks were about 38% higher than those who only got materials or only one talk (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.19–1.61, 14 trials, 32484 people). Among people who had not called a line, and whom the study side phoned, it was about 25% higher (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.15–1.35, 65 trials, 41233 people). Both are moderate-certainty evidence.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Stead LF, Koilpillai P, Fanshawe TR, Lancaster T (2016). Combined pharmacotherapy and behavioural interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 3, CD008286. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008286.pub3>；Matkin W, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Hartmann-Boyce J (2019). Telephone counselling for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD002850. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002850.pub4>；中国疾病预防控制中心 (2021). 「中国戒烟平台」微信小程序正式上线：「在健康中国行动控烟行动工作组指导下中国疾病预防控制中心和世界卫生组织驻华代表处联合制作了中国权威戒烟资源库『中国戒烟平台』微信小程序。小程序内容主要包括：戒烟热线、戒烟门诊详细信息，以及线上戒烟服务资源等」；同一站点页脚标注「健康咨询电话：12320」. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/yckz/gzdt/202203/t20220310_296389.html>
- Notes: How to find it in China: search WeChat for the mini-program 「中国戒烟平台」. It lists local quit lines and quit clinics. You can also call 12320 and ask whether your area has a quit clinic. Most of the trials above were done abroad. Quit clinics differ a lot in how intensive the service is, so treat the figures only as a direction. The medicine to pair with this is in §2.3 (quit-smoking medicine).

### 6. Consider an e-cigarette only after you still cannot quit; people who do not smoke should not touch one
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The device plus pods is a few tens to a few hundred yuan a month. In China you can buy only tobacco flavour, and only in a shop that holds a tobacco retail licence.
- In plain words: Switching cigarettes for a nicotine e-cigarette, about six tenths more people quit than with a nicotine patch, and this conclusion is solid. It is not harmless, and there are still no safety data for long-term use. People who did not smoke to start with get nothing from it.
- Benefit: A review that keeps being updated took in 80 randomised trials and 29861 people. Nicotine e-cigarettes against nicotine replacement: quit success about 61% higher (RR 1.61, 95% CI 1.23–2.12, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in; high-certainty evidence, 11 trials, 4114 people). In absolute numbers, that is about 4 extra people quitting per 100 (1 to 7 extra). The share with serious adverse events was roughly the same in the two groups (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.02, moderate-certainty evidence). Against behavioural support alone, or no support, it was about 75% higher (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.39–2.20, low-certainty evidence).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, et al. (2026). Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD010216. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub11>；国家烟草专卖局 (2022). 电子烟管理办法（国家烟草专卖局公告 2022 年第 1 号，2022 年 5 月 1 日施行）第十八条：从事电子烟零售业务，要向烟草专卖行政主管部门申领烟草专卖零售许可证。第二十二条：「禁止向未成年人出售电子烟产品。」第二十三条第二款：「任何个人、法人或者其他组织不得通过本办法规定的电子烟交易管理平台以外的信息网络销售电子烟产品、雾化物和电子烟用烟碱等。」第二十六条：「禁止销售除烟草口味外的调味电子烟和可自行添加雾化物的电子烟。」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5697988.htm>
- Notes: Disputed. The World Health Organization does not recommend e-cigarettes as a way to quit. The reason is that long-term safety data are not enough, and they also get young people started on nicotine. Britain has put them into quit services. China has not banned e-cigarettes outright, but the rules are tight, and they are written in the 《电子烟管理办法》. Only tobacco flavour may be sold. Fruit flavours, and devices you can fill with your own liquid, may not. You can buy only in a physical shop that holds a tobacco retail licence. Selling on other websites, online shops and social apps, outside the national e-cigarette trading platform, is not legal. Selling to minors is not allowed either. So the fruit-flavoured e-cigarettes you can still find come through illegal channels, outside regulation. The "high" e-cigarettes laced with synthetic cannabinoids spread that way, see §22.4 (do not take a pod someone hands you). What the review measured were regulated nicotine products. Liquids of unknown origin are not in it. In order, try §2.3 (quit-smoking medicine) first. The evidence there is harder, and it is cheaper.

### 7. Do not drink sugary drinks, and switching to sugar-free does not count as solving it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea also saves money. The hard part is changing the habit of sweet drinks, including the sugar-free ones.
- In plain words: People who drink more than two bottles of sweet drinks a day have about two tenths higher chance of dying over the same period than people who hardly drink any. One to two bottles a day is more than one tenth higher too. Switching to sugar-free showed no benefit: people who drink more than two cups of artificially sweetened drinks a day were instead about a quarter higher.
- Benefit: The United States has two large studies that tracked a group of people. Both only recorded, with no groups: about 11.8 ten thousand people, and 3.6 ten thousand deaths. People who drank 2 portions or more a day had about 21% higher death risk than people who drank less than 1 portion a month (HR 1.21). People who drank 1–2 portions a day were about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC tracking study only recorded, with no groups: 45 ten thousand people, 10 countries. People who drank 2 cups or more a day against people who drank less than 1 cup a month: sugary soft drinks were about 8% higher (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.16). Artificially sweetened drinks (the sugar-free version) were about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.16–1.35). All soft drinks together were about 17% higher (HR 1.17, 1.11–1.22).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Malik VS 等 (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>；Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: Disputed, on sugar-free drinks. The link for artificially sweetened drinks is stronger than for sugary ones, and this is very likely the cause running backwards: people who are already overweight, or who already have diabetes, are the ones who switch to the sugar-free version. So do not take this as saying artificial sweeteners are more poisonous. It does at least show that the claim "switch to sugar-free and you are fine" has no evidence behind it. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea is the steadier move. Also, this kind of study only tracks and records. It did not run a grouped trial. People who drink a lot of sweet drinks already live worse in other ways, so the figures may be on the high side.

### 8. Do not chew betel
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, takes no time, and you also save the money you spent on betel. The hard part is giving up the chewing habit.
- In plain words: People who chew betel have about two tenths higher chance of dying over the same period than people who do not. The risk of diabetes and metabolic syndrome is nearly five tenths higher. Oral cancer and oesophageal cancer are a separate count on top.
- Benefit: Counted together, 17 Asian studies and 38.8 ten thousand people. People who chew betel had about 21% higher death risk than people who do not (relative risk 1.21, P=0.02). The death item was counted on 17.96 ten thousand of those people. The risk of diabetes was about 47% higher (1.47). The risk of metabolic syndrome was about 51% higher (1.51). Betel itself is also a known risk factor for oral cancer and oesophageal cancer
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>
- Notes: The longer you have chewed, and the more you chew each day, the higher the oral-cancer risk. After you stop, the risk goes down over time. Betel that contains tobacco is more dangerous

### 9. Switch the salt at home to a low-sodium salt (a potassium salt)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bag costs a few yuan more than ordinary salt. You swap it while you are buying, and it takes no extra time. The taste barely changes.
- In plain words: For people who have had a stroke, or who are over 60 with high blood pressure, switching the salt at home to a low-sodium salt made the chance of dying within five years about 12% lower, and stroke about 14% lower.
- Benefit: A trial that split people into two groups at random, done in rural China, 20995 people, all of them people who had had a stroke or people over 60 with high blood pressure, followed for 4.74 years. Result: the low-sodium-salt group against the ordinary-salt group had about 12% lower death risk (RR 0.88). Stroke was about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events were about 13% lower (RR 0.87). Events of blood potassium being too high showed no statistical difference between the two groups.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Neal B 等 (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>；O'Donnell M 等 (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889>（the opposing side, PURE）
- Notes: Disputed. The PURE study only tracked people for a long time and did not split groups. It reports: people who excreted less than 3 g of sodium a day had about 27% higher risk of death plus cardiovascular events. People who excreted 7 g or more a day were about 15% higher. On its result, eating too little and eating too much are both worse, and the middle is best. But in the rural China randomised trial cited here, the low-sodium salt only replaces part of the salt. It does not push sodium that low. Also note that the people in the trial were older people at high risk. Healthy young people get much less from switching salt. People with poor kidney function, or people taking a potassium-sparing drug, should ask a doctor before they switch.

### 10. Brush properly, clean between the teeth once a day, and replace missing teeth in time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Floss or interdental brushes cost a few tens of yuan a year, and a cleaning is one or two hundred yuan a time. You spend an extra two or three minutes a day. The hard part is making the cleaning between the teeth something you do every day.
- In plain words: People who use floss or an interdental brush every day have about one tenth lower chance of dying over the same period. Older people who have lost all their teeth have about nine tenths higher chance of dying. People with fewer than 20 teeth left are about double.
- Benefit: A study in Japan, 9676 people, followed for 6 years. People who used a tool to clean between the teeth had about 11% lower death risk (risk ratio 0.89). People who used a tongue-coating cleaner had about 23% lower (0.77). Another count of several studies together, on older people living in the community: people with not a single tooth left had about 1.87 times the chance of dying (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.35–2.59, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). People with fewer than 20 teeth had about 2.04 times (1.67–2.49)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789>；Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>
- Notes: Disputed. This kind of study only tracks and records. People with bad teeth often also have worse health and less money overall. Whether the teeth dragged the body down, or a poor body dragged the teeth down, is not clear. But this costs very little, and gum disease and missing teeth themselves affect eating. How much one implant costs, and which dental items insurance pays, see §24.12 (ask the package price before you get an implant)

### 11. Walk to 7000–8000 steps a day, or add up 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Counted in steps, 60–90 minutes of walking a day can be split into the commute and the shopping. Counted in exercise time, 20–45 minutes a day. The hard part is keeping it up for a long time.
- In plain words: People who walk about 7800 steps a day have about four and a half tenths lower chance of dying over the same period than people who walk only 3500. Counted in time it is the same: 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week is about three tenths lower than no exercise. Past a certain amount it stops falling further. Over 60, 6000–8000 steps is enough. Hitting either way of counting is enough.
- Benefit: Counted together, 15 studies that tracked a group of people, recording only, no groups. These 15 had 47471 people and 3013 deaths. People were split into four bands by daily steps. The lowest band had a median of 3553 steps/day. The other three bands had medians of 5801, 7842 and 10901 steps/day. Death risk in those three bands was about 40%, 45% and 53% lower than the lowest band (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). For people over 60, once they reach 6000–8000 steps, walking more levels off. For people under 60, it levels off after 8000–10000 steps. Another analysis counted several studies together: it starts to work from about 3867 steps/day. After that, each extra 1000 steps/day lowers death risk by about 15%. Counted in exercise time: several tracking studies pooled (recording only, no groups), people whose activity reached 1–2 times the lower end of the guideline had about 31% lower death risk than people who did not exercise (HR 0.69). That amount is 7.5–15 MET·h/week, about 150–300 minutes a week of brisk walking. People who did not reach the lower end were still about 20% lower (HR 0.80). People who reached 3–5 times the lower end were about 39% lower (HR 0.61), and that is the bottom of the drop. Adding more does not go lower, but it does no harm either (10 times or more is HR 0.69). Another pooled study that measured with an accelerometer: the quarter with the most moderate-to-vigorous activity had about 48% lower death risk than the quarter with the least (HR 0.52). An accelerometer is a device worn on the body that measures activity directly.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Paluch AE 等 (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>；Banach M 等 (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>；Arem H 等 (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>；Ekelund U 等 (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: Steps and exercise time are the same thing, only counted differently, and hitting one of them is enough. The stretch from 4000 steps up to 7000 steps is the best value. You do not have to make it to ten thousand steps. Note that this kind of study only tracks and records: among the people who walk least and move least are people who were already ill and could not walk. They moved little because they were already failing, not because moving little made them die early. So the gap that comes out is often too large. The studies that measured activity with a device followed people for a short time, and the people were on the old side, so the real benefit is not as large as "risk down to 0.52 times". But the trend that more movement is better is clear.

### 12. If you have high blood pressure or high blood lipids, take the medicine on schedule as the doctor said, and do not stop it yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Volume-purchased blood-pressure drugs and statins are a few yuan to a few tens of yuan a month. You take them once a day, and it does not take much time. The hard part is keeping on taking them, and not stopping because you feel fine.
- In plain words: For every 10 mmHg the blood pressure drops, the chance of dying drops about 13%, and stroke about 27%. A statin that drops LDL cholesterol by 1 unit drops the chance of dying by about one tenth. People who take the medicine on time have a three tenths to four and a half tenths lower chance of dying than people who often miss doses.
- Benefit: Counted together, several trials that split people at random: for every 10 mmHg drop in systolic pressure, death risk was about 13% lower (RR 0.87). Systolic pressure is the higher number in a blood-pressure reading. Major cardiovascular events were about 20% lower (RR 0.80). Stroke was about 27% lower (RR 0.73). Heart failure was about 28% lower (RR 0.72). Taking a statin brings low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL) down. For every 1.0 mmol/L drop in LDL, death risk was about 10% lower (RR 0.90). The same drop made major vascular events about 22% lower (RR 0.78). Another count of several tracking studies together, recording only, no groups: people who took ≥80% of what the doctor ordered had lower death risk than people who often missed doses. For statins this was about 45% lower (RR 0.55). For blood-pressure drugs it was about 29% lower (RR 0.71).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>；Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>；Chowdhury R 等 (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>
- Notes: Only people the doctor has judged should take the medicine do this. Healthy people do not take it. The "keep taking the medicine" figures come from tracking records. People who can keep taking medicine are also more self-disciplined, so the benefit gets overstated. The figures from the two sets of randomised trials above are more reliable. This section does not list blood-sugar control on its own: the benefit of pushing blood sugar lower, for the overall death rate, is not as consistent as the evidence for blood pressure and blood lipids.

### 13. Sleep about 7 hours a night, and keep the schedule fixed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You have to set aside time to sleep. For most people that means swapping phone time for sleep. The hard part is fixing the time you go to bed and the time you get up.
- In plain words: People who sleep under 7 hours for a long time have about one tenth higher chance of dying over the same period. The more irregular the schedule, the higher the risk. The most regular sleepers are two tenths to nearly five tenths lower than the least regular. How regular the schedule is tells you more about death risk than how long you sleep.
- Benefit: Counted together, 16 tracking studies, recording only, no groups: 138 ten thousand people, 11.3 ten thousand deaths. People who slept short had about 12% higher death risk (RR 1.12). People who slept long were about 30% higher (RR 1.30). Another pooling split by hours of sleep: the 7-hour band had the lowest risk. Under 7 hours, each hour less was about 6% higher (RR 1.06). Over 7 hours, each hour more was about 13% higher (RR 1.13). UK Biobank has wrist-watch records on about 6.1 ten thousand people, recording only, no groups. People were split into five bands by how regular the schedule was. The four more regular bands had 20%–48% lower death risk than the least regular band. How irregular the schedule is predicts death better than how long you sleep. Another UK Biobank study measured sleep timing with a wrist watch in 51,562 people, and 3,853 of them newly developed cardiovascular disease in that time. Social jet lag means how far apart the midpoint of sleep is on work days and on weekends. People whose social jet lag reached 2 hours or more had about 30% higher cardiovascular risk (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). This link is there in people whose sleep length is normal too.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Cappuccio FP 等 (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>；Yin J 等 (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>；Windred DP 等 (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>；Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Notes: What you change is sleeping too little, and a messy schedule. You do not need to cut sleep time on purpose. People who sleep long have higher risk mostly because the cause runs backwards: depression, chronic illness and sleep apnoea make people sleep more. Also, a regular schedule is supported so far by two tracking studies, both recording only with no groups, and taken alone that is grade B evidence. How to make up a late night is in §2.38 (make up the sleep the next night after a late night).

### 14. Play racket sports such as tennis, badminton and table tennis often
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Court fees are a few tens of yuan a time. About 2 hours a week. The hard part is getting the people and the court together, and still going every week.
- In plain words: People who often play tennis, badminton or table tennis have about half the chance of dying over the same period as people who do not, and cardiovascular death is about 56% lower. Swimmers are about 28% lower. Running and football showed no difference in this set of data.
- Benefit: A tracking study of 8.03 ten thousand people in Britain compared people who did a given sport with people who did not do that sport. People who played racket sports had about 47% lower death risk (risk ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.69, the range the figure can credibly lie in). Cardiovascular death was about 56% lower (0.44, 0.24–0.83). Racket sports means tennis, badminton and table tennis. For swimmers those two figures were 0.72 and 0.59, for aerobics 0.73 and 0.64, and for cycling 0.85. Running and football showed no statistical difference
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Notes: Disputed. This kind of study only tracks and records. People who play these sports are healthier to start with, and they also socialise more. The running group showing no difference also says that what may be at work is "what kind of person picked what sport". Do not take this entry as saying running is useless. This section has another entry that counts by total amount of exercise

### 15. Pile up short bursts of hard effort, such as climbing stairs and hurrying on foot, to four or five minutes a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and you do not have to carve out extra exercise time. The hard part is remembering to take the stairs and to walk faster, instead of taking the easy way.
- In plain words: People who do no exercise at all, if they have three bursts of a minute or two of hard effort a day, have about four tenths lower chance of dying over the same period than people with none of it, and cardiovascular death is nearly half lower. Hard effort means climbing stairs, hurrying on foot, and the like.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study looked at 2.52 ten thousand people who do not exercise on purpose. Average follow-up was 6.9 years, and 852 of them died. Some people had 3 bursts a day, 1–2 minutes each, of short vigorous activity. Against people with none of it, their death risk and cancer-death risk were 38%–40% lower. Cardiovascular death risk was 48%–49% lower. People whose bursts of this kind added up to 4.4 minutes a day had death risk and cancer-death risk 26%–30% lower. Cardiovascular death risk was 32%–34% lower
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes: Disputed. This study measured activity with a wearable device, which is more reliable than asking people to recall it on a form. It is still only a tracking record, with no grouped trial, and it followed people for only 6.9 years. The people studied do no exercise at all. People who already exercise on a schedule do not fall under this entry

### 16. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and push-ups cost nothing. Train 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes each time. The hard part is putting it on the weekly schedule.
- In plain words: People who train strength for 30–60 minutes a week have a one to two tenths lower chance of dying over the same period than people who do not. Training more does not get better. Strength and aerobic work together work best.
- Benefit: Counted together, several tracking studies (recording only, no groups): people who did strength training had 10%–17% lower death risk than people who did not. More is not better. The drop is largest at about 30–60 minutes a week, about 10%–20%, and training more does not add benefit. People who did both strength and aerobic work had lower death risk than people who did neither.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Momma H 等 (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: This kind of study only tracks and records, and the amount of exercise is what the participants reported themselves. The evidence for "training too much makes it worse" is very weak, so do not limit the amount for that reason. Strength training also helps older people avoid falls and keep muscle, and those points are written in section 1.

### 17. Do not sit for too long at a stretch; get up and move every so often
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and standing up a few times does not take much time. The hard part is that once you sit down you forget the time, so you need a reminder.
- In plain words: People who sit the longest have 2.6 times the chance of dying over the same period as people who sit the least. Sitting a long time in one stretch is a separate risk on top. But if you have 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day, the extra risk from sitting a long time is basically offset.
- Benefit: A tracking study in the United States, recording only, no groups, measured with an accelerometer. People were split into four parts by total sitting time each day. The part that sat the most had 2.63 times the death risk of the part that sat the least (HR 2.63). Split again into four parts by how long one stretch of sitting lasted: the part with the longest stretches had 1.96 times the risk of the part with the shortest stretches (HR 1.96). So total sitting, and how long one stretch lasts, each relate on their own. Another pooling counted studies of more than a million people together, recording only, no groups. People who sat more than 8 hours a day and barely exercised had 1.59 times the death risk of another kind of person (HR 1.59). That other kind sat under 4 hours a day and was the most active. About 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day can offset the extra risk from sitting a long time. In the most active group, even with more than 8 hours of sitting a day, the HR was only 1.04, and no statistical difference showed.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Diaz KM 等 (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>；Ekelund U 等 (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: The reliable conclusion is: how long you sit matters less than how much you move, and when the amount of exercise is enough the risk from sitting a long time basically disappears. Note that this kind of study only tracks and records. Among the people who sit the most are a large number who were already ill, and "risk as high as 2.63 times" clearly contains "they sat a long time because they were ill first". Also, in the same pooling, the risk of watching television more than 3 hours a day is there at every activity level, and the most active group only pushes that starting point out to 5 hours.

### 18. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausages, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and may even save money, and it takes no time. The hard part is eating less of the thing you like.
- In plain words: People who eat the most ham, bacon and sausage have about two tenths higher chance of dying over the same period than people who eat the least. One extra portion of processed meat a day also raises the chance by two tenths.
- Benefit: Counted together, several tracking studies (recording only, no groups): the group that ate the most processed meat had about 23% higher death risk than the group that ate the least (RR 1.23). Red meat in total was about 29% higher (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat was about 10% higher (RR 1.10), but that item showed no statistical difference. Another pooling is counted by the amount eaten each day. One extra portion of processed meat a day raised death risk by about 23% (RR 1.23). One extra portion of red meat a day raised it by about 10% (RR 1.10).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>；Johnston BC 等 (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621>（the opposing side, the NutriRECS guideline）
- Notes: Disputed. The NutriRECS guideline used a standard way of scoring evidence (GRADE) and rated these studies "not very reliable". It gave only one very weak suggestion: keep eating as you do now. What the two sides argue about is whether the evidence is hard enough, not the direction. No study says processed meat is good for you. Unprocessed red meat has a small effect, and it also shows no statistical difference. What you need to hold down is processed meat.

### 19. Drink less, or do not drink
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is a bit of awkwardness when you turn down a drink at a social occasion.
- In plain words: Once you pass 100 g of pure alcohol a week, the more you drink the earlier you die. 100 g of pure alcohol is about 2.5 L of beer. At age 40, people who drink 100–200 g a week live about half a year less; 200–350 g is one or two years less; over 350 g is four or five years less. Counted by health overall, the amount with the best value is none.
- Benefit: Counted together, 83 studies that followed people forward, recording only, no groups: 60 ten thousand people who were drinking. The lowest point of death risk was at no more than 100 g of pure alcohol a week. That amount is about 2.5 L of 5% beer, or 300 mL of 40-degree spirits. At age 40, people who drank 100–200 g a week had about 6 months less life expectancy. People who drank 200–350 g a week had 1–2 years less. People over 350 g had 4–5 years less. The three items above are reported in the original as years of life. The Global Burden of Disease study GBD 2016 added health losses of all kinds together, and the drinking amount with the lowest risk was 0 drinks a week. Another pooling corrected for bias in how the studies were designed. People who drank 1.3–24 g a day against people who never drank in their lives had about 7% lower death risk (RR 0.93). But that item showed no statistical difference. People who drank 45–64 g a day were about 19% higher (RR 1.19). People at 65 g or more a day were about 35% higher (RR 1.35).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Wood AM 等 (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>；GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>；Zhao J 等 (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>；Di Castelnuovo A 等 (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437>（the opposing side）
- Notes: Disputed. The side that holds "a little drinking is good for you" is a pooling of 34 long-term tracking studies. It reports: people who drank a small amount had overall death rates at most 17%–18% lower. The amount it gives is no more than 4 drinks a day for men and no more than 2 drinks a day for women. Within that amount, people who drank more had lower death rates instead. The other side holds that the "do not drink" group has people mixed in who quit because of illness, and people whose health was already poor. One pooling stripped those interferences out on purpose, and after that the protective effect was gone. The Global Burden of Disease study also gives 0 as the safest. The steady reading is: drinking a little does not clearly hurt lifespan, and drinking a lot certainly does. There is no basis for "starting to drink for your health". How to drink less is in §2.21 (count the drinks first, then see a doctor). People who drink every day cannot quit cold on their own, see §2.20 (do not quit cold on your own)

### 20. If you drink every day and you shake and your heart races as soon as you stop, do not quit cold on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One registration in psychiatry or addiction medicine, a few yuan to a few tens of yuan. Severe cases need a few days in hospital.
- In plain words: People who drink a large amount every day and then stop suddenly get withdrawal. The light end is shaking, sweating, a racing heart and not sleeping. The heavy end is seizures, or delirium tremens, and that is an emergency that can kill. Norway followed more than thirty thousand people. Among those who had had delirium tremens, 8% died each year afterwards. If you want to quit, go to a hospital first and say plainly how much you drink each day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review states it plainly: people who have drunk heavily for a long time get a withdrawal syndrome after they stop suddenly or cut the amount sharply. The signs include tremor, autonomic excitement, anxiety, insomnia, abnormal perception, seizures and delirium tremens, and in ordinary internal-medicine practice this is "common and can be life-threatening". A national register cohort in Norway took in 36287 people diagnosed from 2009 to 2015 with alcohol dependence, an alcohol-withdrawal state, or delirium tremens: yearly death rate 8.0% in the delirium-tremens group, 5.0% in the withdrawal-state group, and 3.6% in the alcohol-dependence group. The standardised mortality ratio in the delirium-tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in), which means the death risk was about 9.8 times that of the general population of the same age.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103>；Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Notes: How to judge it yourself: you drink almost every day, one day without a drink and you shake, sweat, your heart races and you cannot sleep, or you need a bit in the morning before you feel all right. If one of those fits, do not stop cold on your own. The usual hospital practice is to use a benzodiazepine to get through those few days, and to give thiamine (vitamin B1) at the same time. This is graded B because the Norwegian cohort says "what happened later to people who had had delirium tremens", not "what happens if you quit cold on your own", and the step between the two is an inference. You still quit the drinking. You just quit it at a hospital. How much a week counts as a lot is in §2.19 (drink less or not at all). How to drink less is in §2.21 (count the drinks first, then see a doctor).

### 21. If you want to drink less, first count how much you drank in a week, then talk to a doctor for a few minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Counting drinks is one note a day. Asking in passing at a clinic costs nothing extra either.
- In plain words: A doctor spends a few minutes looking at how much you drink, states the risk, and helps you set a target. A year later people drink 20 grams less pure alcohol a week on average, about half a litre of beer. Talking longer did not add benefit. For people who already cannot stop, two drugs have been shown to work abroad. About 1 extra person in every 12 treated no longer goes back to drinking too much as soon as they drink.
- Benefit: Counted together, 34 randomised trials and 15197 people: people who got a brief intervention drank 20 grams less pure alcohol a week, one year later, than people who got the least intervention or none (95% CI 12 to 28 grams less, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in; moderate-quality evidence). Baseline drinking in these studies averaged 244 grams a week. A brief intervention means advice or lifestyle counselling in no more than 5 sessions, totalling under 60 minutes. Talking longer did not add benefit. On drugs: counted together, 122 randomised trials and 22803 people, acamprosate let 1 extra person in every 12 treated (95% CI 8 to 26) not return to any drinking. Oral naltrexone at 50 milligrams a day let 1 extra person in every 12 treated (8 to 26) not return to heavy drinking.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4>；Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Notes: The benefit size is set at "small" because both of these pieces of evidence measured how much was drunk, not the death rate, and by this book's rule a substitute endpoint is recorded as "small". Most of the drug trials were done abroad. Most of the people in them already met the diagnosis of alcohol dependence, and the trials also gave a psychosocial intervention at the same time, so the figure is "the extra benefit on top of that". Whether these two drugs are indicated in China, and whether they can be prescribed, follows the doctor and the drug label. Do not buy them online yourself. If withdrawal has already appeared, see §2.20 (do not quit cold on your own).

### 22. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 28 grams a day is about two or three hundred yuan a year. You eat them in passing, and it takes no time.
- In plain words: People who often eat nuts have about two tenths lower chance of dying over the same period than people who do not. Once a week already shows a difference, about one tenth lower. The difference is clearest at a handful a day.
- Benefit: Two US tracking studies, 11.9 ten thousand people, more than 300 ten thousand person-years. A person-year is the number of people times the years each was followed. Against people who ate no nuts: people who ate them less than once a week had about 7% lower death risk (risk ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). Once a week was about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93). 2–4 times a week was about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90). 5–6 times a week was about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91). 7 times a week or more was about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Buy the plain kind, with no salt added. Nuts are not low in calories, so do not eat them as an open snack. Note that this kind of study only follows a group of people for a long time. People who eat nuts have a better way of living overall, so discount the figures

### 23. Swap some of the red meat for fish and poultry
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time. This is swapping one meat for another, not buying more. The hard part is shifting a bit of what you prefer to eat.
- In plain words: Two extra portions of processed meat or red meat a week raise the chance of dying over the same period by about 3%. Swap the same number of portions for fish or poultry, and no rise shows. The effect of this entry is on the small side for this section. It is something you swap in passing, not something to strain over.
- Benefit: Counted together, 6 US tracking studies and nearly 3 ten thousand people. Two extra portions of processed meat a week raised death risk by about 3% (risk ratio 1.03, 95% CI 1.02–1.05, the range the figure can credibly lie in). Two extra portions of unprocessed red meat a week were also about 3% higher (1.03, 1.01–1.05). The same eating swapped for poultry was 0.99 (0.97–1.02). Swapped for fish it was 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither of those two showed a statistical difference
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: The difference from two portions a week is very small. Do not expect a few extra years of life from swapping meat. When you spend the same money on meat, you can pick fish and poultry first

### 24. Swap some white rice and white flour for whole grains
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brown rice, oats and wholewheat flour cost a bit more than white rice and white flour, and they take no extra time. The hard part is getting used to the texture slowly.
- In plain words: People who eat 90 g more whole grains a day have about 17% lower chance of dying over the same period. 90 g is about three portions. Up to about 200 g a day, it is still going down.
- Benefit: Counted together, several tracking studies (recording only, no groups): 90 g more whole grains a day lowered death risk by about 17% (RR 0.83). 90 g is about 3 portions. This benefit keeps going up to 210–225 g a day. In another pooling, 1 extra portion a day was about 8% lower (RR 0.92).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: You do not have to switch all of it to whole grains. Switching half is already in the stretch where the benefit is clearest. Note that this kind of study only tracks and records. People who eat whole grains were healthier to start with, so the figures are on the high side. Results also differ a lot between studies (I² 83%; the larger this number, the less the studies agree).

### 25. Drink tea more than three times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few tens to a few hundred yuan a year. Brewing a cup takes a few minutes, and it does not take much time.
- In plain words: People who have a habit of drinking tea have about 15% lower chance of dying over the same period. Counted from age 50, they live 1.26 years longer on average, and have 1.41 extra years free of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: A study of 10.09 ten thousand people in China (China-PAR), and half of them were followed for a full 7.3 years. People with a tea-drinking habit had about 15% lower death risk (risk ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). Counted from age 50 as the start: people with a tea habit had 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and 1.26 more years of life expectancy
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>；茶叶霉菌毒素：Cui P 等 (2020). Quantitative analysis and dietary risk assessment of aflatoxins in Chinese post-fermented dark tea. Food and Chemical Toxicology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830>；Zhou H 等 (2022). Mycotoxins in Tea (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze): Contamination and Dietary Exposure Profiling in the Chinese Population. Toxins. <https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins14070452>；绿茶提取物与肝损伤：Hu J 等 (2018). The safety of green tea and green tea extract consumption in adults - Results of a systematic review. Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2018.03.019>
- Notes: Disputed in this: this kind of study only tracks and records, and among Chinese men who drink tea the share who smoke and drink is high. The authors already took off part of that influence, but they cannot take it off cleanly. One more point: do not drink it scalding hot. The temperature of hot drinks is in the entry in this section on the temperature of hot drinks. Some people worry that tea contains mycotoxins. 158 samples of dark tea were checked in China, and 2 of them had aflatoxin. Counted by how much tea people drink, the amount taken in was below the international acceptable value. Another check covered 352 samples of tea and 16 mycotoxins, and only ochratoxin A in dark tea had an average content over the limit. Counted by how much people in China drink, neither check came out with a dietary risk. Keep tea somewhere dry, and do not drink any that has got damp and mouldy. Green-tea extract capsules are different: a large amount of high-concentration catechins swallowed at once can hurt the liver, and brewed tea does not have this problem. So what is said here is only brewed tea. Do not replace it with an extract supplement

### 26. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, with no sugar and no milk foam
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brewing it yourself is one or two yuan a day. A cup takes a few minutes, and it does not take much time.
- In plain words: People who drink three to four cups of coffee a day have about 17% lower chance of dying over the same period than people who do not drink it.
- Benefit: A review took the results of 201 pooled studies and combined them again. The original studies under those poolings all only recorded, with no groups. Result: people who drank 3–4 cups of coffee a day against people who drank none had 17% lower death risk (relative risk 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>
- Notes: Disputed in this: the original authors state that the evidence comes almost entirely from tracking records, and they also say "reliable randomised controlled trials are needed before one can judge whether it is causal". That means people have to be split into two groups at random and compared. Tracking records alone cannot settle cause. When you drink it, drink it black. Adding sugar and milk foam will offset the benefit. Pregnant women, and people with arrhythmia, anxiety or insomnia, do not fall under this entry

### 27. Eat a full 5 portions (about 400 g) of fruit and vegetables a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan to a bit over ten yuan a day. You spend some time each day washing and cutting. The hard part is buying every day and eating enough every day.
- In plain words: 200 g more fruit and vegetables a day lowers the chance of dying over the same period by about one tenth. People who eat a full 5 portions a day are about 13% lower than people who eat only 2. Eating more than that does not go lower.
- Benefit: Counted together, several tracking studies (recording only, no groups): 200 g more fruit and vegetables a day lowered death risk by about 10% (RR 0.90). This benefit keeps going up to 800 g a day. Two large US tracking studies plus a pooling of 26 studies: people who ate a full 5 portions a day had about 13% lower death risk than people who ate only 2 (HR 0.87). The best mix is 2 portions of fruit plus 3 portions of vegetables, and more than that does not go lower.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>；Wang DD 等 (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Notes: A full 5 portions is enough. You do not need to make it 10, and the two studies agree on this point. Note that this kind of study only tracks and records, and the interference is obvious: people who eat more vegetables differ from others in income, education and exercise. So "about 10% lower risk" is already an upper bound.

### 28. Eat less ultra-processed food (crisps, instant noodles, pastries, ready meals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You only buy something else. You cook yourself, or you spend time picking ingredients that have hardly been processed, and that takes some time every day. The hard part is pushing back, for a long time, against the pull of "convenient is fine".
- In plain words: People who eat the most ultra-processed food have about two tenths higher chance of dying over the same period than people who eat the least, and cardiovascular death is five tenths higher. This kind of evidence is rated not very certain, so read the figures with a discount.
- Benefit: A review took several pooled studies and combined them again. The original studies under those poolings all only recorded, with no groups. Result: people who ate a lot of ultra-processed food against people who ate little had about 21% higher death risk (RR 1.21), and cardiovascular death about 50% higher (RR 1.50). The evidence grades for these two items are "highly suggestive" and "convincing". But scored by the standard way of scoring evidence (GRADE), the certainty is low or very low.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lane MM 等 (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Notes: Disputed on three points. One is that this classification (NOVA) puts foods with very different nutrition into one class. Two is that it overlaps a lot with sugary drinks and processed meat (§2.7 and §2.18), so you cannot tell which item is doing the work. Three is that scored by the standard way of scoring evidence (GRADE), the grade is also low. So once you have done those two entries, how much extra this one can still bring is not clear. The opposing evidence is the low rating given by that same review. So far there is no original study that reaches the opposite conclusion. So do §2.7 and §2.18 (do not drink sugary drinks, eat less processed meat) first, and then look at this one.

### 29. Do not burn coal or wood for cooking and heating; switch to electricity or gas
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A rural household has to change the stove, plus the fuel bill, a few hundred to over a thousand yuan a year. You change it once and you are done, and it does not take time for long. People in cities are basically already on electricity or gas.
- In plain words: People who cook with coal or wood have about one tenth higher chance of dying over the same period. People who heat with coal or wood are about 14% higher. People who have already switched to electricity or gas have a 13%–33% lower chance of dying than people still burning coal or wood.
- Benefit: A study in China that followed chronic disease forward, recording only, no groups: 27.1 ten thousand adults with no cardiovascular disease. People who cooked with solid fuel such as coal or wood had about 11% higher death risk than people who used electricity or gas (HR 1.11). People who heated with solid fuel were about 14% higher (HR 1.14). People who had already switched from solid fuel to clean fuel, against people still burning it, were about 13% lower on cooking (HR 0.87). On heating they were about 33% lower (HR 0.67). Outdoor PM2.5 is a separate account. Counted together, 104 tracking studies: for every 10 µg/m³ higher the long-term concentration people breathed, death from natural causes was about 8% higher (RR 1.08). Natural causes means death from disease, not from an accident.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Yu K 等 (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151>；Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Notes: This kind of study only tracks and records. Households that switched fuel are often richer too, and "risk down to 0.67 times" has the family's circumstances mixed in. As for outdoor PM2.5, what one person can do is limited: move house, wear a mask, use a purifier. Air purifiers have no study with the death rate as the endpoint, so this section gives no figure for them.

### 30. Let hot things sit before you drink them; do not drink scalding tea, soup or coffee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You wait an extra two or three minutes each time. The hard part is holding off when you want it.
- In plain words: Drinking very hot tea, the chance of oesophageal cancer is 8 times that of drinking warm tea. Even "hot" is 2 times. Drinking it less than two minutes after pouring it out, the risk is 5 times that of waiting more than four minutes.
- Benefit: In a high-incidence area for oesophageal cancer in northern Iran, 300 patients were compared with 571 people who did not have the disease. Everything below is against people who drank warm tea. People who drank "hot" tea had about 2.07 times the chance of oesophageal squamous-cell cancer (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.28–3.35, the range the figure can credibly lie in). People who drank it "very hot" had about 8.16 times (3.93–16.9). People who drank it less than 2 minutes after it was poured had about 5.41 times the chance of people who waited more than 4 minutes (2.63–11.1)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>；Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer lists drinks hotter than 65 ℃ as class 2A, which means probably carcinogenic. The same assessment found that coffee itself is not carcinogenic. The Chaoshan area and the Taihang Mountains in China have a high rate of oesophageal cancer, and that is tied to the habit of drinking it while it is still scalding

### 31. Get some sun when you go out in the day; do not go all day without light
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. A bit over ten minutes a day, on the way to work and at the lunch break, is enough.
- In plain words: Women who deliberately avoided the sun had 0.6 to 2.1 years less life expectancy than the women who got the most sun. People who did not smoke but avoided the sun had a lifespan close to that of the smokers who got the most sun.
- Benefit: A study in Sweden, 2.95 ten thousand women, followed for 20 years. Against the group that got the most sun, people who deliberately avoided sun exposure had 0.6 to 2.1 years less life expectancy. The authors wrote that "non-smokers who avoided sun exposure had a life expectancy comparable to the smokers in the group with the most sun exposure"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes: Disputed in this: Sweden is at a high latitude with little sun, and the conclusion cannot be moved straight to China. Also, people who avoid the sun may already be frail and unwilling to go out. Also do not stay out until you burn. At noon in strong light you still need cover, because sun exposure raises the risk of skin cancer

### 32. Keep BMI at 20–25, and lose weight if you are overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Diet plus exercise takes time every day. This is the entry in the whole section that takes the most willpower: you have to watch what you eat and keep moving for a long time, and holding the loss is harder than losing it.
- In plain words: The chance of dying is lowest in the BMI band 20–25. At BMI 27.5–30 it is about two tenths higher, at 30–35 about four and a half tenths higher, and at 35–40 close to double. People in East Asia are more sensitive to being overweight. For every 5 higher in BMI, the risk is about four tenths higher.
- Benefit: Individual data from 239 studies that followed people forward were counted together (recording only, no groups). This analysis kept only three kinds of people: people who never smoked, people with no chronic disease when they joined, and people who were still alive 5 years after they joined. Result: death risk was lowest at BMI 20–25. BMI 25–27.5 was about 7% higher (HR 1.07). 27.5–30 was about 20% higher (HR 1.20). 30–35 was about 45% higher (HR 1.45). 35–40 was about 94% higher (HR 1.94). 40–60 was 2.76 times (HR 2.76). For people with BMI over 25, each extra 5 kg/m² raised the risk by about 39% in East Asian populations (HR 1.39).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>；Flegal KM 等 (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905>（the opposing side）
- Notes: Disputed. One side holds that "a bit fatter means you live longer". After they pooled the studies they got: overweight people had about 6% lower death risk, and mildly obese people about 5% lower. The split between the two sides is mainly whether to strip out three kinds of data: people who smoke, people who are already ill, and the data from the first few years after joining. The reason to strip them is that people with serious illness lose weight first. The analysis that pooled 239 studies put those three kinds back out, and after that the risk from being overweight showed up again. Also note that no randomised trial has proved that losing weight itself lowers the overall death rate. The benefit above comes from comparing people of different weights side by side. It does not mean that losing the weight will lower the risk by that much. People who want to lose weight do not need to work on the timing of meals. Breakfast and 16:8 light fasting have no extra benefit, see §6.26 (do not count on breakfast or 16:8 to help you control your weight)

### 33. Eat chilli more than four times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time.
- In plain words: People who eat chilli more than four times a week have about 23% lower chance of dying over the same period than people who rarely eat it, and cardiovascular death is about one third lower.
- Benefit: A study of 2.28 ten thousand people in Italy, half of them followed for a full 8.2 years, and 1236 people died. People who ate chilli 4 times a week or more had about 23% lower death risk than people who rarely ate it (risk ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.66–0.90, the range the figure can credibly lie in). Cardiovascular death was about 34% lower (0.66, 0.50–0.86)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes: Disputed in this: this was a tracking record done against a Mediterranean diet, and people who eat chilli may have a diet closer to the local traditional way overall. Also note that people with gastro-oesophageal reflux, haemorrhoids or irritable bowel get worse symptoms from chilli, so they do not have to force it

### 34. Drink one or two portions of milk or yoghurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan a day, and it takes no time.
- In plain words: People who drink more than two portions of milk or yoghurt a day have about 17% lower chance of dying over the same period than people who drink none.
- Benefit: The PURE tracking study covered 21 countries and 13.6 ten thousand people, average follow-up 9.1 years, and 6796 people died. People who ate more than two portions of dairy a day had about 17% lower death risk than people who ate none (risk ratio 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.96, the range the figure can credibly lie in), trend P=0.0052
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Notes: Disputed in this: PURE took in a large number of low- and middle-income countries, and being able to drink milk every day there itself shows the household is well off. That layer of interference is hard to strip out cleanly. Also, people who cannot tolerate lactose can switch to yoghurt or low-lactose milk. They do not have to force down cow's milk

### 35. You do not need to give up eggs, but do not eat three or four a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and takes no time.
- In plain words: Half a whole egg more a day raises the chance of dying over the same period by about 7%. Swap that half for egg white, fish, poultry or nuts, and each of those death risks is lower instead. You do not need to give up eggs. Just do not eat three or four a day.
- Benefit: A US tracking study of 52.1 ten thousand people, and 12.9 ten thousand people died. Half a whole egg more a day raised death risk by about 7% (risk ratio 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). Swapping that half whole egg for the same amount of egg white, poultry, fish, dairy, or nuts and beans, death risk was lower, and death risk from cardiovascular disease, cancer and respiratory disease was lower too
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>
- Notes: Disputed. The dispute is large: there are also studies that followed large numbers of people for a long time, and poolings of several studies, that found no link between eggs and the death rate. US dietary guidelines have already dropped the daily cap on cholesterol. What to avoid is only three or four a day. One a day is nothing to worry about

### 36. Take a bath if you can, and do not only shower
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: You spend a bit more on water and electricity. Half an hour each time.
- In plain words: People who bathe almost every day have about 28% lower risk of a cardiovascular event than people who bathe less than twice a week, and brain haemorrhage is about 46% lower.
- Benefit: A study of 3.0 ten thousand people in Japan, followed for 19 years, with 2097 cardiovascular events. People who bathed almost every day had about 28% lower risk of a cardiovascular event than people who bathed no more than twice a week (risk ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). Coronary heart disease was about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke was about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Brain haemorrhage was about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>
- Notes: Disputed in this: this result was produced in a Japanese population and a bathtub culture, and most households in China are not in a position to bathe every day. Also, do not make the water too hot, and do not stay in too long. For older people and people with heart or brain vessel disease, water that is too hot, and staying in too long, is dangerous instead. Every year in Japan a large number of older people die suddenly in the bath

### 37. Keep naps within half an hour, do not go past an hour, and if you can only get through the day by sleeping one or two hours, go and find out why
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Set an alarm before you sleep, and it takes almost no willpower.
- In plain words: People whose naps stay within an hour do not have a higher risk of death or chronic disease, and their minds are a bit clearer. People who sleep past an hour have about three tenths higher risk of coronary heart disease, and about two tenths higher risk of diabetes and obesity. So set an alarm and wake yourself at half an hour. If you can only get through the day by sleeping one or two hours, that is a signal to go and get checked.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses and 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review means pooling existing pooled studies once more. The conclusion: people whose naps were under 60 minutes had no rise in overall death rate or in chronic-disease risk, and the improvement in mental performance was also the clearest (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). A short nap of 20 to 30 minutes improved athletic performance the most (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). People whose naps went past 60 minutes had about 30% higher risk of coronary heart disease, and about 20% higher risk of diabetes and obesity. Another study had 1338 people over 56 wear a wrist watch that measured naps directly, instead of filling in a form themselves. Follow-up was up to 19 years, and 926 people died in that time. Each extra hour of napping raised death risk by about 13% (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). One extra nap a day raised it by about 7% (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). People who napped in the morning were about 30% higher than people who napped earlier in the afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Du P, Li J, Hua Z, 等 (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013>；Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, 等 (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938>；Dashti HS 等 (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Notes: Disputed. Both of the studies above only recorded, with no groups. People who nap a long time are already more likely to have sleep apnoea, anaemia, low thyroid function, depression or some other chronic disease. So how much of "people who sleep longer die more" is caused by the nap itself is not clear. Causal inference done with genes (Mendelian randomisation, 45.3 ten thousand people plus 54.1 ten thousand people) supports only one thing: the more often people nap, the a bit higher the blood pressure and the waist. It did not find a causal effect of napping on coronary heart disease or diabetes. So do not treat "nap less" as a way to lower the death rate. The half about "go and find out why" is the author's suggestion, and it is grade C: if you are so sleepy in the day that you have to sleep a long time, first check sleep at night. Snoring and stopping breathing in sleep need a check for sleep apnoea, and then a blood count and the thyroid. How a short nap wakes you up is in §3.11 (sleep 10 minutes when you get sleepy in the afternoon). How long to sleep at night is in §2.13 (sleep 7 hours a night).

### 38. Make up the sleep the next night after a late night; do not save it for the weekend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The one or two hours you make up are hours you already want to sleep.
- In plain words: People who did not make the sleep up the next night after sleeping short had about 15% higher death risk than people with a regular schedule, and people who were badly short and still did not make it up were about 42% higher. People who did make it up showed no rise in risk. So after a late night, go to bed early the next evening.
- Benefit: UK Biobank, 85,618 people, average age 61.8, wrist watches recording sleep day by day, median follow-up 8 years. The study first worked out each person's own sleep need, and called a night "short" when they slept 2.5 hours or more under what they needed. The first night after that short stretch counted as a "make-up night", and sleeping more than they needed that night counted as made up. Split that way into five patterns. Against people with a regular schedule, people who slept short and did not make it up had about 15% higher all-cause death risk (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). People whose shortfall added up to more than 3.5 hours and who still did not make it up were about 42% higher (HR 1.42, 1.24–1.63). Both groups that did make it up had no significant link, and among them making it up after a short night was HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Among people who already slept short, not making it up was about 19% higher (1.19, 1.01–1.40), and a severe shortfall with no make-up was about 38% higher (1.38, 1.17–1.63). This result also came out again in another set of 4,586 people in a US health survey.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, 等 (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Notes: This is a study that only recorded, with no groups. People who cannot make the sleep up may already be busier and in worse health, and that layer cannot be stripped out cleanly. "The two groups that made it up showed no rise in risk" only means no difference was found. Their point estimates are still greater than 1, so making it up does not equal being fine. So after a late night, pay it back as soon as you can, and you also cannot use this as a reason to stay up whenever you like. The make-up sleep in this study means sleeping a bit more on the first night after a short night. It does not include saving it all for the weekend. Making "stay up on work days, make it up at the weekend" a fixed weekly rhythm is called social jet lag, and that itself is linked to cardiovascular disease, see §2.13 (sleep about 7 hours a night, and keep the schedule fixed). The entry on getting up at a fixed time at the weekend too is in §3.2 (keep a fixed wake-up time, at the weekend as well), and it does not clash with this entry: this entry has you go to bed early that night, not sleep until noon the next day.

### 39. The longer you work night shifts, the higher the cardiovascular risk; move off them early if you can
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Moving posts or changing jobs may mean less pay, and the night-shift allowance goes too. Finding a job takes time, and making up your mind is hard. If you count this in when you are choosing a job, the cost is zero.
- In plain words: People on night shifts have about 13% more cardiovascular disease than people not on night shifts, and about 27% more cardiovascular death. And the longer you do it, the more: each extra 5 years of night shifts adds about 7% more cardiovascular disease. So count this by the years, and move off the shift early if you can. As for night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much softer than the claim that goes around.
- Benefit: Counted together, 23 tracking studies, recording only, no groups. People on night shifts had about 13% higher risk of a cardiovascular event (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16, this is the range the figure can credibly lie in). Cardiovascular death was about 27% higher (RR 1.27, 1.18–1.36). By years: each extra 5 years of night shifts raised the rate of cardiovascular disease by about another 7% (RR 1.07, 1.04–1.09), and cardiovascular death by about another 5% (RR 1.05, 1.03–1.06). By disease, coronary heart disease incidence was about 22% higher (1.22, 1.16–1.28), coronary death about 22% higher (1.22, 1.10–1.36), ischaemic-heart-disease death about 39% higher (1.39, 1.06–1.84), and stroke death about 49% higher (1.49, 1.04–2.12). The item for stroke incidence was not significant (1.06, 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Xi J, Ma W, Tao Y, 等 (2025). Association between night shift work and cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1668848>；Esposito G, Bravi F, Santucci C, 等 (2025). Night shift work and breast cancer risk in healthcare workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Occupational Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqaf040>；Shen QM, Li ZY, Tan YT, 等 (2026). Night shift work and risk of total and site-specific cancer: results from a prospective cohort study among Chinese men. Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health. <https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4290>；Czeisler CA, Johnson MP, Duffy JF, 等 (1990). Exposure to bright light and darkness to treat physiologic maladaptation to night work. New England Journal of Medicine, 322(18), 1253-1259. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199005033221801>
- Notes: These are all studies that only recorded, with no groups. People on night shifts already differ from others in smoking, weight and income, and the adjustment is not clean. The claim that night shifts cause cancer has much softer evidence than the claim that goes around. In an analysis that pooled 12 studies and 12,132 breast-cancer cases, people who had worked night shifts had only about 5% higher risk, and the credible range crossed 1 (RR 1.05, 0.96–1.14). The band that had done a full 20 years was about 25% higher (1.25, 1.01–1.55), but after the authors ran an influence analysis this item was no longer significant, and after correcting for possible publication bias it was close to 1. Their own words were that this link is "far from established". Another study that followed Chinese men for 16.1 years, with 8,202 cancer cases, found no link between night shifts and overall cancer or most common cancers. Only pancreatic cancer, in the band of 11 to 20 years accumulated, was about 59% higher (HR 1.59, 1.09–2.31). So this entry is counted on cardiovascular disease, not on cancer. This does not mean everyone should leave a night-shift post. You count the account that is counted by years yourself. If you are already doing it, first change what you can change: quitting smoking is in §2.1 (quit smoking, the earlier the better), blood pressure and blood lipids are in §2.12 (if you have high blood pressure or high blood lipids, take the medicine on schedule as the doctor said), and how to make up sleep after a night shift is in §2.38 (make up the sleep the next night after a late night). There are two other things you can try: bunch eating into the daytime, and move the body clock with strong light plus strict darkness in the day. The second is the only method that has been shown to actually move the body clock. Under ordinary indoor lighting, six night shifts in a row left the body clock unmoved. Switch to strong light of 7000 to 12000 lux plus near-total darkness in the day, and in four days it had flipped right over. **The light at night has to be bright enough, sleep in the day has to be strictly darkened, and you wear sunglasses on the way home to block the morning light. Miss any one of the three and it does not count**, and missing the last one means the whole thing was wasted. The endpoints of both methods only reach metabolic measures and sleep. No trial has measured whether doing them lowers cardiovascular risk. If you live by day again on your days off, the clock gets pushed back. The doses, the original figures and the other sources, together with how the body tells the time, are all in [docs/生物钟和夜班.md](../../../docs/生物钟和夜班.md).

### 40. Buy pre-packed cooking oil with an SC number, and do not buy loose home-pressed peanut oil from a small workshop
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: You buy it somewhere else, and the money is about the same. You only look once at whether the pack has a food-production licence number starting with SC. The hard part is persuading older relatives who are used to buying home-pressed oil.
- In plain words: Peanuts that get damp and mouldy grow aflatoxin. It is a definite carcinogen, and it mainly hurts the liver. After Guangzhou brought small workshops under control, residents who often ate home-pressed oil had about three and a half tenths fewer abnormal liver-function results. Pregnant women in Guangxi who ate home-pressed peanut oil had about nine tenths higher risk of a low-birth-weight baby, and about eight tenths higher risk of a preterm birth.
- Benefit: Aflatoxin is a class 1 carcinogen as set by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (it is established that it can give people cancer), mainly liver cancer. A study in Guangzhou measured aflatoxin B1 in home-pressed peanut oil from small workshops from 2010 to 2022. The median was 1.29 μg/kg, and the middle half of the samples sat between 0.12 and 6.58. After the local rules on small workshops came out, the toxin in the oil dropped at once by 2.865 μg/kg, and then by another 2.593 each year after that. The share of abnormal liver function among residents in the area who often ate home-pressed oil fell with it (PR 0.650, 95% CI 0.469–0.902, the range the figure can credibly lie in), about 35% lower. In a Guangxi study that followed 1611 pregnant women, 81.7% ate home-pressed peanut oil. The women who ate it had about nine tenths higher risk of a low-birth-weight baby (aOR 1.9, 1.1–3.2), and about eight tenths higher risk of preterm birth (aOR 1.8, 1.1–3.0).
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Lei J, Li Y, Wang Y, 等 (2024). The impact of small food workshops management regulations on aflatoxin B1 in home-made peanut oil and the liver function of high-consumption area residents: an interrupted time series study in Guangzhou, China. Frontiers in Public Health, 12. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1484414>；Zhong Y, Lu H, Jiang Y, 等 (2024). Global Health Action, 17. <https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2024.2336312>；IARC (2012). Chemical Agents and Related Occupations. IARC Monographs on the Evaluation of Carcinogenic Risks to Humans, Vol 100F. <https://publications.iarc.fr/123>；国家卫生计生委、国家食品药品监督管理总局 (2017). 食品安全国家标准 食品中真菌毒素限量（GB 2761-2017）
- Notes: Both studies only tracked and recorded, with no control group. The endpoint of the Guangzhou study is a liver-function test, not liver cancer and not death. The median toxin in the home-pressed oil was in fact under the national limit of 20 μg/kg for peanut oil. The risk comes from eating it for years, and from the odd high batch. The national limit applies to all peanut oil, but pre-packed oil has to pass a production licence and a factory test, while small workshops are run under each province's own rules and are sampled much less. ### 41. Cook with vegetable oil instead of lard and butter; you do not need to keep switching oils for your health, and do not count on flaxseed oil to protect the heart
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It does not cost more money. Vegetable oil is already the main oil in most households. The hard part is dropping the habit of stir-frying and mixing rice with lard.
- In plain words: Eating less saturated fat such as lard and butter, and switching to vegetable oil, cuts events such as heart disease and stroke by about one tenth and seven hundredths, but the overall death risk showed no change. Switching back and forth among soybean oil, corn oil and rapeseed oil, there is no evidence of any difference. Eating more flaxseed oil also showed no drop in heart-disease risk.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 15 randomised trials and 56,675 people, each trial lasting at least two years. Eating less saturated fat, cardiovascular events together were about 17% lower (RR 0.83, 0.70–0.98). Overall death rate barely changed (RR 0.96, 0.90–1.03), and cardiovascular death did not change either (RR 0.95, 0.80–1.12). Switching to polyunsaturated fat (the main part of soybean oil and corn oil) and switching to carbohydrate showed no difference in effect. Data on switching to monounsaturated fat (the main part of rapeseed oil and olive oil) are too thin to say. Another Cochrane review pooled 19 randomised trials and 6461 people. Eating more omega-6 (linoleic acid) gave RR 1.00 (0.88–1.12) for overall death, and RR 0.97 (0.81–1.15) for cardiovascular events. No benefit showed, and no harm showed. A third review pooled 86 randomised trials and 162,796 people. Eating more plant-source omega-3 (the main part of flaxseed oil and perilla oil) gave RR 1.01 (0.84–1.20) for overall death, and RR 1.00 (0.82–1.22) for coronary events. Basically no effect.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hooper L, Martin N, Jimoh OF, 等 (2020). Reduction in saturated fat for cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (5), CD011737. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011737.pub3>；Hooper L, Al-Khudairy L, Abdelhamid AS, 等 (2018). Omega-6 fats for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11), CD011094. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011094.pub4>；Abdelhamid AS, Brown TJ, Brainard JS, 等 (2020). Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (3), CD003177. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003177.pub5>；中国营养学会 (2022). 中国居民膳食指南（2022）. 人民卫生出版社
- Notes: Disputed: some people hold that soybean oil and corn oil have too much omega-6, which promotes inflammation, so you should eat less of them and switch oils. The randomised trials in the omega-6 review above showed no harm, and the authors rated the evidence quality as low themselves. The benefit size is set at medium from cardiovascular events being 17% lower. The overall death rate did not change. Whatever the oil, control the amount. The dietary guideline suggests 25 to 30 grams of cooking oil per person per day.

### 42. Turn the range hood on when you stir-fry or deep-fry, and leave it on from the moment you light the flame until you are done
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: If the home already has a range hood, you only spend a little more on electricity. If it does not, buying one with installation is a few hundred to over a thousand yuan. The hard part is remembering to turn it on every time you light the flame, and not turning it off because it is noisy.
- In plain words: Chinese women who do not smoke, and who never or rarely turn the range hood on while stir-frying, have about twice the risk of lung cancer as women who usually leave it on. Having lived in a home with poor kitchen ventilation also raises the risk by about five tenths, and it is clearest in households that burned coal.
- Benefit: Counted together, 10 case-control studies, all of them on Chinese women who did not smoke. Case-control means comparing people with lung cancer to people without it, and asking back about past habits. People exposed to cooking fumes had about 1.74 times the chance of lung cancer (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.57–1.94, the range the figure can credibly lie in). Of those, 4 studies compared people exposed to fumes who also did not use a range hood, and that was about 2.11 times (1.54–2.89). A study in Taiwan compared 1302 women with lung cancer who did not smoke with 1302 women who did not have it. It looked only at people who had cooked for ten years or more. People who had the range hood on for more than two thirds of those years, against people who had it on for less than one third, had about half the risk (odds ratio 0.49, 0.32–0.76). A study in Shanghai followed people forward: 7.1 ten thousand women who did not smoke, from 1996 to 2009, and 429 lung cancers were found. Women who said they had lived in a home with poor kitchen ventilation had about 49% higher risk of lung cancer (HR 1.49, 1.15–1.95). Women who burned coal and also had poor ventilation for 20 years or more had about 2.03 times (HR 2.03, 1.35–3.05). Looking only at whether they had ever burned coal, no difference showed (HR 1.03, 0.84–1.26). Which oil they used for stir-frying also showed no difference.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Xue Y, Jiang Y, Jin S, Li Y (2016). Association between cooking oil fume exposure and lung cancer among Chinese nonsmoking women: a meta-analysis. OncoTargets and Therapy, 9, 2987-2992. <https://doi.org/10.2147/OTT.S100949>；Chen TY, Fang YH, Chen HL, 等 (2020). Impact of cooking oil fume exposure and fume extractor use on lung cancer risk in non-smoking Han Chinese women. Scientific Reports, 10, 6774. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-63656-7>；Kim C, Gao YT, Xiang YB, 等 (2015). Home kitchen ventilation, cooking fuels, and lung cancer risk in a prospective cohort of never smoking women in Shanghai, China. International Journal of Cancer, 136(3), 632-638. <https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.29020>
- Notes: Disputed: the figures that support turning the range hood on come mainly from case-control studies. After the diagnosis, patients recall the past, and they easily under-report how often they turned the hood on, so the figures may be on the high side. In the Shanghai study that followed people forward, the rise in risk fell mainly on households that burned coal and also had poor ventilation. How much difference the hood makes in households that only burn gas, that study cannot give a separate figure. So the evidence grade is only B, and the benefit is set at large from a risk difference of about two times. Not burning coal or wood for cooking is in §2.29 (do not burn coal or wood for cooking and heating). The person this entry benefits is mainly whoever usually holds the ladle at home.

### 43. If hot flushes and night sweats around menopause get in the way of life, go to gynaecology and have hormone treatment assessed; do not just endure it, do not buy the drugs yourself, and do not use it to prevent heart disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One visit to gynaecology or a menopause clinic. The doctor asks the history and looks at whether you have a reason not to use hormones, and writes a prescription only if it fits. Once you are on it, you come back for checks as the doctor requires. The hard part is putting down the fear that "touch a hormone and you get cancer", and also not going to buy it yourself because you heard it fights ageing.
- In plain words: For hot flushes and night sweats around menopause, hormone treatment cuts the number of hot flushes by about 75% against a placebo. The price is a bit more breast cancer, blood clots and stroke, about 8 extra people a year in each of those, per 10 thousand women. Women who start between 50 and 59 do not have a higher overall death rate. It cannot be used to prevent heart disease or dementia in old age.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 24 randomised trials and 3329 people, comparing oral hormones with placebo. Women on hormones had about 75% fewer hot flushes a week than women on placebo (95% CI 64.3–82.3, the range the figure can credibly lie in). Women who had had the uterus removed and used oestrogen only, followed for a cumulative 13 years, had breast cancer about 21% lower instead (HR 0.79, 0.65–0.97). Followed out to 18 years, the 50 to 59 group was 0.89 (0.79–1.01), and that was no longer significant either. The Cochrane review of 2017 looked only at women aged 50 to 59, and the only thing clearly higher was blood clots when oestrogen and progestogen were used together, with an absolute risk under 1/500. Women over 65 on combined oestrogen and progestogen had dementia rise, over 4 years, from 9 cases per thousand people to 11 to 30 cases. The US Preventive Services Task Force in 2022 opposed using hormones to prevent chronic disease (a grade D recommendation), and the conclusion was that there is no net benefit.

The following kinds of people are generally not suited to it: people who have had breast cancer, people who have had a blood clot, people with high cardiovascular risk, and people who are very overweight. The doctor judges the specifics. A woman who still has a uterus and uses oestrogen alone will have more endometrial cancer, so she also uses a progestogen. Only a woman whose uterus has been removed can use oestrogen alone. Hormones can also cut fractures, but they are usually considered only when other drugs against osteoporosis do not fit. Osteoporosis is first in §1.39 (a bone-density check) and §1.40 (if osteoporosis is found, take the medicine). The benefit size is set at large from hot flushes being about 75% fewer. The endpoint is the symptom, not the overall death rate. The person this entry benefits is mainly you yourself, and it also includes your mother and your spouse.
