# 34. Do not let the medicines you keep at home hurt you

Fever medicines, painkillers, cold remedies, stomach medicines and anti-diarrhoeal drugs — you can buy many of them yourself at a pharmacy. Being able to buy one without a prescription does not mean you can take it any way you like. This section does not cover which drug to take for which illness. It only picks out the few things where one action avoids a serious outcome.

Every entry here is counted on the mortality lens, including health endpoints like liver failure, stomach bleeding and newborn kidney failure. The benefit is mostly "one fewer serious accident". The research cannot give a "doing this cuts it by X" number, so the benefit magnitude is judged from how serious the consequence is, and each entry's notes say what that judgement rested on.

Where something is already written elsewhere, this section only points the way. For what to do first after someone swallows a medicine or a cleaning product, see §13.20 (swallowing something by mistake). For taking the full course of a chronic-disease drug as your doctor prescribed, see §16.1 (take the full course your doctor prescribed). For buying prescription drugs online, where a prescription has to be reviewed first, see §28.6 (get a prescription at a hospital for weight-loss drugs).

The people who benefit from this section's entries are you and your family, the top two tiers.

### 1. Before taking two cold remedies or two painkillers at the same time, read the ingredient lists, and let only one of them contain paracetamol (acetaminophen)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You glance at the ingredient list on each box, one or two minutes.
- In plain words: Many cold remedies have paracetamol mixed in, so taking two boxes together means a double dose, and too much of it harms the liver. In the United States, nearly three in ten of the patients whose acute liver failure it caused died without getting a liver transplant. The Chinese package insert advises at most 2 grams a day, and no alcohol while you are taking it.
- Benefit: 22 hospitals in the United States consecutively registered 662 cases of acute liver failure over 6 years. Of these, 275 cases (42%) were caused by paracetamol, and the median dose swallowed was 24 grams. Among those 275 people, 131 (48%) took too much by accident, not on purpose. Among the people who took too much by accident, 38% were taking two or more medicines containing this ingredient at the same time. Among those 275 people, 27% died without getting a liver transplant, and 8% received a liver transplant. In 2020 the National Medical Products Administration required over-the-counter package inserts to state that an overdose can cause severe liver injury, and that the recommended maximum is no more than 2 grams a day. The insert must also state that you should as far as possible avoid combining it with medicines containing paracetamol or other fever-and-pain drugs. United States regulations require this kind of over-the-counter medicine to name three liver-harming situations in the first line of its warning: going over the daily maximum, taking it together with other medicines containing paracetamol, and drinking more than 3 drinks a day while taking it.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Larson AM, Polson J, Fontana RJ, et al.; Acute Liver Failure Study Group (2005). Acetaminophen-induced acute liver failure: results of a United States multicenter, prospective study. Hepatology, 42(6):1364-1372. <https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.20948>；国家药监局关于修订对乙酰氨基酚常释及缓释制剂说明书的公告（2020年第15号）及附件2《对乙酰氨基酚常释制剂非处方药说明书修订要求》（湖南省药品监督管理局转载）. <http://mpa.hunan.gov.cn/mpa/xxgk/tzgg/wjtz/202003/t20200311_11809335.html>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(1)(iii)(A) Liver warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Notes: Paracetamol also goes by the Chinese name 扑热息痛. A compound cold remedy whose generic name carries the two characters 氨酚 — 氨酚伪麻 or 氨咖黄敏, for example — contains this ingredient. If you are unsure, take all the boxes to the pharmacist at the pharmacy. B is assigned because those United States figures are a case registry with no control group, so you cannot work out how much trouble reading the ingredient list avoids. The benefit is set to "large" from the consequence: nearly three in ten of the acute liver failure patients died.

### 2. For a child's fever do not use aspirin, nimesulide or metamizole (dipyrone), and do not give a minor a cold remedy that contains metamizole
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You glance at the generic name and the ingredient list when you buy a fever medicine.
- In plain words: Do not use any of these three on a child's fever. Aspirin is linked to Reye's syndrome, which damages the brain and the liver, and about three in ten of the sick children recorded in the United States died. Cases dropped by more than ninety percent after the warning went out. Nimesulide is banned under age 12, and metamizole is banned under age 18.
- Benefit: Reye's syndrome causes brain disease and fatty change in the liver, and it usually appears after flu or chickenpox. In nationwide surveillance in the United States from 1981 through 1997, 1207 cases were reported in people under 18. In 82% of the sick children, salicylate was detectable in the blood, which is the ingredient in aspirin and drugs like it. The overall case fatality rate was 31%. Warnings about salicylate drugs began in 1980. That year 555 cases were reported, the peak, and from 1987 on there were no more than 36 cases a year. In 2011 the State Food and Drug Administration ruled that oral nimesulide preparations must not be used in children under 12. In 2020 the National Medical Products Administration ruled that metamizole tablets must not be used in adolescents and children under 18. Compound Artemisia-metamizole tablets (复方青蒿安乃近片), Zhongganling tablets and Zhongganling capsules (重感灵, a Chinese compound cold remedy), which contain metamizole, are likewise banned under 18.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Belay ED, Bresee JS, Holman RC, Khan AS, Shahriari A, Schonberger LB (1999). Reye's syndrome in the United States from 1981 through 1997. New England Journal of Medicine, 340(18):1377-1382. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199905063401801>；关于加强尼美舒利口服制剂使用管理的通知（国食药监安〔2011〕209号）. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/gzwj/gzwjyp/20110515145101552.html>；国家药监局关于修订安乃近相关品种说明书的公告（2020年第34号）及附件1至3. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/directory/web/nmpa/xxgk/ggtg/ypggtg/ypshmshxdgg/20200317171201660.html>
- Notes: Metamizole is not a first choice for adults either. Its package insert states that it may cause agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia and anaphylactic shock, and that it is only for short-term use when the illness is acute and severe and there is no other effective drug. Zhongganling sounds from its name like an ordinary cold remedy, but there is metamizole in it. Ask a paediatrician or a pharmacist which fever medicine your child should use and how much to give by weight. If a baby under 3 months has a fever, go straight to hospital, see §20.8 (go straight to hospital at 38 °C). B is assigned because the Reye's syndrome numbers come from surveillance reports, and the drop in cases followed the warning in time rather than coming from a controlled trial. The benefit is set to "large" because cases fell from 555 a year to under 36 after the warning.

### 3. If you are over 60, have bled from the stomach, or take anticoagulants or steroids, ask a doctor before taking ibuprofen or a painkiller like it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You ask a doctor or pharmacist once.
- In plain words: Painkillers like ibuprofen, naproxen and aspirin can injure the stomach. In the trials of large doses, people taking ibuprofen had about 4 times the risk of stomach bleeding or a hole in the gut that people not taking it had. The risk is higher if you are older, have bled from the stomach before, also take anticoagulants or steroids, or drink every day.
- Benefit: These drugs are called non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and the common ones are ibuprofen, naproxen and diclofenac. One meta-analysis pooled 280 randomised trials of these drugs against placebo, 124513 people in all. Compared with placebo, the risk of a perforation, obstruction or bleed in the upper digestive tract was: ibuprofen RR 3.97 (95% CI 2.22–7.10, about 4 times), naproxen 4.22 (2.71–6.56), diclofenac 1.89 (1.16–3.09). All of these drugs roughly doubled the risk of heart failure. United States regulations require this kind of over-the-counter drug to carry a stomach bleeding warning. The warning lists six groups at higher risk: people over 60, people who have had a stomach ulcer or stomach bleeding, people taking anticoagulants or steroids, people taking another drug of this kind at the same time, people drinking more than 3 drinks a day while taking it, and people taking more of it or taking it for longer than the insert says.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Coxib and traditional NSAID Trialists' (CNT) Collaboration; Bhala N, Emberson J, Merhi A, et al. (2013). Vascular and upper gastrointestinal effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs: meta-analyses of individual participant data from randomised trials. Lancet, 382(9894):769-779. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)60900-9>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(2)(iii)(A) Stomach bleeding warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Notes: The paper's conclusion is about large-dose use. It gives no separate number for the risk of taking the insert's dose for a day or two now and then. Many compound cold remedies contain an ingredient of this kind too, so taking one together with a painkiller doubles up, and you should read the ingredient list first. If your doctor has you on long-term low-dose aspirin to prevent clots, do not stop it on your own, and do not add a painkiller on your own either. For how to use these painkillers for period pain, and when to go to a gynaecologist, see §3.25 (take ibuprofen-type painkillers for period pain, do not just endure it).

### 4. When a child under 2 has a cold, do not give compound cold remedies or cough medicine on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and you save what the medicine would cost.
- In plain words: The National Medical Products Administration requires 14 children's compound cold remedies to carry a warning: parents should not give the product to a child under 2 on their own, and should use it under the guidance of a doctor or pharmacist. In the trials done in children, cough medicines and anti-allergy medicines worked no better than placebo.
- Benefit: Announcement No. 57 of 2021 from the National Medical Products Administration revised the package inserts of 14 products, among them paracetamol-pseudoephedrine-dextromethorphan oral solution (氨酚麻美口服溶液), paediatric paracetamol and chlorphenamine granules (小儿氨酚黄那敏颗粒) and paediatric paracetamol and amantadine granules (小儿氨酚烷胺颗粒). The added warning reads: "parents or guardians are advised not to give this product to infants and toddlers under 2 on their own; it should be used under the guidance of a doctor or pharmacist". The precautions added: use it strictly according to the dose and method in the package insert, and avoid an overdose. They also added: avoid taking it together with cold remedies containing the same or similar active ingredients. One Cochrane systematic review included 29 randomised controlled trials, 10 of them done in children. In the children's trials, none of these groups did better than placebo: cough medicines, antihistamines (the anti-allergy ingredient), antihistamines plus a decongestant (the ingredient that clears the nose), and cough medicine plus a bronchodilator. 21 studies reported adverse effects, and the preparations containing antihistamines and dextromethorphan had more of them.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国家药监局关于修订氨酚麻美口服溶液等14个品种药品说明书的公告（2021年第57号）及附件. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/directory/web/nmpa/xxgk/ggtg/ypggtg/ypshmshxdgg/20210423105404141.html>；Smith SM, Schroeder K, Fahey T (2014). Over-the-counter (OTC) medications for acute cough in children and adults in community settings. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11):CD001831. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001831.pub5>
- Notes: Many of these 14 products have 氨酚 in the name and contain paracetamol as well, so taking one together with a fever medicine doubles up, see §34.1 (paracetamol). One trial in that review found honey better than placebo, but you must not give honey to a child under 1, see §20.6 (honey). B is assigned because the review had few trials for each class of drug and the authors did not pool a number. The benefit is set to "medium" as a judgement: these drugs have no proven benefit in small children, and the risk is mainly overdose and doubled-up ingredients.

### 5. After 20 weeks of pregnancy, do not take ibuprofen or a painkiller like it on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain words: After 20 weeks of pregnancy, most of the amniotic fluid is made by the baby's kidneys. Taking a drug like ibuprofen then may harm the baby's kidneys and lower the amount of amniotic fluid. Among the reports the United States received, there were newborn deaths. Low-dose aspirin that a doctor prescribes is not covered by this.
- Benefit: The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a safety warning in October 2020. Using non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs after about 20 weeks of pregnancy may cause kidney problems in the fetus and lead to too little amniotic fluid. The warning covers prescription and over-the-counter drugs alike, including aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and celecoxib. The FDA went through adverse event reports up to July 2017 and found 35 cases of too little amniotic fluid or newborn kidney impairment, all with serious outcomes. 5 of them were newborn deaths, all of them with newborn kidney failure. In the literature, most cases of too little amniotic fluid recovered within 72 hours to 6 days after the drug was stopped. The FDA advises pregnant women not to use these drugs after 20 weeks unless a doctor specifically recommends it. Low-dose aspirin of 81 milligrams, prescribed by a doctor to treat a specific problem in pregnancy, is not covered by this limit. When you need something for pain or fever, the FDA mentions paracetamol and other drugs as options, and says to ask a pharmacist or doctor first.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2020-10-15). FDA recommends avoiding use of NSAIDs in pregnancy at 20 weeks or later because they can result in low amniotic fluid. Drug Safety Communication. <https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-recommends-avoiding-use-nsaids-pregnancy-20-weeks-or-later-because-they-can-result-low-amniotic>
- Notes: The FDA points out that many over-the-counter medicines for colds, flu and sleeplessness contain ingredients of this kind, so read the ingredient list. The United States over-the-counter label used to warn only about the last 3 months of pregnancy, and this time the line moved earlier, to about 20 weeks. For low-dose aspirin against pre-eclampsia, see §27.5 (low-dose aspirin). B is assigned because the numbers come from adverse event reports, and how many people took the drug with nothing going wrong is not known. The benefit is set to "medium" as a judgement: the outcome is serious, but it happens rarely.

### 6. If you buy omeprazole yourself for acid reflux and heartburn, take it for at most 7 days; if you have trouble swallowing, vomit blood or pass black stools, go straight to hospital
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You take it for the number of days the insert gives.
- In plain words: You have been able to buy enteric-coated omeprazole tablets without a prescription since 2022, but the insert says not to take it on your own for more than 7 days, and not to take it again within two months. Do not take it on your own if you have trouble swallowing or pain when swallowing, vomit blood, or pass blood or black stools, because these can be signs of something serious. The drug can also mask the symptoms of stomach cancer and hold up the diagnosis.
- Benefit: Announcement No. 68 of 2022 from the National Medical Products Administration moved enteric-coated omeprazole tablets from prescription to over-the-counter, and published a model package insert at the same time. The model gives the indication as "short-term relief of heartburn and acid reflux symptoms caused by excess stomach acid". The precautions state: do not use it for more than 7 days, and consult a doctor or pharmacist if the symptoms do not ease. Do not take it again within two months, and seek care at once if the symptoms keep coming back. Do not use it if you have trouble swallowing or pain when swallowing, vomit blood, or pass blood or black stools, because these can be signs of something serious. When alarm symptoms appear — clear weight loss, repeated vomiting, trouble swallowing, vomiting blood or black stools — a malignant tumour has to be ruled out first, because the treatment can mask the symptoms and hold up the diagnosis. If you are over 55 and have new symptoms, or your symptoms have changed, consult a doctor. People taking clopidogrel should avoid taking omeprazole at the same time.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国家药监局关于奥美拉唑肠溶片处方药转换为非处方药的公告（2022年第68号）及附件2《非处方药说明书范本》. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/yaopin/ypggtg/20220826171517123.html>
- Notes: Clopidogrel is the clot-prevention drug commonly taken by people who have had a stent put in or have had a heart attack. Omeprazole weakens it, so ask your doctor first if you are taking it. B is assigned because these are package insert rules, and no study has worked out how much trouble following them avoids. The benefit is set to "medium" as a judgement: what it prevents is stomach bleeding or stomach cancer being left too late, but most acid reflux and heartburn is not serious.

### 7. Do not ask your doctor for antibiotics for a common cold
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and you save what the medicine would cost.
- In plain words: Taking antibiotics for a cold does not get you better any faster than not taking them. For adults who take them, the risk of side effects is more than twice the risk of not taking them. If you have had a runny nose with pus for less than 10 days, antibiotics have no proven benefit either.
- Benefit: One Cochrane systematic review pooled 6 randomised trials with 1147 people. Comparing antibiotics with placebo for a cold, the share of people who were not better or whose symptoms persisted showed no clear difference (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.60–1.14, the difference was not significant). The risk of adverse effects was 1.8 times that in the placebo group (95% CI 1.01–3.21). Among adults it was 2.62 times (1.32–5.18), and in children there was no increase (0.91, 0.51–1.63). For acute purulent rhinitis, a runny nose with pus lasting less than 10 days, antibiotics had no clear benefit either (RR 0.73, 0.47–1.13). Adverse effects were more common (RR 1.46, 1.10–1.94, about 46% higher). The authors conclude that routine antibiotics are not recommended in either situation.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Kenealy T, Arroll B (2025). Antibiotics for the common cold and acute purulent rhinitis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 11:CD000247. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000247.pub4>
- Notes: This version was republished in 2025, and the abstract states that the literature search ran up to 2013. Antibiotics are prescription drugs in China and a pharmacy has to see a prescription to sell them, and you should not take leftover antibiotics at home on your own either. The benefit is set to "medium" as a judgement: what it prevents is adverse effects, not death.

### 8. When you have diarrhoea, drink oral rehydration salts first to replace the water, and do not give an anti-diarrhoeal drug to a child under 5
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A box of oral rehydration salts costs a few yuan to a bit over ten yuan. Keep a box at home.
- In plain words: With diarrhoea, what matters most is replacing the water, not stopping the diarrhoea. Mix oral rehydration salts with water as the insert says and drink that, and do not use fruit juice drinks instead, because something that sweet leaves you shorter of water. Do not give loperamide or an anti-diarrhoeal drug like it to a child under 5, because in a serious case the bowel gets blocked, which can kill.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization's manual on treating diarrhoea states that "antidiarrhoeal" drugs and anti-vomiting drugs have no practical benefit for acute or persistent diarrhoea in children. They do not prevent dehydration and they do not improve nutrition. Some have dangerous, sometimes fatal side effects and must never be given to a child under 5. Drugs like loperamide, which slow the bowel down, may cause severe paralytic ileus, which can kill, and may also drag the infection out. The manual also states that drinks with too much sugar pull the body's water into the bowel and cause high-sodium dehydration, with soft drinks and commercial fruit juice drinks as examples. What the WHO manual recommends is the low-osmolarity formula, with a total osmolarity of 245 mOsm/L. Compared with the old formula, it cut unplanned intravenous fluids by 33%. One Cochrane review pooled 8 trials with a similar result (OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.79, about four in ten fewer).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: World Health Organization (2005). The treatment of diarrhoea: a manual for physicians and other senior health workers, 4th revision. <https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43209>；Hahn S, Kim S, Garner P (2002). Reduced osmolarity oral rehydration solution for treating dehydration caused by acute diarrhoea in children. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (1):CD002847. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002847>
- Notes: When you buy them, look at the osmolarity on the insert and pick the low-osmolarity formula. The manual states that blood in a child's stool is usually bacterial dysentery, and that you have to see a doctor for antibiotics rather than only replacing fluids at home. The benefit is set to "medium" as a judgement: the numbers to hand compare two rehydration salt formulas, not drinking them against not drinking them.

### 9. Do not take a painkiller on more than 15 days in a month, or a compound painkiller on more than 10 days, or the headache may be coming from the medicine
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to keep count of the days you take it, and hold back from reaching for it.
- In plain words: If headaches keep coming back and you take a painkiller every day, the pain may get worse the more you take. The International Classification of Headache Disorders calls this medication-overuse headache. Taking an ordinary painkiller on 15 or more days a month, or a compound painkiller on 10 or more days, for more than 3 months running, is enough to count. Most people get better after they stop the drug they were taking too much of.
- Benefit: The International Headache Society's International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition, gives the diagnostic criteria. A person who already had a headache disorder has headache on 15 or more days a month, and at the same time has regularly overused headache medicine for more than 3 months. For non-opioid painkillers such as paracetamol, aspirin and ibuprofen, the overuse threshold is 15 or more days a month. For compound painkillers the threshold is 10 or more days a month. A compound painkiller means two or more classes of drug with a pain-relieving effect put together, or one with an added helper ingredient such as caffeine, for example paracetamol plus codeine. If you rotate several non-opioid painkillers, the days are added together. The classification notes that epidemiological evidence from several countries shows that more than half of the people with headache on 15 or more days a month have this kind of headache. Most patients get better after stopping the overused drug, and preventive headache treatment also works better.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society (2018). The International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition. Cephalalgia, 38(1):1-211. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0333102417738202>；ICHD-3 在线版 8.2 Medication-overuse headache（含 8.2.3、8.2.5）. <https://ichd-3.org/8-headache-attributed-to-a-substance-or-its-withdrawal/8-2-medication-overuse-headache-moh/>
- Notes: A painkiller tablet with the character 咖 in its name usually has caffeine added, so count it as a compound and use the 10-day limit. If the number of headache days in a month keeps climbing, go to a neurologist instead of relying on more medicine. B is assigned because the classification gives diagnostic thresholds, not a controlled trial. The benefit is set to "medium" as a judgement: what you get back is fewer days of headache, and death is not involved.

### 10. Clear out expired medicines and leftovers you will not use, put them in the hazardous waste bin together with the packaging, and do not keep them at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You go through the medicine box once a year, a bit over ten minutes.
- In plain words: Nearly half of what young children swallow behind an adult's back are prescription tablets. About three in ten of the prescription medicines they swallow belong to the grandparents. Clearing out the medicines you will not use means one less thing a child can find. Put what you clear out into the hazardous waste bin with the packaging, or hand it to a pharmacy that collects old medicine.
- Benefit: Emergency department surveillance data in the United States estimate that from 2004 to 2013, children under 6 who swallowed medicine behind an adult's back made about 640 thousand emergency visits (estimated value 640161 visits). In 2013 alone there were about 59 thousand (59092). From 2010 to 2013, the most common were oral prescription tablets, at 45.9%. Among prescription drugs the most common were opioid painkillers (13.8%) and benzodiazepine sleeping and sedative drugs (12.7%). 5 poison control centres in the United States asked the parents who called them for help in 2017. Among the children who swallowed a prescription medicine, 30.7% took a grandparent's medicine. For more than half (51.5%) of the prescription medicines, an adult had already taken them out of the original packaging before the child got hold of them. In China, the National Hazardous Waste List (2025 edition) lists waste medicines and their packaging produced in everyday household life as hazardous waste within household rubbish. Shanghai's sorting and disposal guidance puts waste medicine under hazardous waste, and states that this includes expired medicines, drug capsules, tablets and inner drug packaging, and that "waste medicine should preferably be put out together with its packaging". Shanghai also requires that expired waste medicine collected from households by pharmacies go through temporary transfer points set up by each district's market regulation department. The United States FDA says that the best way to deal with expired and unused medicine is to hand it to a take-back site.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Lovegrove MC, Weidle NJ, Budnitz DS (2015). Trends in Emergency Department Visits for Unsupervised Pediatric Medication Exposures, 2004-2013. Pediatrics, 136(4):e821-e829. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2015-2092>；Agarwal M, Lovegrove MC, Geller RJ, et al. (2020). Circumstances Involved in Unsupervised Solid Dose Medication Exposures among Young Children. ### 11. While you are on an antibacterial drug like a cephalosporin or metronidazole, and for 7 days after you stop, do not drink alcohol, and do not touch alcohol-containing medicines like Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui or cough syrup
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You go a bit over a week without alcohol, and glance at the ingredients of other medicines to see whether ethanol is in them.
- In plain words: Some cephalosporins and drugs like metronidazole meet alcohol and leave you flushed, your heart racing, your chest tight and vomiting, and in a severe case your blood pressure drops. It is not only spirits and beer, because alcohol-containing medicines like Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui and cough syrup can set it off too. Do not touch any of them while you are taking the drug or in the 7 days after you stop.
- Benefit: This is called a disulfiram-like reaction. A 2023 safe-medication public information piece from the Fujian Provincial Medical Products Administration says that drinking before or after taking some cephalosporin drugs may set off this reaction, which "can seriously harm health and even endanger life". The symptoms include facial flushing, sweating and palpitations, and can go as far as difficulty breathing, a drop in blood pressure and anaphylactic shock. The drugs the piece lists, taken from the Clinical Medication Guidelines of the Pharmacopoeia of the People's Republic of China, include cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefoperazone, metronidazole, tinidazole and furazolidone. The piece states that "for 7 days after taking the drug, drinking alcohol and consuming alcohol-containing medicines, foods and so on is forbidden", with liqueur chocolates and Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui as examples. The reason is that the enzyme the drug suppresses often takes 4 to 5 days to recover. A hospital in Guangzhou compiled 82 cases over 2 years. 57 cases (69.51%) were caused by cephalosporins, and 10 cases (12.20%) by nitroimidazoles (metronidazole, tinidazole and the like). The alcohol came from alcoholic drinks in 40 cases (48.78%) and from ethanol-containing medicines in 31 cases (37.80%). The oral ones included Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui, cough syrup and motherwort fluid extract. Of the 82 cases, 68 were mild, 9 moderate and 5 severe, they eased within 1 to 3 hours, and nobody died. The United States package insert for metronidazole requires no alcohol during treatment and for at least 3 days after stopping, and no products containing propylene glycol either.
- Evidence: B
