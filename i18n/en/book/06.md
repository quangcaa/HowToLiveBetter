# 6. The list of things that look worth it and are not

This section takes in things that "look cheap and effective". Once you open the original studies, their benefit is close to zero, equal to zero, or in some cases negative. The order looks at two things: how much money people spend on it, and how hard the evidence for it is. The earlier an entry is, the more it is worth stopping first.

The entries in this section fall into the blocks below by theme, with entry numbers in brackets.

**Supplements**: a multivitamin (§6.1), ordinary fish oil (§6.2), vitamin D (§6.3), antioxidant supplements to prevent cancer (§6.4), chondroitin for knee arthritis (§6.5), vitamin C to prevent colds (§6.6), spending a lot on health products (§6.10), taking probiotics long term (§6.11), detox and colon-cleanse products (§6.13), heavy calcium after a fracture (§6.30).

**Check-ups and small findings**: a whole-body PET-CT and a tumour-marker package (§6.7), a general check-up package (§6.18), high uric acid with no pain, do not start the drug (§6.19), gallstones with no pain, do not have the gallbladder out (§6.20), do not give up calcium to prevent kidney stones (§6.21).

**Health sayings**: a band to help you lose weight (§6.8), buying organic food (§6.9), cold showers to boost immunity (§6.12), do not force yourself to drink water (§6.14), blue-light glasses (§6.16), treating blood donation as health care (§6.17), breakfast or light fasting to control weight (§6.26), a "correct" lifting posture to prevent back pain (§6.29).

**Spending money on luck and mood**: paying for fortune-telling and horoscopes (§6.15), crystals and pixiu (§6.22), buying things to improve your mood (§6.23), spending more to move up a notch among the people around you (§6.24).

**Willpower and sex**: willpower is like a muscle (§6.25), masturbation harms the body (§6.27), conversion of sexual orientation (§6.28).

### 1. Do not take a multivitamin in order to live longer or to prevent cardiovascular disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 0.5 to 2 yuan a day, 200 to 700 yuan a year. Taking it takes no time. It is one pill. Taken for a long time it is easy to become dependent, and a day you skip it feels as if you left something undone.
- In plain words: 1.4 ten thousand male doctors took a multivitamin for eleven years. People with a major heart or vessel event, and people who died, were as many as among those who did not take it. Put 9 trials and more than 5 ten thousand people together, and no effect on death shows either. Not buying saves two hundred to seven hundred yuan a year. People found to be short of a nutrient, and pregnant women taking folic acid, are not in this entry.
- Benefit: The PHS II trial took 14,641 male doctors and followed them for 11.2 years (this is the median: half were followed longer, half shorter). People who took it and people who did not had the same share of major heart and vessel events. The HR for a major cardiovascular event was 1.01 (HR is a risk ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same). 95% CI 0.91–1.10 is the range this figure can credibly lie in. Death was the same: all-cause death HR 0.94 (0.88–1.02). USPSTF, the US body that scores preventive measures, in 2022 counted together 9 randomised controlled trials and 51,550 people. The result was no link between a multivitamin and overall death. The grade is I, which means the evidence is not enough to say. It looks like a good deal because "one pill fills every nutrient" sounds smooth, and a day costs only a few tenths of a yuan
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sesso HD et al. (2012). Multivitamins in the prevention of cardiovascular disease in men: the Physicians' Health Study II randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.14805>；US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Vitamin, Mineral, and Multivitamin Supplementation to Prevent Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.8970>；Gaziano JM 等 (2012). Multivitamins in the Prevention of Cancer in Men. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.14641>（the cancer result in the notes）
- Notes: Disputed: cancer was also looked at in the same group of male doctors. People who took it had about 8% fewer cancers (0.86–0.998, P=0.04, just over the line of "not like a fluke"). The share who died of cancer was about 12% lower (0.77–1.01). That range crosses 1, so it may only be chance. This benefit is very small, and it has been seen only in male doctors. People found to be short of a nutrient, pregnant women (folic acid), and people whose everyday diet is genuinely very one-sided, are not included. They supplement as the doctor says

### 2. Do not take ordinary fish oil in order to prevent cardiovascular disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 1 to 3 yuan a day, 400 to 1000 yuan a year. Taking it takes no time.
- In plain words: Two large trials took 2.6 ten thousand people and 1.5 ten thousand people with diabetes. People who took 1 gram of fish oil a day had as many major heart and vessel events, and as many deaths, as people who did not. "Eating fish helps" does not equal "a fish-oil capsule helps". Not buying saves four hundred to one thousand yuan a year.
- Benefit: The VITAL trial took 25,871 people, 1 gram of fish oil a day, followed for 5.3 years (median). Major heart and vessel events and death showed no difference on either side. Major cardiovascular events HR 0.92 (HR is a risk ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same). 95% CI 0.80–1.06 is the range this figure can credibly lie in. It crosses 1, which means no difference shows. All-cause death HR 1.02 (0.90–1.15). The ASCEND trial took 15,480 people with diabetes, also 1 gram a day, average follow-up 7.4 years, and likewise showed no difference. Serious vascular events RR 0.97 (0.87–1.08). All-cause death RR 0.95 (0.86–1.05). It looks like a good deal because many people take "eating fish helps" straight across as "a fish-oil capsule helps"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Manson JE et al. (2019). Marine n-3 Fatty Acids and Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1811403>；ASCEND Study Collaborative Group (2018). Effects of n-3 Fatty Acid Supplements in Diabetes Mellitus. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1804989>；Bhatt DL 等 (2019). Cardiovascular Risk Reduction with Icosapent Ethyl for Hypertriglyceridemia. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792>（the high-purity prescription fish-oil trial in the notes）
- Notes: Disputed: one trial did work. The REDUCE-IT trial took 8,179 people who already had cardiovascular disease or diabetes, were all on a statin, and still had high triglycerides (135–499 mg/dL). They took 4 g a day of a pure-EPA prescription drug. The share with an event was 17.2% against 22.0%. The risk was about a quarter lower (HR 0.75, 0.68–0.83). But that is a high-dose prescription drug for high-risk patients. It is not ordinary people taking a fish-oil supplement. Eating fish itself is also not included here

### 3. Do not give vitamin D, in order to live longer, to a person who is not short of it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 0.2 to 0.5 yuan a day, 100 to 200 yuan a year. The money is not much. The real price is thinking you have already done the prevention.
- In plain words: People who were not short of vitamin D to start with, supplemented for 5 years, had the same number of cancers, cardiovascular diseases and deaths as people who were not supplemented. People found to be short, people with osteoporosis, and people who get no sun for a long time are not in this entry. They supplement as the doctor says.
- Benefit: The VITAL trial took 25,871 people, 2000 IU a day, followed for 5.3 years (median). Cancer, heart and vessel trouble, and death all showed no difference. The kind of cancer that spreads HR 0.96 (HR is a risk ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same; 95% CI 0.88–1.06 is the credible range). Major cardiovascular events HR 0.97 (0.85–1.12). All-cause death HR 0.99 (0.87–1.12). The D-Health trial took 21,315 Australians over 60. They took 60,000 IU a month and were followed for 5 years. This one also showed no difference. All-cause death HR 1.04 (0.93–1.18). The share who died was 5.3% against 5.1%. It looks like a good deal because some studies only track and record, with no intervention, and find that people with low vitamin D die more. That gets misread as "supplementing it works"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Manson JE et al. (2019). Vitamin D Supplements and Prevention of Cancer and Cardiovascular Disease. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1809944>；Neale RE et al. (2022). The D-Health Trial: a randomised controlled trial of the effect of vitamin D on mortality. Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-8587(21)00345-4>
- Notes: Disputed: neither trial went out of its way to recruit people who were truly short of vitamin D, so the conclusion covers only "people who were not short to start with". People found to be short, people with osteoporosis, people who get no sun for a long time, and infants and young children, still supplement as the doctor says. This looked only at overall death, cancer and heart and vessels. It did not look at the rest

### 4. Do not take antioxidant supplements (beta-carotene, vitamin E, vitamin A) in order to prevent cancer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 0.5 to 2 yuan a day. For a smoker, the real cost is that the lung-cancer risk goes up with it.
- In plain words: This one is not merely useless. Counted together, 78 trials and nearly 30 ten thousand people, people who took antioxidant supplements had a slightly higher death rate instead. In two large trials done on purpose in smokers, beta-carotene raised the share who got lung cancer by 18% to 28%, and death by 8% to 17%. Smokers, and people who have been exposed to asbestos, should especially not take it.
- Benefit: Cochrane counted together 78 randomised controlled trials and 296,707 people. People who took antioxidant supplements had an overall death RR of 1.02 (RR is a risk ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same; 95% CI 0.98–1.05 is the credible range). Looking only at the batch of trials that were designed more tightly and less likely to drift, RR 1.04 (1.01–1.07), a bit higher. Beta-carotene alone RR 1.05 (1.01–1.09). Vitamin E alone RR 1.03 (1.00–1.05). The ATBC trial took 29,133 male smokers, 20 mg a day. The share who got lung cancer was 18% higher (3%–36%), and overall death was 8% higher (1%–16%). The CARET trial took 18,314 smokers or people exposed to asbestos. Their lung-cancer risk was about 28% higher (RR 1.28, 1.04–1.57). Death risk was about 17% higher (all-cause death RR 1.17, 1.03–1.33). It looks like a good deal because the chain "antioxidant means anti-ageing, and anti-ageing prevents cancer" sounds like common sense at every step
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Bjelakovic G et al. (2012). Antioxidant supplements for prevention of mortality in healthy participants and patients with various diseases. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007176.pub2>；The Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta Carotene Cancer Prevention Study Group (1994). The effect of vitamin E and beta carotene on the incidence of lung cancer and other cancers in male smokers. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199404143301501>；Omenn GS et al. (1996). Effects of a combination of beta carotene and vitamin A on lung cancer and cardiovascular disease. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199605023341802>
- Notes: USPSTF, the US official body that scores preventive measures, in 2022 gave beta-carotene and vitamin E a grade D, which means it advises against using them. Smokers and people exposed to asbestos have already been harmed by this. Antioxidants that are already in vegetables and fruit are not included. Keep eating those

### 5. Do not count on glucosamine or chondroitin to treat knee arthritis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 2 to 5 yuan a day, 700 to 1800 yuan a year.
- In plain words: People with osteoarthritis of the knee who took a dummy pill for 24 weeks, six tenths still said it hurt less. People who took glucosamine or chondroitin were only four or five percentage points above that, small enough that it may only be chance. Seven hundred to one thousand eight hundred a year basically buys that six tenths of a feeling.
- Benefit: The GAIT trial took 1,583 people with knee osteoarthritis, for 24 weeks. Among people who took a placebo (a dummy pill), 60.1% also said the pain eased. Glucosamine was only 3.9 percentage points higher (P=0.30). Chondroitin was 5.3 percentage points higher (P=0.17). The two together were 6.5 percentage points higher (P=0.09). These gaps are all too small to rule out chance. The real drug used as a comparison in the same trial, celecoxib, was 10.0 percentage points higher (P=0.008). It looks like a good deal because "the raw material for repairing cartilage" sounds exactly on target, and a dummy pill alone had six tenths of people saying it worked
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Clegg DO et al. (2006). Glucosamine, chondroitin sulfate, and the two in combination for painful knee osteoarthritis. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa052771>
- Notes: Disputed: in the same trial, among the people who were already in severe pain, the share for whom the two together worked was 79.2%, against 54.3% in the comparison group (P=0.002). But the authors say this was dug out of the data afterwards. It can only be a lead, not a conclusion. What has evidence for knee arthritis is losing weight and training muscle, see section 2

### 6. Do not rely on vitamin C to prevent colds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: An effervescent tablet is about 1 to 2 yuan, 300 to 700 yuan a year.
- In plain words: Taking vitamin C every day basically does not make you catch fewer colds, and starting it only after you have a cold is hit and miss. Taking it every day can shorten one cold by about 8%, which is half a day less of feeling bad. Whether that is worth three to five hundred a year is for you to count. Marathon runners and soldiers marching in a cold region are the exception. Among them, the people who catch a cold can be half as many.
- Benefit: Cochrane counted together 29 controlled trials and 11,306 people. Ordinary people who take vitamin C every day basically do not catch fewer colds. The share who catch a cold RR 0.97 (RR is a risk ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same). 95% CI 0.94–1.00 is the credible range. The trials that started it only after a cold, the original says "no consistent effect was seen": some trials worked, some did not. In the same review, taking it every day for a long time can shorten the time a cold feels bad by about 8% (adults, 95% CI 3%–12%), and in children by 14% (7%–21%). Marathon runners, skiers and soldiers marching in a cold region are the exception. In 5 trials and 598 people, the people who caught a cold were about half as many. Incidence RR 0.48 (95% CI 0.35–0.64). It looks like a good deal because it is cheap, sour-sweet and easy to drink, and the saying "vitamin C prevents colds" has been passed around for half a century
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hemilä H, Chalker E (2013). Vitamin C for preventing and treating the common cold. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000980.pub4>
- Notes: Disputed: in the same review, taking it every day really can shorten the time a cold feels bad. Adults by about 8% (3%–12%), children by 14% (7%–21%). That works out at about half a day less of being ill for one cold. Marathon runners, skiers and soldiers marching in a cold region are the exception. In 5 trials and 598 people, the people who caught a cold were about half as many (RR 0.48, 0.35–0.64). The great majority of people are in neither of those two groups

### 7. Do not give yourself, when you have no symptoms, a "whole-body PET-CT" or a "tumour-marker package"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A whole-body PET-CT is about 7000 to 10000 yuan a time, and you also take one dose of ionising radiation. A tumour-marker package is a few hundred yuan. The real cost comes later: a finding of one "abnormality" that is in fact a false alarm (this is called a false positive). The follow-up checks, the tissue sample and the operation, the money and the suffering, are all in that stretch.
- In plain words: A person with no symptoms who goes to be checked finds, most often, a false alarm. More than eight tenths of people who have a whole-body CT have at least one "abnormality" found, nearly four tenths are called back for a further check, and most of it is benign. Women checked with an ovarian-cancer marker die of ovarian cancer slightly more often instead, and some people have an operation for nothing because of a false alarm.
- Benefit: USPSTF, the US body that scores preventive measures, gave a grade D to screening women with no symptoms for ovarian cancer with CA-125 and ultrasound, which means it advises against doing it. The basis is the PLCO trial. The share who died of ovarian cancer was 0.34% in the screening group and 0.29% in the usual-care group. RR 1.18 (95% CI 0.82–1.71). Checking brought no benefit. The share who had an operation for nothing because the check was wrong (a false positive) was 0.2%–3.25% across the trials, and up to 15% of those people had a serious surgical complication. Another study gave a whole-body CT screen to 1,192 people with no symptoms. Of them, 86% had at least one "abnormality" found, and 37% were advised to have a further check. The original says "most of the findings, as described, were benign". It looks like a good deal because "one check of the whole body, find it early and treat it early" fits intuition too well
- Evidence: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2018). Screening for Ovarian Cancer: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA（the same recommendation also has an official USPSTF page）. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2017.21926>、<https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/ovarian-cancer-screening>；Furtado CD et al. (2005). Whole-body CT screening: spectrum of findings and recommendations in 1192 patients. Radiology. <https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2372041741>
- Notes: The tumour-marker part is backed by a randomised controlled trial. The whole-body CT and PET-CT part has only tracking records of the kind "see what happened later to people who had it done", with no grouped comparison, so the evidence is one grade weaker, equivalent to grade B. The screens that really have evidence, such as colorectal cancer, cervical cancer, breast cancer, and low-dose chest CT for people at high risk, are a different matter, see section 1. A person who already has symptoms, or a person already diagnosed with cancer, having a PET-CT, that is diagnosis, not screening, and it is a separate case

### 8. Do not count on a band or a watch that quantifies your exercise to help you lose weight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: The device is 200 to 2000 yuan. You also have to split your attention every day to look at those figures.
- In plain words: Overweight people on the same diet and exercise plan, the people wearing an exercise band lost 3.5 kilograms in two years, and the people not wearing one lost 5.9 kilograms instead. If you want to lose weight, spending money on a device does not help. What works is the exercise itself.
- Benefit: The IDEA trial took 471 overweight or obese adults and followed them for 24 months. The diet and exercise plan was the same for everyone. The only difference was whether they wore a wearable device. The group that wore one lost 3.5 kilograms (95% CI 2.6–4.5, this is the credible range). The group that did not lost 5.9 kilograms (5.0–6.8), more instead. The two groups differed by 2.4 kilograms (1.0–3.7, P=0.002). It looks like a good deal because "you can manage only what you measure" sounds reasonable, and the device is bought once and done
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Jakicic JM et al. (2016). Effect of Wearable Technology Combined With a Lifestyle Intervention on Long-term Weight Loss: The IDEA Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.12858>
- Notes: This is only one trial, and it used a device worn on the arm from the early 2010s, so you cannot take it as saying "every wearable device is useless". Exercise itself works, see section 2. The conclusion is one sentence: if you want to lose weight, the device is not money you have to spend

### 9. Do not pay a premium for organic food in order to be "healthier"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Usually 1 to 3 times the price of the same ordinary food.
- In plain words: There is no strong evidence that organic food is more nutritious. Only 3 studies really looked at people's health after they ate it, and none of them showed a difference. Organic produce really is 30 percentage points less likely to have a pesticide residue detected, but detected does not equal over the limit. Eating more vegetables and fruit itself helps, and whether it is organic does not change that.
- Benefit: One systematic review put together 17 studies done in people and 223 studies that measured what is in the food. The conclusion is "a lack of strong evidence that organic food is significantly more nutritious than conventional food". Only 3 of these studies looked at people's health after they ate it (they looked at allergy and symptomatic infection), and the two sides showed no difference. Organic produce was 30 percentage points less likely to have a pesticide residue detected, but detected does not equal over the limit. It looks like a good deal because "no pesticide means safer, and safer means healthier" sounds like common sense at every step
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Smith-Spangler C et al. (2012). Are organic foods safer or healthier than conventional alternatives?: a systematic review. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-157-5-201209040-00007>
- Notes: Studies that look directly at people's health after they eat it are very few. So the conclusion is "there is no evidence it is better", not "it has been proved to have no benefit". Effects on the environment, animal welfare, and whether it tastes better are not covered here. Eating more vegetables and fruit itself works, and whether it is organic does not change that

### 10. Do not spend a lot of money on health products, herbal pastes and tonics in order to "regulate the body"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: From a few hundred yuan to over ten thousand yuan. Once it is packed as a gift, the price often multiplies several times.
- In plain words: Common ingredients such as a multivitamin, fish oil and vitamin D have already been checked one by one in the first six entries of this section, and none of them showed an effect. The line on the pack, "a health food is not a medicine and cannot replace medicine in treating disease", is something the department that regulates health foods requires to be printed. This money is mostly bought for parents and carried to the door. The thought is not the problem. The thing chosen is.
- Benefit: What you get back is the money saved. The reason is in the first 6 entries of this section: the most common ingredients in health products, a multivitamin, fish oil, vitamin D, antioxidants, glucosamine and vitamin C, showed none of the expected benefit in randomised controlled trials. The figures for each are in the benefit column of those 6 entries. What the rules state only says what it is allowed to claim. It cannot be taken as evidence that it does not work. The State Administration for Market Regulation requires the front of a health-food pack to mark, on no less than 20% of the area, one sentence: "a health food is not a medicine and cannot replace medicine in treating disease". The same rules also lock the use of a health food inside the range of "supplementing dietary nutrients, maintaining or improving the body's state of health, or lowering risk factors for disease". It looks like a good deal because "it does not treat disease, it only regulates" is a sentence that is never quite wrong, and nobody can prove it false. And when you spend this money as a gift, what carries it is the relationship, not the evidence
- Evidence: C
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2019). 市场监管总局就《保健食品标注警示用语指南》和《保健食品原料目录与保健功能目录管理办法》有关情况举办专题新闻发布会. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/tssps/sjdt/tpxw/art/2023/art_4b658b824b1b4b0ba57c09a56cc93aad.html>
- Notes: The conclusion is "there is no evidence for it", not "it has been proved ineffective", so it is only grade C. A product whose ingredients are written clearly, look up the matching study in the earlier entries. People found to be short of a nutrient, and people under treatment, follow the doctor. What is being ruled out is the product, not the thought. If an older person bought it themselves and is already taking it, and they have not used it to replace a medicine they are on, and it has not caused a problem, you do not have to go to the door and argue it. Arranging the vaccines they should have and the screens they should do matters more. The same money swapped for the things below, each one has a source you can turn to in the book. Go with an older person for the yearly flu vaccine, see §1.20. The shingles vaccine after 50 is in §1.21. The pneumococcal vaccine at 65 and over is in §1.22. Buy a blood-pressure monitor, watch that they take the blood-pressure drug in full as the doctor said, and get the pressure to target, see §1.7. Change the bathroom and the stairs at home, and go with them to train balance and leg strength, see §1.13. Cancer screening at the right age, go with them once, see §1.17–1.19. If an older person is already bedridden for a long time, pressure-sore protection and long-term care insurance are in §17.7 and §17.8. If you are taking something to the door, fruit, rice and flour, and food they like, are more solid than a herbal paste. What really has to be stopped is two kinds: investment-for-retirement that takes the money first, and anything that will replace a medicine, see §17.5

### 11. Do not take probiotics long term when you have no gut problem
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 2 to 5 yuan a day, 700 to 1800 yuan a year.
- In plain words: Taken together, 45 trials in healthy adults, probiotics do not change the bacteria in the gut for the long term. Stop them and it goes back, and blood lipids do not improve either. Specific situations such as diarrhoea brought on by antibiotics have separate evidence, and they are not in this entry.
- Benefit: One review put together 45 trials done in healthy adults. The conclusion is that it "failed to support that probiotics can cause a lasting change in the gut flora, and also failed to improve blood lipids in healthy adults". The bacteria in the gut do change, but only for a time, and they go back when you stop. It looks like a good deal because "gut health" is a phrase wide enough that almost any use of it is not quite wrong, and one bottle a day has a very low unit price
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Khalesi S et al. (2019). A review of probiotic supplementation in healthy adults: helpful or hype? Eur J Clin Nutr. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-018-0135-9>
- Notes: The same review also says healthy people had some small improvements, such as stool that was better formed and of a suitable softness, and more lactobacilli in women's vaginas. Diarrhoea caused by antibiotics, and one particular strain used for one particular illness, each have their own evidence, and those are separate cases. This is aimed only at a healthy person taking it every day as a health product

### 12. Do not force yourself to take cold showers in order to "boost immunity"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You have to be uncomfortable for 30 to 90 seconds a day, and keeping it up in winter takes willpower. People with cardiovascular disease also need to be careful. A cold stimulus is a risk for them.
- In plain words: In a trial of 3018 people, people who took cold showers had 29% fewer sick-leave episodes, but the total days of leave were the same as other people's, so it does not amount to being ill less. In 2025, 11 trials counted together, after a cold-water immersion the body's inflammation markers rose briefly instead, and the immune markers did not change. If you like it, shower as you like. Just do not treat it as a health investment.
- Benefit: The Netherlands ran a randomised controlled trial, 3,018 people, a cold rinse of 30, 60 or 90 seconds after a hot shower. These people had 29% fewer sick-leave episodes (IRR 0.71, this is a ratio of counts). ### 16. Do not buy blue-light glasses to "protect your eyesight", and do not believe "a few months facing a screen will ruin your eyes", but swelling, pain and redness in the eye is an emergency

### 17. Do not treat blood donation as health care: "detox, lower blood lipids, prevent a heart attack" has no support from a randomised trial, and donating often really does cause iron deficiency

- Cost: Costs nothing. One donation, the form, the blood draw, and staying to be watched and rest, takes about an hour. For a few weeks after that, the body keeps replacing the iron it lost.
- In plain words: Saying blood donation can detox, lower blood lipids or prevent a heart attack is not reliable. People who can keep going to donate were healthier to start with. Take that layer off, and the drop in death rate for one extra donation a year shrinks from nearly two tenths to 7.5%. In the trial that really split people and compared them, the number of deaths was no different. The other way round, donating too often really does leave a person weak and cold, and the reason is iron deficiency.
- Benefit: The Finnish Kuopio study followed 2862 men aged 42 to 60, for an average of nearly 9 years. In the 24 months before the count started, 153 people had donated blood, and 1 of them (0.7%) had an acute heart attack. Among the 2529 who had not donated, 316 (12.5%) had one (difference of the two proportions P<0.0001). After taking off the effect of age and other coronary risk factors, people who had donated had a heart-attack risk about 88% lower. Relative risk 0.12 (95% CI 0.02–0.86, P=0.035). The Nordic SCANDAT database has 1,182,495 blood donors, a cumulative 9,526,627 person-years of follow-up, of whom 15,401 died. When only features such as age and sex are taken off, one extra donation a year means a death rate 18.6% lower (95% CI 16.8%–20.4%). Take off as well the layer "only people who stay healthy are allowed to keep donating", and the drop is only 7.5% left (5.7%–9.4%). The authors' conclusion is that it "cannot be interpreted as solid evidence of a health benefit". The FeAST randomised controlled trial found 1277 patients with symptomatic peripheral artery disease of the legs, split at random into two groups. One had blood let every 6 months to reduce iron. One was the comparison. What they mainly looked at was all-cause death, 125/636 (20%) against 148/641 (23%). HR 0.85 (HR is a risk ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same). 95% CI 0.67–1.08, P=0.17. Death plus non-fatal heart attack and stroke together was 180 (28%) against 205 (32%), HR 0.88 (0.72–1.07, P=0.20). Neither showed a difference. For women, 16 weeks, 14 weeks and 12 weeks. After the interval was shortened, clearly more blood was collected within two years (the men's 8-week group collected 1.69 units more than the 12-week group, 95% CI 1.59–1.80). The two thyroid hormones thyroxine and triiodothyronine were also lower (P<0.002). This claim looks as if it holds because people who donate blood are healthy people who have been screened over and over, and the hypothesis "letting the iron out protects the heart" did not check out in a randomised trial

### 19. Do not start a uric-acid-lowering drug because a check-up found high uric acid but you have never had pain
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and it also saves the long-term drug cost and the money for regular rechecks, and it also spares you one unnecessary drug risk.
- In plain words: For a person whose uric acid is over the line, but who has never had a gout attack and has never grown a tophus, the American College of Rheumatology advises not to start a uric-acid-lowering drug first. You have to treat 24 people for a full 3 years to prevent one gout attack. Counting on it to protect the kidneys also falls through. The kidney function of people who took the drug kept falling, as fast as people who took a dummy pill. Treat it after a real attack.
- Benefit: The 2020 gout guideline of the American College of Rheumatology talks about asymptomatic hyperuricaemia. It means blood uric acid >6.8 mg/dL, and no gout attack before, and no tophus under the skin. The guideline conditionally advises these people not to start any uric-acid-lowering drug. The drugs include allopurinol, febuxostat and probenecid. The certainty of the evidence is rated "high". The footnote under the table states "calculated by attributable risk, 24 patients must be treated for 3 years to prevent one gout attack". The CKD-FIX randomised controlled trial found 363 people. They had stage 3 or stage 4 chronic kidney disease, no history of gout, but a risk of worsening. One group took 100–300 mg of allopurinol a day. One group took a placebo. The trial ran for 104 weeks. eGFR is a measure of kidney function, in mL/min/1.73 m². One group changed by −3.33 a year (95% CI −4.11 to −2.55). The other group changed by −3.23 a year (−3.98 to −2.47). The two groups differed by −0.10 (−1.18 to 0.97), P=0.85, and kidney function fell at the same speed. Serious adverse events were 46% against 44%. The PERL randomised controlled trial found 530 people with type 1 diabetes and early or mid-stage diabetic kidney disease. They took allopurinol for 3 years, then stopped for 2 months to wash the drug effect out. Allopurinol really did lower blood uric acid from 6.1 to 3.9 mg/dL. But after stopping, the kidney-function measure GFR of the two groups differed by only 0.001 mL/min/1.73 m². The credible range of this difference is 95% CI −1.9 to 1.9, P=0.99. Albumin leaking in the urine was instead 40% higher in the allopurinol group (0 to 80). It looks like a good deal because the intuition "an out-of-range figure should be pressed down with a drug" is too smooth
- Evidence: A
- Sources: FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. - Notes: What is being ruled out is "starting a uric-acid-lowering drug for life when you have never had an attack", not that high uric acid does not matter. The same guideline also lists exceptions. The situations below can be considered for starting the drug. One is a first attack together with stage 3 or above chronic kidney disease. Two is blood uric acid >9 mg/dL (about 535 µmol/L). ### 20. Do not have the gallbladder taken out preventively because a check-up found gallstones but you have never had pain
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and it also saves one operation under general anaesthesia and one hospital stay. What you take on is remembering which few signals, the moment they appear, mean you have to go to hospital at once.
- In plain words: Of people who have stones in the gallbladder but have never had pain, about eight tenths stayed without symptoms over nearly nine years. Leave it alone, and the share who get an emergency such as cholecystitis or pancreatitis each year is about one in a thousand to three in a thousand. Taking the gallbladder out is not zero risk either. About 1.5% of people have the bile duct injured. So both the European and the British guidelines say: no symptoms, do not take it out first.
- Benefit: The Italian MICOL population survey did ultrasound on 11,229 people aged 29 to 69. It found gallstones in 856 people, of whom 580 had no symptoms, followed for an average of 8.7 years. Of these 580, 453 (78.1%) stayed without symptoms, 61 (10.5%) developed mild symptoms, and 66 (11.4%) developed severe symptoms. During follow-up 189 gallbladder removals were done in all, of which 41.3% were done in people with no symptoms. The authors' conclusion is that "expectant treatment remains an effective way of handling it for most patients". Expectant treatment means watch first, and do not operate. One US HMO did a follow-up count. An HMO is a managed medical organisation. 691 people with a diagnosis of gallstones were followed for an average of 78±61.6 months. Of them, 135 people with no symptoms were followed for 58±50.2 months (median 46.3 months). Only 10% developed symptoms of gallstones, and 7% had an operation. Of 50 deaths in the whole group, only 2 were related to the biliary tract, and both were in the group that had symptoms. The authors' conclusion is that "patients with silent stones do not need surgery before symptoms appear". A silent stone is a stone that makes no sound and does not flare. Article 1.2.1 of the British NICE guideline CG188 is written this way. "People with a normal gallbladder and biliary tract in whom asymptomatic gallstones are found by chance should be told they do not need treatment before symptoms appear". The 2016 gallstone guideline of the European Association for the Study of the Liver writes the risks of both sides together. About 0.7% to 2.5% of people with no symptoms develop symptoms each year. Acute cholecystitis, acute pancreatitis, obstructive jaundice (the biliary tract is blocked and the person turns yellow) or cholangitis occurs in 0.1% to 0.3% each year. Only after the first colic do complications rise to 1% to 3% a year, so most people have one bout of pain before a big event. On the surgery side, among 51041 gallbladder removals in the Swedish national register, 1.5% injured the bile duct. Of those, about one fifth (0.3%) was a partial or complete cut of the duct. People whose duct was injured had a death rate of 3.9% within one year. People whose duct was not injured had 1.1%. The guideline also writes that after removal, 10% to 40% of patients still have discomfort, or get better for a while and then it comes back. The guideline's conclusion is: for a person with no symptoms, surgery, whether open or laparoscopic, does not lengthen life, because the death and complication risk of the surgery itself exceeds the risk of not removing it. Handling it after symptoms appear also costs less in total. A calculation in 1983 gave specific numbers: preventive removal in a 30-year-old man means living 4 days less on average than watching first, and a 50-year-old lives 18 days less. To this day there has not been one randomised trial comparing removal and not removing. Preventive removal looks like a good deal because the intuition "it will hurt sooner or later, so take it out once while you are young" is too smooth

### 21. Do not give up calcium in order to prevent kidney stones
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The other way round, it is less trouble. You do not have to watch every day which things contain calcium.
- In plain words: People who have had a calcium-oxalate stone are often advised to eat less calcium. The trial result is the other way round. Men who kept getting stones, who ate a normal amount of calcium and at the same time ate less animal protein and salt, had only half as many recurrences in five years as people on a low-calcium diet. The British guideline also states not to restrict calcium.
- Benefit: One randomised controlled trial ran for 5 years. It found 120 men who kept growing calcium-oxalate stones and whose urine calcium was also high (this is called idiopathic hypercalciuria). They were split at random into two groups. One group ate a normal amount of calcium, 30 mmol a day. This group at the same time ate less animal protein (52 g a day) and less salt (50 mmol of sodium chloride a day). The other group ate the traditional low-calcium diet, 10 mmol a day. Recurrence over 5 years was 12/60 against 23/60. The unadjusted relative risk was 0.49 (95% CI 0.24–0.98, this is the credible range), P=0.04. The recurrence risk in the group that ate a normal amount of calcium was only half. During follow-up, urine calcium in both groups fell by about 170 mg a day. But the oxalate passed in the urine was different. The low-calcium group rose by 5.4 mg a day on average. The normal-calcium group fell by 7.2 mg a day on average. Article 1.8.1 of the British NICE guideline NG118 states not to restrict the calcium eaten each day. Adults keep 700 to 1,200 mg a day. Children and adolescents, by age, 350 to 1,000 mg a day. Giving up calcium looks like a good deal because the intuition "the stone is made of calcium, so eat less calcium" is too smooth
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Borghi L, Schianchi T, Meschi T, et al. (2002). Comparison of two diets for the prevention of recurrent stones in idiopathic hypercalciuria. New England Journal of Medicine, 346(2), 77-84. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa010369>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，article 1.8.1. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Notes: Disputed: this trial was done only in men, and these people all kept growing calcium-oxalate stones and had high urine calcium, so it cannot be applied straight to every type of stone. Also the trial group changed three things at once, eating a normal amount of calcium, eating less animal protein and eating less salt, so the whole benefit cannot all be credited to the one thing "not giving up calcium". Low salt and less animal protein are working too. What is being ruled out is "actively giving up things that contain calcium", not "you should supplement extra calcium". What is changed here is the calcium in the three meals of the day. A calcium supplement is a different matter. Whether a person who has had a stone should take a supplement, ask the doctor in charge. The entry on drinking more water is in §16.8 (after a kidney stone, drink water up to 2.5–3 litres a day)

### 22. Do not buy crystals, bracelets, pixiu and objects of that kind in order to "turn your luck", "draw wealth" or "nourish you"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. All you have to do is not count "turning your luck" or "nourishing you" among the reasons for buying it. The hard part is not following when everyone else is wearing one and everyone else says it works.
- In plain words: Saying that wearing it can change your luck, draw wealth or nourish you has no evidence that can be checked. It is the same reason fortune-telling looks accurate. The words are vague enough that you fit them onto yourself. When a seller's words reach the effect of treating disease, that is false advertising. In 2026 a livestream room was investigated for this. Buying it as an ornament is fine.
- Benefit: A classic classroom experiment showed that everyone received the same vague "personal analysis", yet people generally thought it was very accurate. This phenomenon is called the Barnum effect. It explains where the experience "after I put it on, things really did go smoothly" comes from. That author's conclusion is "being accepted by the person does not prove the interpretation is correct". An official case: on 4 June 2026 the State Administration for Market Regulation published a batch of typical cases of "cracking down on fakes and clearing the source" in the traditional-craft market. Among them, a jewellery shop in Sihui, Guangdong, invented the material of the goods in a livestream room. It also falsely claimed the goods had effects such as "preventing and treating rheumatism, raising immunity, improving sleep quality, and clearing the blood vessels". This shop misled and deceived consumers by scripting and acting. This violated article 9, paragraph 1, of the Anti-Unfair Competition Law (nationwide)

- Sources: Forer BR (1949). The fallacy of personal validation: A classroom demonstration of gullibility. - Notes: There is only one conclusion: its effect cannot be checked, and no predictive ability can be measured either. This does not judge any belief or custom, and it is not aimed at uses such as a gift, a keepsake or something passed down in the family. Separate two things. An effect a seller claims has a written rule to rely on, and you can go and complain. A saying such as "turning your luck" has no study, and it also cannot be proved false, so you can only hold it by drawing your own budget. Before you buy, treat it first as an ordinary ornament, and ask yourself one sentence: "if it changes nothing, do I still buy it at this price?" ### 23. Do not count on buying things to improve your mood or your sense of status
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The hard part is holding back from placing an order when your mood is bad.
- In plain words: The more a person values money and material things, the less happy they feel themselves, but the link is very weak, and it does not separate which causes which. Another study found that spending money on an experience makes a person happier than buying a thing. So when your mood is bad, spending the money on one outing, or one meal eaten with people, is more likely to help than filling a shopping cart.
- Benefit: One meta-analysis (many studies counted together) had 259 independent samples and 753 effect sizes. The result is: the more a person values material things, the lower the happiness they report themselves. On the multidimensional scale used most often, the correlation coefficient r = −0.19 (after correction ρ = −0.24). On a scale that measures only the one aspect "valuing money", the link is weaker, r = −0.08 to −0.11. Split by which result is being looked at, the strength differs. The link is strongest with risky health and spending behaviour, and with a negative view of yourself, r = −0.28 to −0.44. The link is weakest with life satisfaction and negative emotion, r = −0.13 to −0.15. The authors also did a mediation analysis (checking what in the middle carries it across). The result suggests this negative link may be because a psychological need was not met. Another study: in two surveys of different groups of people, respondents all said money spent to obtain a life experience made them happier than buying a thing. In the laboratory experiment that followed, the good mood after recalling one time of spending money on an experience was also higher than recalling one purchase (a nationwide figure does not apply; both of these used foreign samples)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Dittmar H, Bond R, Hurst M, Kasser T (2014). The relationship between materialism and personal well-being: A meta-analysis. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 107(5), 879-924. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037409>；Van Boven L, Gilovich T (2003). To do or to have? That is the question. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85(6), 1193-1202. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.6.1193>
- Notes: Disputed: most of the studies included asked a group of people at the same point in time, looking at whether two things move together, and they cannot separate which comes first. The authors themselves also say in the conclusion that seeing the direction clearly still needs more experiments and long-term follow-up studies. So it cannot be read as "buying things makes a person unhappy". It can only be read as "counting on buying things to become happy is not much to count on". And the link is very weak: r = −0.19 explains only about a few percentage points of the difference, and people differ a lot from each other. Both studies used foreign samples. There are no data of the same kind for a Chinese population that can be cited. This does not oppose spending money on things you like. What it opposes is treating shopping as a way to regulate mood, because that makes your first reaction the next time your mood is bad still to place an order. The specific actions for saving money are in section 5. What really has evidence when your mood is bad is in the second half of section 22 and in section 29. The extra budget for "one notch above other people" is in §6.24 (moving up a notch).

### 24. Do not spend extra to change house, change car or change circle in order to "move up a notch among the people around you"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. All you have to do, before you add to the budget, is ask one more sentence: does this extra price buy the thing itself, or my place in this group of people. The hard part is not following when the people around you have all changed.
- In plain words: Compare people with the same income, and the more the neighbours earn, the lower the happiness this person reports. This happens only between neighbours who have the same education as you and whom you see often. So the extra money spent in order to move into a place where everyone is better off than you buys moving yourself down a notch. But when incomes rise together, a person still feels better off. The absolute level still counts.
- Benefit: Two rounds of the US National Survey of Families and Households (NSFH) revisited the same people. The author matched these data to local average income. Local average income (PUMA) was estimated from the 5% sample of the 1990 census. After taking off the person's own income and a series of personal features, the result is: the higher the neighbours' income, the lower the happiness the person reports. This effect is large, it stays steady under another way of calculating, and it is highly significant. The author's wording is that the drop in happiness from "neighbours' income rising" and from "one's own income falling by the same amount" are of the same order of magnitude. After changing it to the same person compared with themselves before and after (this is called adding individual fixed effects), the direction of the coefficient does not change and the size is also close, only the significance drops to the 10% level. Restricting the comparison further to the same state and the same survey round (this is called fixed effects for California and the survey round), the result is basically unchanged. This shows it is not caused by "people who like to move to a poor place were happier to start with", and it is not caused by some unmeasured factor at the state level. The effect is stronger in people who often see their neighbours, and it is not significant in people who only see friends outside the community. Renters and people who own their home do not differ. This point does not support the explanation "an expensive place has high house prices, so the money you can actually spend is less". Happiness falls only with the income of neighbours who have the same education, and it barely reacts to the income of neighbours with a different education. And it is carried across mainly through "satisfaction with one's own material situation", not through satisfaction with other sides such as family life. Last, if one's own income and the neighbours' income rise together by the same proportion, happiness still rises
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Luttmer EFP (2005). Neighbors as Negatives: Relative Earnings and Well-Being. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 120(3), 963–1002. <https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/120.3.963>；Luttmer EFP (2004). Neighbors as Negatives: Relative Earnings and Well-Being. NBER Working Paper No. 10667（the wording and the order of magnitude cited in this entry were checked word for word against this version）. <https://www.nber.org/papers/w10667>
- Notes: The reasons for grade B are several. The data come from a questionnaire that revisited the same people, there is only this one study, and the sample is abroad. The result looked at is happiness the respondent reported themselves, not money. The author did use statistical means to take off several kinds of interference, such as the same person compared before and after, and the same state compared within the same round, but it is still not a randomised trial. There are also no data of the same kind for a Chinese population that can be cited. The benefit size is set at "medium", following the lens of §6.23 (do not count on buying things to improve your mood). The money saved really might reach the level of ten thousand yuan. But "how much happiness this saved money can buy" has no number behind it, so it is not recorded as "large" on the ten-thousand-yuan level. ### 26. Do not count on eating breakfast, or on 16:8 light fasting, to help you control your weight. Pick a meal time you can keep up for a long time

- In plain words: The popular saying is that skipping breakfast makes a person overeat at lunch and dinner, so the day's total is more instead. In the trials it was not like that: people who skipped breakfast ate more than two hundred kilocalories less in a day, and their weight was a little lighter too. Compared with simply eating less, 16:8 made no difference in weight over a year either. People who skip breakfast for a long time really do have about 17% more cardiovascular disease, while overall death shows no difference.
- Benefit: First look at the saying "skipping breakfast makes a person eat more later". One meta-analysis counted 13 randomised trials together.
