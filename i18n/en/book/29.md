# 29. After a major blow

A relative dies, you are told of a serious illness, you lose your job, you divorce. These things have one thing in common in the research: what happens is not only to the mood, but also to the body. The stretch with the highest risk is concentrated in the first few weeks to the first year. This section does not talk about how to "come through it". It only talks about which things in this stretch must have someone watching them, which decisions must be put off, and when you should go and register. Most entries count the chance of death. The four entries that talk about spending money and benefits count money: do not start by paying for grief counselling (§29.9), call 12356 and register at a psychology clinic (§29.11), put irreversible big decisions off (§29.12), and do not use death as a way of paying off debt (§29.13). The two kinds of figures are not converted into each other. If there is no relative and no friend to turn to, how the sentence "find someone to watch" lands, see §29.6.

The procedures, the body and the money to collect at the time a relative dies, see section 25. Guardianship and a will while an older person is alive, see section 17. What you can claim after losing a job, see section 7. Exercise and light when the mood is low, see section 3 and section 22. When there are suicidal thoughts, call 12356 first, see §1.25 (call 12356). The time scale of a thought, see §1.32 (the moment a suicidal thought comes up). The after-effects after being brought back, see §1.33 (do not treat being brought back as a safety net). This section does not repeat those.

### 1. In the first few days after a relative has just gone, do not stay alone. If you have heart disease, high blood pressure or diabetes, keep taking the medicine. If the chest hurts as if something is pressing on it, call 120
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Do not stay alone these few days. Take the medicine as usual.
- In plain words: On the first day after a relative dies, the chance of a heart attack is 21.1 times the usual. After that it is lower day by day. For people aged 60 to 89, within 30 days after a partner dies, the share of heart attack or stroke about doubles. After a month it falls back. A person who already has cardiovascular disease should not stop the medicine these few days. Chest pain as if something is pressing, being unable to catch your breath, or one side of the hands or feet having no strength: call 120 at once.
- Benefit: 1985 patients with acute heart attack were compared with themselves, across different stretches of time before and after the onset. Of them, 270 (13.6%) had lost an important person in the 6 months before onset. 19 had lost that person within 1 day before the heart attack. Within 24 hours after an important person died, the rate of acute heart attack rose 21.1 times (95% CI 13.1–34.1). The true multiple falls roughly between 13 and 34. After that it fell day by day. Turned into actual people: among people whose ten-year heart-attack risk is 5%, every 1394 who have just lost a relative produce 1 extra case. Among people whose risk is 20%, every 320 produce 1 extra case. Another study in Britain compared 30447 widowed people aged 60 to 89 with 83588 matched controls. Within 30 days after bereavement, heart attack or stroke happened to 50 people on the widowed side (0.16%). On the control side, 67 people (0.08%). IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15), that is, the chance of an event is about 2.2 times the controls. Of that, heart attack was 2.14 (1.20–3.81), about 2.1 times. Stroke was 2.40 (1.22–4.71), about 2.4 times. After 30 days it weakened. Within 90 days two further kinds of illness were also looked at. One is acute coronary syndrome that is not a heart attack (the heart's blood supply has an acute problem, but it does not yet amount to a heart attack), IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29). One is pulmonary embolism (a clot blocking a vessel in the lung), IRR 2.37 (1.18–4.75).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>；Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>
- Notes: Hand the medicine box to someone else to manage. Do not rely on remembering it yourself. Do not drive a long distance these few days. Do not get through it by drinking. The account of alcohol, see section 2. How to recognise a heart attack or a stroke, and how to call a car, see section 13. Both studies went back through population data. They cannot separate how many people were going to fall ill anyway. But the direction and the stretch of time the two point to are the same. A person who has no one to hand the medicine box to, and who is bound to be alone in the room these few days, see §29.6.

### 2. In the week you are told of a serious-illness diagnosis, do not go alone to collect the report. Put decisions other than treatment off
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Find someone to go with you to collect the report. Push big decisions other than treatment back a few days.
- In plain words: In the first week of receiving a cancer diagnosis, the chance of suicide is 12.6 times that of people without the diagnosis. The chance of dying of cardiovascular disease is 5.6 times. By the first year, the suicide risk falls to 3.1 times. The harder the cancer is to treat, the clearer this is. So on the day you collect the report, take someone with you. Do not stay alone after you get home. Things such as resigning or selling the house, leave them until the next clinic visit.
- Benefit: Swedish national records followed more than 607 ten thousand people (6,073,240 people), from 1991 to 2006. Compared with people not diagnosed with cancer, the relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8), that is, about 12.6 times. The true multiple falls roughly between 8.6 and 17.8. The incidence that week was 2.50 per 1000 person-years, that is, follow 1000 people for one year and 2.5 cases appear. By the first year it fell to 3.1 (2.7–3.5, 0.60 per 1000 person-years), about 3.1 times. Cardiovascular death in the first week was 5.6 (5.2–5.9, 116.80 per 1000 person-years), about 5.6 times. In the first 4 weeks it was 3.3 (3.1–3.4, 65.81 per 1000 person-years). The rise in risk fell back quickly within the first year after diagnosis. The harder a cancer is to treat, and the lower the chance of surviving it, the clearer the rise. Comparing the same patients with themselves across different stretches of time, the conclusion was the same.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Notes: These are data on cancer patients. Switched to another major diagnosis, there is no original text of the same weight. The people it happens to are in fact few: the suicide rate in the first week is 2.50 per 1000 person-years, that is, follow 1000 people for one year and 2.5 cases appear. Writing it down is not to frighten. It is to say that in "the first few days after you have just found out", someone should be beside you. Long-term management, reimbursement and rechecks after the diagnosis, see section 16 and section 24. If you cannot find someone to go with you, see §29.6 (turn "that person" into a neighbour and the community list).

### 3. After losing a job, first fix the rhythm of daily routine, medical insurance and looking for work. Do not stay at home all day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. First go and register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance, then set looking for work into a fixed schedule. The hard part is still getting up on time when no one is pushing you.
- In plain words: Afterwards, an unemployed person's chance of death is about six tenths higher than a person in work. It is clearer for men and for people who are not old. The first ten years are the highest. After the effect of smoking and drinking is taken out, the rise is about one quarter less. That part you can manage yourself. First go and register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance. While you are claiming, medical insurance is paid on your behalf by the unemployment insurance.
- Benefit: 42 studies, 235 estimates of death risk, and more than 2000 ten thousand people were pooled. After the effect of conditions such as age was levelled, the unemployed people's death risk from all causes added together had an average HR of 1.63, about six tenths higher than people in work. Men were higher than women. The effect was clear for people in the early and middle part of a working life, and smaller in the late part. The risk was highest in the first 10 years of follow-up, and fell after that. In the part of the studies that also counted health behaviours such as smoking and drinking, the average HR was 24% lower. That is, the rise that was worked out was about one quarter less.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Notes: This is a study that went back through population data. It cannot separate the direction of cause. People whose bodies were already poor are also more likely to lose a job. But "after the part of the effect that comes from habits of living is taken out, the effect is 24% lower" gives a direction you can start on. What you can claim after losing a job, where to claim it, and how to continue social insurance, see section 7. How much compensation you should get if you are dismissed, and do not sign a voluntary resignation, see section 19.

### 4. If a relative died by suicide, accident or homicide, do not count on toughing it out. Go and look for professional help on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Calling 12356 costs no money. If you need to see a doctor, registering at a psychology clinic is the price of an ordinary clinic visit. The hard part is opening your mouth and looking for someone.
- In plain words: A person whose spouse died by suicide has, within five years, a chance of being found to have a mental disorder about eight tenths higher than other people (men) and about seven tenths higher (women). Compared with people whose spouse died of another cause, it is also seven tenths to double. People bereaved in this way more easily feel kept at a distance, and feel shame. That is exactly the reason to go and look for someone on your own. Do not wait until you get better by yourself.
- Benefit: Danish national records followed 670 ten thousand adults, from 1980 to 2014, a total of more than 1.36 hundred million person-years (the years everyone was followed, added together). For people widowed because a spouse died by suicide, the risk of a newly found mental disorder within 5 years, compared with other people, was IRR 1.8 for men (95% CI 1.6–2.0), that is, about eight tenths higher. For women it was 1.7 (1.6–1.8), that is, about seven tenths higher. Compared with people widowed by other causes of death, the IRR was 1.7 for men (1.5–1.9) and 2.0 for women (1.9–2.2), that is, seven tenths higher to double. Among this group, suicidal behaviour, mortality, and the share needing municipal assistance were also higher. Illnesses of the body, such as cirrhosis and sleep disorders, also had a higher risk. Another 57 studies were summarised one by one, and not pooled into one figure. Bereavement by suicide is related to the four things below. One is a higher risk of suicide in the partner. Two is a higher risk that parents are admitted to psychiatry after a child dies by suicide. Three is a higher risk of suicide in the mother after an adult child dies by suicide. Four is a higher risk of depression in the children after a parent dies by suicide. Compared with people bereaved by another violent death, people bereaved by suicide more often said they were kept at a distance and felt shame.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>；Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>；Kristensen P 等 (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76>（the review mentioned in the notes）
- Notes: A sudden, violent death means an accident, a homicide, or a disaster. After this kind of death, the shares of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and grief that gets stuck are all higher than after a death from illness. Recovery is also slower. What to do when you yourself have suicidal thoughts, see §1.25 (call 12356).

### 5. In the first half-year after being widowed, hand meals, sleep, the chronic-disease medicine and the rechecks to one fixed person to watch
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Find one specific person. A child, a brother or sister, or a friend will do. Have them come to the door or telephone regularly. The hard part is opening your mouth and entrusting this.
- In plain words: Less than half a year after being widowed, the chance of dying in the same period is about four tenths higher than for a person who was not widowed. After half a year it falls to about one and a half tenths higher. For men it is about two tenths and three higher. For women that band is so small it may only be a coincidence. This gap is not something you can get through by trying to look on the bright side. What to do is to land the medicine, the meals and the rechecks on one specific person. Do not count on remembering it yourself.
- Benefit: 15 studies that registered people first and then followed them, and 2,263,888 people, were pooled. When widowhood was under 6 months, the death RR was 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57), that is, the chance of dying in the same period was about four tenths higher than for a person who was not widowed. After 6 months it was 1.14 (1.10–1.18), down to about one and a half tenths higher. For men the RR was 1.23 (1.18–1.28), about two tenths and three higher. For women it was 1.04 (1.00–1.08, not statistically significant, that is, this small gap may only be a coincidence). Under 65 and 65 or over, no clear difference was seen. Another analysis pooled 123 papers, 1377 risk estimates, and more than 5 hundred million people. After differences in conditions were levelled, the HR was 1.23 (1.19–1.28), that is, overall about two tenths and three higher. Men were still higher than women. Men 1.27 (1.19–1.35), two tenths and seven higher. Women 1.15 (1.08–1.22), one tenth and five higher.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>；Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Notes: This is a study that went back through population data. Other factors mixed in cannot be removed. A couple's way of living and their illnesses were already similar. But the pattern "the first half-year is much higher than afterwards" is the same in two pooled analyses done independently of each other. It is worth treating as a window of looking-after. Men's risk is higher, and they are also less likely to open their mouths and ask for help. If there is a widowed older person at home, under section 17 sort out the accounts, the guardianship and the will together. If you cannot find such a person (living alone, having lost an only child, or the children also gone), hand this to the community and the phone. See §29.6.

### 6. If you have no relative and no friend, turn "the person watching you" into three things: a neighbour who can enter your door, the community's visiting list, and an emergency contact in the phone
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The hard part is opening your mouth on your own, and saying one sentence to a neighbour and the residents' committee: "I live alone, and something has just happened at home."
- In plain words: When there is no relative and no friend, two things have to be looked after. If something happens, someone finds you. Day to day, you take medicine and eat on time. Taking medicine and eating rely on you. Write them as a list, and set an alarm. Do not rely on memory. Someone finding you relies on three things. Leave a spare key with a neighbour. Go to the residents' committee and ask whether you can be put on the visiting-and-care list. Set an emergency contact in the phone, and do not switch it off.
- Benefit: 44,573 outpatients all had a risk or a history of atherothrombosis (plaque growing in the vessels, the kind of trouble that is easily blocked by a clot). Doctors in many countries registered them into the international project REACH and followed them. They joined from December 2003 (month 12) to December 2004 (month 12), and were followed for 4 years. Of them, 8,594 (19%) lived alone. Among people living alone, death from all causes added together over 4 years was 14.1%. Among people living with others it was 11.1%. Cardiovascular death was 8.6% against 6.8%. For both, log-rank P&lt;0.01. The difference is not very likely to be a coincidence. The gap differed by age band (interaction P=0.03). From 45 to 65, death for living alone against living with others was 7.7% against 5.7%. After other conditions were levelled, HR 1.24 (95% CI 1.01–1.51), that is, about two tenths and four higher. From 66 to 80 it was 13.2% against 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01–1.26), about one tenth higher. Over 80 it was 24.6% against 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79–1.06), and no rise was seen. A 2022 opinion of the Ministry of Civil Affairs and other departments, ten departments in all, provides that visiting-and-care services are to be carried out. The objects are older people who live alone, whose nest is empty, who are left behind, who have lost ability, who are severely disabled, and who are in a special family-planning household. The way of serving is "regular visits into the home, telephone and video, and remote monitoring". The township or subdistrict checks the ground, and the village or residents' committee assists, to grasp the basic information and "the wish to receive visiting-and-care services", and to bring them into grassroots grid management, that is, to assign them by area to a named person to watch. By the end of 2025, "ensure that the monthly visiting rate for older people in special difficulty reaches 100%". "For older people in special difficulty who have lost ability, visiting and care not less than once a month." The document also encourages the household to fit an intelligent call system, an intelligent electricity and water meter, a health-monitoring product, and an old-age monitoring device, so that "once monitoring is abnormal, it can warn in time, and at the same time send a prompt to the emergency contact". If a service person finds an urgent problem, they "shall at the first moment help to dial the emergency-help number".
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>；民政部等十部门 (2022). 关于开展特殊困难老年人探访关爱服务的指导意见（民发〔2022〕73 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Notes: How to do the three things, in concrete terms. One: give a spare key, or a temporary code for the door lock, to one neighbour or to the property management, and say one sentence, "I am alone these few days." No friendship is needed. Only that someone knows you are in the room. Two: go to the community residents' committee, say that you live alone, and ask whether you can be put on the visiting-and-care list. Among older people, living alone, an empty nest, and having lost an only child are objects the authorities name. While you are there, ask whether the locality has an intelligent caller or an intelligent water meter of the kind that is fitted free. Three: set an emergency contact and a medical emergency card in the phone. These few days, do not mute it and do not switch it off. There are two reasons for giving B. One is that the link between living alone and mortality comes from a study that went back through population data. It cannot remove the reverse reading that "people whose bodies and conditions were already poor are more likely to live alone". The original text itself also writes that this result awaits confirmation. Two is that the visiting-and-care document covers only older people. People under 60 have no corresponding special system. What can be counted on is one line in the document. It requires grid workers and social workers to "find in time the risk of a psychological crisis such as a family blow, losing a job, or dropping out of school" (see §29.11, if you want someone to talk with, call 12356). Why "someone in the room" is worth money, see §13.1 (press hard on the chest). Of cardiac arrests that happen outside hospital, 79.2% happen at home. When someone beside them does the pressing, 16.1% leave hospital alive. When no one does, only 3.9%. No one in the room means falling into the low band. Two further things that can be done in passing. Handling the funeral itself means running to the funeral home, the community health service, the police, and the housing-fund window (see section 25). Set it as a fixed schedule of going out once a day, and these few days' going-out is already arranged. The hotline can be called again and again. It is not only once (see §29.11, if you want someone to talk with, call 12356). "Living alone" is itself also a long-term risk factor. The death odds ratio for living alone is 1.32, about three tenths higher. See §22.9 (treat seeing people regularly as a health expense). But here only these next few days are managed. The long-term account is counted separately.

### 7. After a parent dies, tell the child truthfully, let the child take part in the farewell, and do not send the child somewhere else "for a change of setting"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The hard part is that you yourself are in pain, and you still have to say it clearly to the child.
- In plain words: A child who loses a father or a mother before 18 has, by early adulthood, a chance of death about five tenths higher than a child who has not been through it. If the parent died by accident or suicide, it is about eight tenths higher. If the parent died of illness, it is about three tenths higher. What the family can do is tell the child truthfully, let the child take part in the farewell, not break off school, and not send the child to a relative's house for a change of setting.
- Benefit: The whole population born in Denmark from 1968 to 2008, and in Sweden from 1973 to 2006, was counted in. Plus 89.3% drawn from people born in Finland from 1987 to 2007. In all, more than 730 ten thousand people. Of them, 189,094 lost a parent before the age of 18. This group's mortality from all causes added together was 50% higher than people who had not been bereaved (mortality ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58, that is, about 1.5 times). Where the parent died of an unnatural cause such as an accident or suicide, it was 1.84 (1.71–2.00), about eight tenths higher. Where the parent died of a natural cause such as illness, it was 1.33 (1.24–1.41), about three tenths higher. It was highest when the child's cause of death and the parent's cause of death were of the same kind.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Notes: When you talk to the child, do not invent "they have gone far away". The arrangement of guardianship and of raising the child has to land in writing. B is not given because the figures are not hard. This is a follow-up of the whole population of three countries. The figures themselves are enough for grade A. B is given because, between this figure and the concrete practice of "how to tell the child, and whether to take part in the farewell", there is no direct trial evidence. The authors also write that data on the parent-child relationship after bereavement, and on the child's way of living, are both missing. Inside the rise there is both a component of inborn constitution and a long-term effect left by the bereavement. Naming a guardian and arranging property, see section 17. The child's maintenance and schooling, see section 18.

### 8. If the grief is still in the same place after half a year, and daily life cannot go on, register at a psychiatry department or a clinical psychology department
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The registration fee plus the treatment fee. This kind of treatment is done by a course. In the trial it was 16 sessions over an average of 19 weeks. The hard part is stepping out yourself to register for this.
- In plain words: About one in every ten bereaved adults gets stuck. For more than half a year they still keep missing the person to the point of being unable to eat, unable to go to work, and unable to look after a child. The person is numb, and feels life has no meaning. This is not not being strong enough. It has diagnostic criteria, and it has a treatment of its own. The response rate of the targeted treatment was 51%. Ordinary psychotherapy was 28%. It also started to work faster.
- Benefit: 14 studies were pooled. The objects were adults whose relative did not die by violence, and who themselves were not being seen in psychiatry. The prevalence of prolonged grief disorder was 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0), that is, about 10 of every 100 bereaved adults. The older the age, the higher the share. Another study tested whether this set of diagnostic criteria was accurate. It found 291 bereaved people, and interviewed them three times, at 0–6, 6–12 and 12–24 months after the bereavement. The core of the diagnosis is an intense missing of the person who died. At least five of these nine items must also be added. The first five are emotional numbness, shock, feeling that life has no meaning, not trusting other people, and suffering because of this loss. The last four are being unable to accept it, not knowing who one is supposed to be, avoiding the reality, and being unable to go on with life. These features have to appear every day, or be serious enough that things cannot be done. And they still exist at least 6 months after the relative died, and they affect daily life. There was also a trial of 95 people drawn by lot into two groups, with 16 sessions of treatment over an average of 19 weeks. The response rate of treatment aimed at complicated grief was 51%. Ordinary interpersonal psychotherapy was 28% (P=0.02, the difference is not very likely to be a coincidence). The former also started to work faster. The number needed to treat was 4.3, that is, on average every 4.3 people treated produces one extra person who improves.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>；Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>；Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Notes: Do not count the months yourself. The real judgement is "it affects eating, sleeping, going to work, and looking after a child". If that matches, go and register. The departments are called psychiatry, psychology, or clinical psychology. Public psychiatric specialist hospitals all have them. This diagnosis is called complicated grief in some materials, and prolonged grief disorder in others. Different standards also require different lengths. Some write 6 months. Some write 12 months. The proportion 9.8% comes from 14 studies whose methods differed quite a lot. The authors also remind that it may not be carried over onto another population.

### 9. Do not start by paying for grief counselling. First see whether your grief has really got stuck (the symptoms in §29.8)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. The other way round, you save a counselling fee.
- In plain words: Arranging psychological counselling for every bereaved person has a very small benefit, and after a while it can no longer be seen, because most people would have eased on their own. But for people who already clearly cannot adapt, the treatment is as effective as treatment for other psychological problems. So first match it against §29.8 (if the grief has got stuck, go to psychiatry). If it matches, then spend this money.
- Benefit: 61 controlled studies were pooled. Giving bereaved people a psychological intervention across the board, the benefit was very small when the treatment had just ended, and after an interval the benefit could no longer be seen. An intervention that only recruited bereaved people who "already clearly cannot adapt" worked as well as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. Studies that did not screen beforehand had poor results, because the group that did not receive the intervention would also have eased on its own over time. Another 14 trials that split people by lot were pooled. Treatment done for people who had already developed a problem was clearly better than the controls both after treatment and at a later return visit, and after an interval the effect was still growing. A preventive intervention done across the board for people who had not yet developed a problem was ineffective. Another study followed 205 people from before widowhood all the way to 6 and 18 months after. This study separated five courses. They were ordinary grief, long grief, long depression, getting better during the bereavement, and resilience (being able to bear it). Of these, the resilient type was the most common. The ordinary-grief type was relatively uncommon.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. - Notes: The meaning is "not everyone needs to do it", not "no one should do it". The high-risk bereaved people in §29.4 (a relative died by suicide, accident or homicide), and the people who have already got stuck in §29.8 (if the grief has got stuck, go to psychiatry), are exactly the people the evidence supports doing it for. ### 10. After a separation or a divorce, do not let medical insurance lapse, and do not hold yourself up with alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Keep the medical insurance going, and stop the alcohol. The hard part is that in this stretch it is easy to hold yourself up with alcohol.
- In plain words: A person who is separated or divorced has a clearly higher chance of dying early than a person who is still married. Men, and younger people, rise more. This is not necessarily caused by the divorce. People who divorce may already have differed in health and income. Two things you can manage yourself: do not let medical insurance and check-ups lapse, and do not hold yourself up with tobacco and alcohol.
- Benefit: 32 studies that registered people first and then followed them were pooled. They covered more than 650 ten thousand people, 16 ten thousand deaths, more than 75.5 ten thousand divorces, and 11 countries. Compared with people still married, adults who were separated or divorced had a clearly higher risk of dying early. Men, and younger people, rose more clearly than women and older people. The authors discussed four possible paths. One is social selection: people whose health or conditions already differed are more likely to reach a divorce. Two is a break in resources: income and protection are cut off. Three is a change in health behaviour: tobacco, alcohol and the daily routine get worse. Four is chronic psychological stress (being tense over a long time).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>
- Notes: The original text only said "clearly higher" and gave group comparisons. It did not give a multiple that can be cited, so no concrete figure is written here. The size of the benefit is set to medium, as "the direction is known, and there is no drop large enough to fit a threshold". Property, debt and bride price at divorce, see section 8 and section 10. The arrangement for raising the child, see section 18.

### 11. If you want someone to talk with, call 12356. Minors and adolescents call 12355. If you need to see a doctor, register at a psychology clinic
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. Both hotlines are free. A psychology clinic is registered as an ordinary clinic visit.
- In plain words: If you want to find someone to talk to, call 12356. This is the national psychological-assistance hotline, and it is free. Minors and adolescents also have the 12355 youth comprehensive service platform. If you need to see a doctor, register at the psychology clinic of a psychiatry, psychology or clinical psychology department. When there is a family blow, a lost job or dropping out of school, community grid workers and social workers are already supposed to find it on their own.
- Benefit: A 2026 plan of the National Health Commission and other departments, 25 departments in all, requires the working system of the 12356 psychological-assistance hotline to be improved. Stable running must be ensured, and the connection rate raised. A provincial 12356 information platform is to be pushed into being. The building of a linkage with service hotlines such as 110 is to be strengthened. The 12355 youth comprehensive service platform is also to be built up and put to use, and to work in linkage with 12356. The targets by 2030 have three items. One: more than 80% of administrative villages and urban communities set up a psychological counselling room or a social-work studio and provide psychological services. Two: public psychiatric specialist hospitals at each level, and each county or district, have at least 1 hospital providing a psychology-clinic service. Three: each province builds a provincial 12356 psychological-assistance platform. The teams for psychological-crisis intervention and psychological assistance are made up of psychiatrists, psychological-treatment technicians, psychological counsellors, social-work professionals and volunteers. These three kinds are grid workers (staff in the community responsible by area), social-work professionals and volunteers. They are to find in time the risk of a psychological crisis such as a family blow, losing a job, or dropping out of school (nationwide, issued in March 2026 (month 3)).
- Evidence: B

- Notes: B is given because the counselling rooms in villages and communities, and the county-level psychology clinics, are targets to be built by 2030. ### 12. In the first three months after a blow, put off every irreversible big decision
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. It is only putting the decision off. You may miss some chances because of that. The hard part is that at the time it will feel as if the chance will not wait.
- In plain words: In this stretch your judgement of all kinds of "chances" is at its worst. People who say they can contact the person who died, people who say they can help you turn the money over and get it back, people who urge you to resign and start again somewhere else, and people who pull you into a partnership, will all come looking. Anything whose amount is over one month's income, or whose cost of going back on it is very large, is put off for three months without exception. First tell it to a person who has no interest in the matter. Do not decide the same day.
- Benefit: Avoid, in the stretch just after a blow, when judgement is at its worst, making a disposal of property that cannot be gone back on. This kind of thing includes selling a house, resigning, putting a pension or a compensation sum into one investment all at once, remarrying in a hurry, and standing guarantee for someone. The amount of this kind of decision is usually on the scale of several ten thousand yuan up to one home. The benefit is therefore set to medium. An official document likewise lists "a family blow, losing a job, dropping out of school" as psychological-crisis risks that the community is to check on its own. The state in this stretch is already treated as a state that needs an outsider to lend a hand.
- Evidence: C
- Sources: author's experience, no direct literature；「家庭变故、失业、失学」as the wording for a psychological-crisis risk, see 国家卫生健康委等 25 部门 (2026). 健全社会心理服务体系和危机干预机制实施方案（国卫医政发〔2026〕8 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Notes: If there is no such person with no interest in the matter beside you, tell it once on 12356 (see §29.11, if you want someone to talk with, call 12356), or write it down yourself and read it again three days later. "Three months" is a line the author drew. No literature supports this concrete length, so it is recorded as grade C. Common frauds aimed at bereaved people and older people, see section 17 (investment for old-age care, use the house for old-age care) and section 6 (fortune-telling). The pits of guarantees and IOUs, see section 8. A few sums of money have to be gone and handled on your own. They are not in the "put it off" group: the housing-fund balance, social-insurance benefits, and the three work-death sums. See section 25 and section 19.

### 13. Do not use death as a way of paying off debt: life insurance within two years does not pay, a work injury is not recognised, and the debt is still taken out of the estate first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. It is only working this account through.
- In plain words: If there is a suicide within two years of a life-insurance contract, it does not pay. It only returns the small sum in the policy that can be returned. A work injury is not recognised either. Of the three work-death sums, not one fen is paid. The debt is taken out of the estate you leave first. The family only gets what is left. They also have to bear the health cost that §29.4 (bereavement by suicide or a violent death) talks about.
- Benefit: Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs a contract whose condition for paying the insurance money is the death of the insured. Counted from the day the contract is formed, or from the day the contract's effect is restored, if the insured dies by suicide within two years, the insurer does not bear the duty of paying the insurance money. If the insured, at the time of the suicide, is a person with no capacity for civil conduct, that is, a person who in law cannot be responsible for their own acts, they are not in this group. In that situation the insurer shall, as the contract agrees, return the cash value of the policy. That is, it only returns the small sum in the policy that can be returned. It does not pay the insured amount. A life-insurance amount is usually on the scale of several tens of ten thousand yuan. The benefit is therefore set to large. Item 3 of article 16 of the Regulation on Work-Injury Insurance provides: self-harm or suicide shall not be recognised as a work injury or as the equivalent of a work injury. The three work-death benefits in article 39 then all fall through. One is the funeral subsidy, paid for 6 months at the previous year's average monthly wage of employees in the locality. Two is the pension for relatives who were supported. Three is the one-off work-death subsidy, 20 times the previous year's national per-capita disposable income of urban residents. Article 1159 of the Civil Code provides: when the estate is divided, the taxes and debts the deceased ought by law to have paid shall be cleared. That is, the estate pays the tax and the debt first, and only what is left is divided. Article 1161 provides: an heir clears the debt up to the actual value of the estate they received. That is, only the part inherited is used to pay, and they do not have to put in more of their own. A person who gives up the inheritance may bear no duty to clear it.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2015 修正). 中华人民共和国保险法第四十四条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811>；国务院 (2010 修订). 工伤保险条例（国务院令第 586 号）第十六条、第三十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>；全国人大 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典第一千一百五十九条、第一千一百六十一条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: Here only the money account is counted. The other entries in this section and in section 1 say not to reach this step. Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs only the first two years. It is written so as to state the article in full. It is not giving a timetable. The family's health cost, see §29.4 (a person whose spouse died by suicide has a higher risk of a new mental disorder within 5 years, by seven to eight tenths).
