# 7. How to survive with no money

This section counts money and entitlements. How much you can claim, how much you can get waived, what services you can get, and what conditions you have to meet — all of that is here, and none of it gets converted into lifespan. Policy changes every year, and it varies by region, so treat the amounts and thresholds here only as a guide: check what your own area has published for the current year. The entries are ordered like this. Items that cost nothing, take little paperwork and work quickly come first. Items that need you to build up long-term eligibility, or that vary heavily by region, come later. Items that rest only on the author's experience, with no official document behind them, come last.

The entries in this section fall into the blocks below, with the entry numbers in brackets.

**Lost your job**: claim unemployment insurance online (§7.1), get recognised as a person with employment difficulties (§7.12), claim a vocational training subsidy (§7.13).

**Owed wages**: report to labour inspection first (§7.2), apply for legal aid (§7.3), work out who actually employed you first (§7.22).

**Emergency safety net**: go to a shelter (§7.4), apply for temporary assistance (§7.6), apply for the minimum subsistence allowance (§7.7), the two disability subsidies (§7.8), replace a lost ID card at once (§7.11), when you truly have nowhere to sleep (§7.21).

**Medical and social insurance**: do not let your resident medical insurance lapse (§7.9), for serious illness, go through medical insurance and medical assistance first (§7.10), do not panic over a gap in social insurance payments (§7.18), add a medical or critical-illness insurance policy (§7.20).

**Finding work and starting over**: public employment services and gig-work markets (§7.5), use a systematic method to job-hunt (§7.14), do not hand over a deposit or your ID (§7.15), a path back even after prison or bankruptcy (§7.19).

**Housing and food**: get on the public rental housing list (§7.16), cut housing and food costs to the minimum (§7.17).

### 1. If you lose your job, claim unemployment insurance online first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You can file the application on your phone through any of three channels: the "掌上12333" app (the official social-insurance phone app), the electronic social security card mini-program inside WeChat or Alipay, or the National Social Insurance Public Service Platform. Having your social security card or your ID card is enough, and most places do not need you to go in person. The condition is that you have already registered as unemployed.
- In plain words: If your employer and you have together paid into unemployment insurance for at least 1 year, and you did not resign of your own accord, you can get paid once you register as unemployed. Pay in for 1 to under 5 years and you can get up to 12 months of payments. 5 to under 10 years gets up to 18 months. 10 years or more gets up to 24 months. While you are receiving the payments, your employee medical insurance premium is paid for you out of the unemployment insurance fund.
- Benefit: You have to meet three conditions at the same time. First, your employer and you together have paid into unemployment insurance for a cumulative 1 year or more. Second, you did not resign of your own accord — the law calls this "having your employment broken off through no wish of your own". Third, you have already registered as unemployed and you are willing to look for work. How long you can draw it is set by how many years you paid in. A cumulative 1 year to under 5 years gets you at most 12 months. 5 years to under 10 years gets at most 18 months. 10 years or more gets at most 24 months. How much you get paid each month is set by the provincial government, and it differs from province to province. The amount sits below the local minimum wage and above the city's minimum-subsistence allowance. The state's target is to gradually raise it to 90% of the minimum wage standard. While you are receiving unemployment insurance payments, you still take part in the employee basic medical insurance scheme and keep its benefits. The basic medical insurance premium you would otherwise owe is paid out of the unemployment insurance fund, and you do not pay it yourself (nationwide; the regulation has been in force since 1999)

- Sources: 国务院 (1999). 失业保险条例（国务院令第 258 号，第十四、十六、十七、十八条；第十六条：「失业保险金自办理失业登记之日起计算」）. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=517>；人力资源社会保障部. 全国人社政务服务平台·失业保险待遇申领办事指南. <https://www.12333.gov.cn/portal/common/bszn/sydysl?pfaId=202105281700000004>；国家发展改革委就业司 (2022). 失业保险金标准，将逐步提高至最低工资的 90%. <https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/fggz/jyysr/jysrsbxf/202206/t20220627_1328819.html>；辽宁省人力资源和社会保障厅 (2019). 关于进一步调整失业保险金标准的通知：「为落实《人力资源社会保障部财政部关于调整失业保险金标准的指导意见》(人社部发〔2017〕71号〕"逐步将失业保险金标准提高到最低工资标准的90%"等要求」. <https://rst.ln.gov.cn/rst/zfxx/zc/ywfl/shbx/2024092014161280895/index.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2010). 社会保险法（第四十八条）. <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5192937/content.html>（国家税务总局政策法规库）
- Notes: Resigning of your own accord does not count as "through no wish of your own", and you cannot draw the payment. So when you leave, do not sign "resigning for personal reasons", see §19.7 (do not sign "resigning for personal reasons"). Your unemployment insurance payments are counted from the day you register as unemployed, so registering a month late means losing a month's money. The first thing to do once you leave is go and register. Registering needs a leaving certificate from your employer, see §19.17 (leaving certificate). Whether you have to honour a non-compete agreement, and what to do if the company will not pay the compensation for it, see §11.12 (signed a non-compete agreement). While you are drawing the payments, your employee medical insurance is paid for you out of unemployment insurance. The amount differs a great deal between provinces, so go by what the place where you are insured has published. The figure "90% of the minimum wage" comes from a 2017 guiding opinion from the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security and the Ministry of Finance (人社部发〔2017〕71号). Its actual wording is "gradually raise the unemployment insurance payment standard to 90% of the minimum wage standard". That is a target, not what is currently paid. The original page on the Ministry's own website could not be opened from this machine, so this line was checked against the quotation in a Liaoning provincial document.

### 2. If your wages are owed, report to labour inspection first, then apply for labour arbitration — neither route charges a fee, and most cases are resolved within a few months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing — neither reporting it nor arbitration charges a fee. You need to put together your labour contract, pay slips, attendance records, transfer records and chat logs, and make 1 to 3 trips to the human resources and social security bureau or the arbitration committee. Applying for arbitration has a 1-year time limit (the law calls this the arbitration limitation period). If you are still working at the company and your wages are being withheld, you are not bound by this 1-year limit. If you have already left, you must bring the claim within 1 year counted from the day you left. The hard part is making trip after trip and digging up evidence piece by piece, which makes it easy to lose heart partway through.
- In plain words: Neither route costs money. First call 12333, or report it to the labour inspection section of the human resources and social security bureau. Once a case is opened, it is investigated within 60 working days, and the company can be ordered to pay within a set time, with an extra 50% to 100% on top if it still does not pay after the deadline. If that does not work, apply for labour arbitration, which closes the case within 45 days. But "closing the case" is not the same as "the money landing in your account": if the company has no assets and the boss has run off, you can win and still not get paid.
- Benefit: The first step is to report it. Call 12333, or report it to the labour inspection section of your local human resources and social security bureau. The law requires the case to be investigated within 60 working days of being opened, with a further 30 working days allowed if it is complicated. Once it is confirmed, the company can be ordered to pay within a set deadline. If it still does not pay once that deadline has passed, it must pay an extra 50% to 100% of the amount owed on top, as compensation. In 2024, labour inspection bodies nationwide investigated 137 thousand cases and recovered 8.65 billion yuan in wages for 654 thousand people. If you are holding an IOU or a pay record and the wages owed are clearly established, you can also apply directly to a grassroots court for a payment order. A payment order is a court order telling the company to pay, issued within 15 days of the court accepting the application. The second step is applying for arbitration. The law requires the case to close within 45 days of being accepted, with a further 15 days allowed. If you are claiming wages you can apply for advance enforcement: part of the judgment is enforced before the whole case has finished being heard. Where the amount owed does not exceed 12 times the local monthly minimum wage, the case counts as final at arbitration: once arbitration has ruled, it is settled, and the company cannot then sue. In 2024, arbitration bodies nationwide handled 4.257 million cases and closed 4.156 million of them. The amount involved in closed cases was 93.47 billion yuan. The mediation success rate was 79.6%, the arbitration closure rate was 98.2%, and the arbitration finality rate was 73.7%. When people say this can drag on for "six months to a year", they mean the part of the cases that go on to court after arbitration: a first-instance trial of 6 months (3 months under the simplified procedure), plus a further 3 months for an appeal. In 2024, courts nationwide concluded 88 thousand cases of claims for labour pay and recovered 28.54 billion yuan, and 998 people were sentenced for the crime of refusing to pay labour remuneration (nationwide)

If the company has no assets and the boss has run off, you can win and still not get paid. The Supreme People's Court only publishes the total amount actually enforced (2.3 trillion yuan in 2024) and says "the completion rate for enforcement stays high". There is no official figure for how much of the money owed in wage cases actually ends up in people's pockets. The bulletin does not define "arbitration finality rate"; this entry reads it as "the share of rulings that did not go on to court afterwards". Owing wages above a certain amount is already enough for a criminal case to be opened. One threshold is owing one person wages for 3 months or more, in an amount of 5000 to 20000 yuan or above. The other is owing 10 or more people a cumulative 30000 to 100000 yuan or above. In either case, once the company has been ordered to pay and still does not, that is enough to open a case. Construction-site migrant workers also have the dedicated wage account and the rule that the main contractor pays first, and going after the main contractor works better than going after the labour subcontractor. Both routes assume there is an employer. An unlicensed workshop or shop counts just the same, and you can still report it and go to arbitration. Doing private work for a household or an individual, such as working as a nanny, does not count as an employment relationship, and you have to go to court instead, see §7.22 (work out who actually employed you first). Migrant workers applying for legal aid to claim wages are not means-tested. ### 3. If you cannot afford a lawyer, apply for legal aid — wage claims, maintenance payments and workplace injury cases are already covered
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Legal aid does not charge the person it helps a single yuan.
- In plain words: Claiming wages, claiming maintenance payments, claiming social insurance or minimum-subsistence benefits, or being left out of pocket after stepping in to help someone in danger — all of these can get free legal aid. Call 12348, or go to your local legal aid centre. You no longer have to go back to your home town for a certificate of financial hardship; it is enough to describe your own financial situation truthfully. A defendant who may be sentenced to death, and a defendant who is blind, deaf, mute or a minor, is assigned a lawyer regardless of means.
- Benefit: The State Council's regulation sets out in so many words the six kinds of civil matters legal aid covers. The regulation's own wording is: "lawfully claiming state compensation; claiming social insurance benefits or minimum-subsistence benefits; claiming a pension or relief payment; claiming maintenance, child support or dependant support; claiming unpaid labour remuneration; asserting civil rights and interests arising from a brave act in aid of another". In plain terms: claiming state compensation, claiming social insurance or minimum-subsistence benefits, claiming pensions or relief payments, claiming maintenance or child support, claiming unpaid wages, and being left out of pocket after stepping in to help someone. On the criminal side, a defendant who may be sentenced to death, and a defendant who is blind, deaf, mute or a minor, is assigned a lawyer regardless of their financial situation
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2003). 法律援助条例（国令第 385 号，第十条、第十一条、第十二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6944.htm>；司法部有关负责人 (2021). 就贯彻实施法律援助法答记者问. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2021-12/29/content_5665114.htm>
- Notes: The Legal Aid Law has been in force since 1 January 2022, and it changed one thing. It used to require "submitting a certificate of financial hardship". Now it is enough for you to describe your own financial situation truthfully, and you no longer have to go back to your home town for a certificate. The access points are the legal aid centres and public legal service centres in each area, and you can also call 12348.

### 4. When you have nowhere else to turn, go to a shelter — it covers food, a bed and the ticket home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Go to the local assistance management station yourself, or tell a police officer or the civil affairs department and have them take you there. On entry you have to truthfully register your name and what you are carrying. The hard part is having to tell someone out loud that you have no money and nowhere to go.
- In plain words: A shelter provides food and a bed, sends you to hospital if you fall ill, and helps you contact your family. If you have no money to get home, it issues you a ticket. You normally stay no more than 10 days. Coming and leaving are both your own choice — this is not detention, and it does not hand out cash. The ticket is issued to your registered household address or your place of residence, so decide where you are heading before you go.
- Benefit: A shelter provides food and lodging that meet hygiene requirements. If you fall suddenly ill while there, you are sent to hospital for treatment. Staff also help you contact your relatives and your employer. If you have no money for a ticket back to your registered household address or your place of residence, you are issued a travel voucher — a bus, train or boat ticket. Your registered household address is your home town, and your place of residence is where you actually live now. The period of assistance is normally no more than 10 days, and it can be extended while you wait for family to collect you, for treatment, or for placement. Police and other administrative officials who come across a homeless or begging person while on duty must tell them they can go and ask for help. People with disabilities, minors, older people and people who have trouble moving must be guided and escorted there (nationwide, since 2003)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2003). 城市生活无着的流浪乞讨人员救助管理办法（国务院令第 381 号，第五、六、七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62246.htm>；民政部 (2003). 城市生活无着的流浪乞讨人员救助管理办法实施细则（民政部令第 24 号，第十一、十二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62510.htm>
- Notes: Asking for help and leaving the shelter are both voluntary; this is not detention. What it gives you is basic food and lodging, not cash. The travel voucher is issued to your registered household address or your place of residence, so work out where you are going before you ask for help.

### 5. Look for work through free public employment services and gig-work markets first, not a fee-charging agency
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Register with a public employment service body at county level or above, such as an employment service centre or a human resources market. You can also register with the local gig-work market. If you do not know the address, call 12333 and ask.
- In plain words: Public bodies such as employment service centres and human resources markets do not charge for job placement, career guidance, policy advice, or handling your employment and unemployment registration. The law states in so many words that these bodies may not use these services to make money. Registering with a gig-work market and posting information there are free too. While you are at it, get your unemployment registration done — you need it both to draw unemployment payments and to be recognised as a person with employment difficulties.
- Benefit: The law makes the following free across the board: employment policy advice; information on who is hiring and where workers are needed; career guidance and job placement; handling employment and unemployment registration; and employment assistance for people with employment difficulties. These bodies may not run for-profit activities or make money from these services. Job-seeking and recruitment information for gig work, and registering and posting it, are also provided to the public free of charge, and this has been brought inside the scope of public employment information services. People in difficulty among gig workers, such as those who go a long time without getting work, and low-income households, are to get extra support (nationwide)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2007, 2015 修正). 就业促进法（第三十五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647636.htm>；人力资源社会保障部等五部门 (2022). 关于加强零工市场建设 完善求职招聘服务的意见（人社部发〔2022〕38 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-07/09/content_5700177.htm>
- Notes: Unemployment registration is a precondition for drawing unemployment insurance payments and for being recognised as a person with employment difficulties, so get it done while you are there. If you do gig work paid by the day, check the amount on the spot, and keep the other party's contact details and a record of the work you did.

### 6. After a sudden crisis, apply for temporary assistance first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Apply to the township government or the sub-district office, and explain clearly what has happened to your household — a fire, a traffic accident, a family member suddenly falling seriously ill, or your unavoidable everyday expenses suddenly rising. After it is reviewed and made public, the county-level civil affairs department approves it. Prepare the materials from the checklist your local office gives you; this usually takes 1 to 2 trips.
- In plain words: If something sudden happens to your household — a fire, a car crash, a family member suddenly falling seriously ill — and it has crushed your basic living for the time being, you can apply to the township government or the sub-district office for temporary assistance. You do not have to already be on the minimum-subsistence allowance. It is usually paid as a single one-off sum, and the amount varies a great deal from place to place.
- Benefit: Two kinds of household can get temporary assistance. One is a household that, because of an accident or a family member suddenly falling seriously ill, cannot for the time being manage even the most basic costs of food, shelter and medical care. The other is a household already on the minimum-subsistence allowance whose unavoidable costs have suddenly risen beyond what it can bear. Which situations are covered and how much is paid are set and published by local governments at county level and above (a nationwide system, with the amount set locally, since 2014)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国务院 (2014, 2019 修订). 社会救助暂行办法（国务院令第 649 号，第四十七、四十八、四十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5468952.htm>
- Notes: You do not have to already be on the minimum-subsistence allowance. It is usually paid as a single one-off sum, the amount varies a great deal by area, and you should go by what your local area has published for that year.

### 7. If your income is below the local minimum-subsistence line, apply for the minimum subsistence allowance
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. A member of the household who shares daily living submits a written application to the township government or sub-district office where the household is registered. If it is inconvenient for you to handle it yourself, you can ask the village or residents' committee to do it for you. You have to accept a check of your household's income and assets. This usually takes 2 to 3 trips, with your ID card, household register and proof of income and assets. The hard part is laying your household's finances open for inspection, and then waiting through the public notice period.
- In plain words: If your whole household's income divided by the number of people is below the local minimum-subsistence line, and your household's assets also meet the rules, you can apply. At the end of 2024, the national average urban minimum-subsistence line was 798.1 yuan per person per month, and the rural line was 593.9 yuan. What gets paid, once approved, is the difference between the minimum-subsistence line and your household's income per person per month. Owning a home, a car or savings, or having children able to support you, may get your application turned down.
- Benefit: If your whole household's income divided by the number of people is below the local minimum-subsistence standard, and your household's assets are also within the rules, you can get the minimum-subsistence allowance. At the end of 2024, the national average urban minimum-subsistence standard was 798.1 yuan per person per month, and the rural standard was 593.9 yuan. The urban scheme covered 6.25 million people, and the rural scheme covered 33.615 million. The allowance is paid monthly as the difference: the local minimum-subsistence standard minus the household's income per person. For example, if the local standard is 798.1 yuan and the household's income is 500 yuan per person per month, each person gets 298.1 yuan a month, not 798.1 yuan. The minimum-subsistence standard is set this way: take the local area's per-person consumption spending from the previous year, multiply it by a set proportion, and adjust it again after a period. In plain terms, it is a discount worked out from how much an average person in the area spent the year before (a national average; it differs by area)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2014, 2019 修订). 社会救助暂行办法（国务院令第 649 号，第九、十、十一条；第十二条：「县级人民政府民政部门按照共同生活的家庭成员人均收入低于当地最低生活保障标准的差额，按月发给最低生活保障金」）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5468952.htm>；国家统计局 (2025). 社会事业向好发展 民生福祉不断增进——「十四五」以来社会民生统计报告. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202509/content_7042627.htm>；民政部等四部门 (2024). 关于进一步做好最低生活保障标准确定调整工作的指导意见（民发〔2024〕16 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202403/content_7007237.htm>
- Notes: The minimum-subsistence allowance is the last line of support, and you have to apply at your registered household address. A home, a car or savings above the local standard, or children able to support you, may get your application turned down. Once it is approved there are other benefits too: a subsidy towards your resident medical insurance, see §7.9 (resident medical insurance); access to medical assistance when you see a doctor, see §7.10 (medical assistance); and not being means-tested when you apply for legal aid, see §7.3 (legal aid).

### 8. If you hold a disability certificate, apply for the two disability subsidies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Bring your disability certificate and submit one application at the sub-district office or the township government.
- In plain words: A person with a disability in a household on the minimum-subsistence allowance can draw a living subsidy. Someone whose disability is graded level 1 or level 2, and who needs long-term care, can draw a care subsidy. You can draw both at once. Each province sets its own standard, and they differ a great deal, so ask the sub-district office for the figure. Bring your second-generation disability certificate and handle it in one visit to the sub-district office or the township government.
- Benefit: The State Council has set up two subsidies. The first is the living subsidy for disabled people in difficulty. ### 22. If your wages are owed, work out who actually employed you first: an unlicensed workshop or a labour subcontractor still goes through labour inspection, and only private work for a household or an individual goes to court
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Work out three things first: whether the other party is running a shop, factory or business; whether the work was subcontracted down from a company or a construction site; and whether you were working inside someone's own home. Whether the other party has a business licence can be checked on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System.
- In plain words: An unlicensed workshop or shop that owes you wages can still be reported to labour inspection and taken to labour arbitration, and the owner personally can be pursued too. If a labour subcontractor on a construction site owes you money, the main contractor or the construction party pays instead. Only private work for a household or an individual, such as working as a nanny, does not count as a labour dispute, and you have to sue in court instead.
- Benefit: Article 2 of the Labour Contract Law limits "employer" to organisations such as enterprises, individual economic organisations and privately-run non-enterprise units, and does not include a natural person. The Supreme People's Court's judicial interpretation on labour dispute cases lists several kinds of dispute that do not count as labour disputes. One is a dispute between a household or an individual and their domestic-service worker. Two, a dispute between an individual craftsperson and a helper or apprentice. Three, a dispute between a rural contracted household and someone it employs. For these you have to sue in court as an ordinary civil dispute, with a 3-year litigation limitation period. An employer with no business licence is not covered by this exception. Article 93 of the Labour Contract Law provides that where an employer lacking the proper qualifications to operate has had a worker perform labour, that employer, or the person who funded it, must pay the labour remuneration, the economic compensation and the damages owed. Article 29 of that interpretation provides that where a worker has a dispute with an employer that never obtained a business licence, had its licence revoked, or kept operating past its licensed term, the employer or the person who funded it must be named as a party. Article 33 of the Labour Security Supervision Regulation provides that the labour security administrative department carries out labour security supervision over anyone with no business licence, or whose licence has been lawfully revoked, who is nonetheless employing labour. Where an individual operating under contract unlawfully takes on a worker and causes them harm, the contracting organisation and the individual operator bear joint liability for damages (Article 94 of the Labour Contract Law). Where a construction party or a main contractor sub-lets or subcontracts a project to an individual or a unit lacking proper qualifications to operate, and this leads to migrant workers' wages going unpaid, the construction party or the main contractor must settle the debt (Article 36 of the Regulation on Guaranteeing Payment of Wages to Migrant Workers) (nationwide)
