# 13. In an emergency: what to do first

This section covers what to do first when something has happened, what to do next, and what not to do. The first-aid and wilderness entries are counted on specific causes of death and on the share of people who live. The threat and scam entries are counted in money and personal freedom. The two kinds of number are not converted into each other. The entries are ordered from the top down by "how common this is × how many lives doing it right or wrong is worth". These entries are above all for you, your spouse and your immediate family. 79.2% of cardiac arrests happen at home, so the person you are most likely to save is someone in your own family. Friends and colleagues come next. For a stranger the benefit is not zero either, but this tier is the lowest, and you may also be shaken down or dragged into a case. So entries of this kind put the actions that protect you, and the clauses that release you from liability, first.

This section only covers actions you take within minutes. Recognising a scam, or being squeezed for money by someone holding something over you, is handled over days, and does not belong here. For the hard anti-fraud rules and the seven most common scams, see §8.3 (hard anti-fraud rules). For someone demanding money over private photos or a recording of a nude video call, see §8.32 (the other side holds something over you and demands money). If the money has already gone out, call 110 or 96110 at once and ask for a payment stop, see §8.2 (payment stop).

The entries in this section fall into the blocks below, with the entry numbers in brackets.

**CPR and choking**: someone collapses and is not breathing (§13.1), an older person falls and you should not rush to lift them (§13.2), someone chokes and cannot speak (§13.26), a baby chokes and cannot make a sound (§13.43), infant CPR (§13.44).

**Sudden acute illness**: the mouth suddenly turns crooked and one side goes weak (§13.3), the room spins and you cannot stand (§13.4), one eye suddenly goes dark (§13.5), one eye is swollen and painful (§13.6), pressing pain in the chest (§13.7), a tearing, ripping pain (§13.8), the worst headache of your life (§13.9), an older person becomes slow after a knock on the head (§13.10), one leg suddenly swells up (§13.11), anaphylactic shock (§13.15), someone collapses in convulsions (§13.16), a person with diabetes suddenly starts shaking (§13.17).

**Injuries and burns**: press on the wound first in major bleeding (§13.12), bitten by a dog or a cat (§13.13), run cool water over a burn (§13.14), do not pull out an object stuck in the body (§13.40), do not move a suspected fracture (§13.41).

**Poisoning, fire and accidents**: cut the power first when someone is electrocuted (§13.18), the carbon monoxide alarm goes off (§13.19), swallowing cleaning product, pesticide or medicine by mistake (§13.20), a chemical splashed on you (§13.21), dizziness and no sweating in the heat (§13.22), giving a heatstroke patient lightly salted water (§13.23), crawl along the floor in a fire (§13.24), you see someone drowning (§13.25), look at the building you are in first in an earthquake (§13.30).

**Wilderness**: stay where you are if you get lost in uninhabited country (§13.27), shivering and slurred speech mean hypothermia (§13.28), do not cut or suck a snakebite (§13.29), meeting a bear, a wild boar or feral dogs (§13.31), caught in a thunderstorm outdoors (§13.32), go up slowly onto the plateau (§13.33), a tick attached to you (§13.34), boil water in the wild before drinking it (§13.35).

**Conflict, and after you have helped someone**: being shaken down for money out in open country (§13.36), coming across a group fight (§13.37), being hurt or out of pocket after helping someone (§13.39).

**Sexual assault and HIV exposure**: possible HIV exposure (§13.38), after a sexual assault (§13.42).

### 1. If someone collapses and is not breathing, press hard on the chest at once, and have a bystander call 120 and fetch an AED
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. A CPR course at the Red Cross or a hospital takes half a day to a day, and learning it once lasts you years. You can do it without having been taught: stack your hands on the middle of the line between the two nipples and press down hard about 2 times a second, without stopping.
- In plain words: The person you are most likely to be pressing on is someone in your own family, because 79.2% of cardiac arrests happen at home. When someone does the compressions, the share of people who leave hospital alive goes from 3.9% up to 16.1%. If the person does not answer when you call out and is not breathing, just start pressing. The place is the middle of the line between the two nipples, you stack your hands and press hard, 2 times a second, without stopping. Have a second person call 120 and bring an AED, and keep your own hands going.
- Benefit: 79 studies with 142,740 people taken together (a meta-analysis): among people whose heart stopped outside hospital (out-of-hospital cardiac arrest), only 7.6% in total left hospital alive. Where the people present did CPR, the share who left hospital alive went from 3.9% up to 16.1%. Someone was there and saw it start in 53% of cases, but only 32% actually got compressions from a bystander. China's BASIC-OHCA registry recorded 38,227 cases attended by emergency services. In 30,282 of them (79.2%) the cardiac arrest happened at home. In this group the bystander compression rate was 20.3%, and 1.2% left hospital alive. The order is: compressions first, then have a second person phone and fetch an AED. Once the machine is switched on, do what its voice tells you
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sasson C 等 (2010). Predictors of survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.109.889576> ; Zheng J 等 (2023). Incidence, process of care, and outcomes of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China: a prospective study of the BASIC-OHCA registry. The Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00173-1> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: For a baby under 1 the technique is different and you have to give breaths, see §13.44 (infant CPR). Do not first press the point under the nose, do not first look for the family, and do not first film a video. Do not hold back from pressing hard out of fear of breaking a rib either. Article 184 of the Civil Code states plainly: where a rescuer gives emergency help voluntarily and injures the person being helped, the rescuer bears no civil liability.

### 2. When an older person falls, or anyone collapses, crouch down and call out to them and call 120 first, and do not rush to lift them up; with a stranger, walking away is legal too, and if you do stop, do not move them with your hands
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Crouching down to ask a question and making a phone call take a few minutes. The hard part is holding back the impulse to reach out and lift. With a stranger there is a further cost to count: calling 120 leaves your number on record, you may get a call back, and you may be questioned once. If the family really does come after you, a lawsuit is a matter of months at the least. And even if the court finds for you in the end, you still pay your own lawyer's fee.
- In plain words: The main line of this entry is older people in your own family and people you know. First see whether they are breathing, and if they are not, press on the chest as in §13.1 (press hard on the chest). Even if they are breathing, do not pick them up: the person may have had a stroke or fainted first and fallen because of it, and forcing them up makes it worse. Crouch down and call out to them, and ask whether they remember how they came to be on the ground. If their back hurts, or it looks like a fracture, do not move them at all, and call 120 where they are.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Health's technical guideline on preventing falls in older people states two things plainly. One is that "falls are the leading cause of injury death among people over 65 in our country". The other is that "when you find an older person has fallen, do not hurry to lift them up; handle it according to the situation". If the person is not fully conscious, call the emergency number at once. If they are vomiting, turn the head to one side and clear the mouth and nose. If breathing and the heartbeat have stopped, start chest compressions at once. "If they have to be moved, keep it steady, and lie them flat if you can". If the person is clear-headed, first ask whether they remember the fall, and "if they cannot remember, it may be a faint or a cerebrovascular accident". If they have a severe headache, or a drooping mouth, trouble speaking, or weak hands and feet, "lifting the older person up at once may worsen a brain bleed or a lack of blood to the brain, and make the condition worse". If there are signs of a fracture, or back pain, or loss of bladder or bowel control, "if you have no relevant professional knowledge, do not move them casually". In all of the above, call the emergency number at once. If the older person wants to get up themselves, you may help them rise slowly, and then "let them sit or lie and rest, and watch them; only leave once you are sure they are all right". On the fear of being shaken down, the law says this. Article 90 of the Supreme People's Court's Interpretation on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law has two sentences. The first is that "a party shall provide evidence to prove the facts on which its own claim is based". The second is that "where, before judgment is given, a party has failed to provide evidence, or the evidence is not enough to prove its factual assertion, the party bearing the burden of proof bears the unfavourable consequences". Article 1198 of the Civil Code reads: "Where an operator or manager of a business or public place such as a hotel, shopping mall, bank, station, airport, sports venue or entertainment venue, or the organiser of a mass activity, fails to fulfil the duty to ensure safety and thereby causes harm to another, they shall bear tort liability." Article 184 of the Civil Code: "Where voluntary emergency assistance causes harm to the person being assisted, the rescuer bears no civil liability". The Supreme People's Court has published a model case. The operator of a pharmacy did CPR on a person in cardiac arrest and broke the person's ribs. The person helped spent 18 days in hospital and claimed over 9 thousand yuan. The court ruled that the rescuer bore no civil liability
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 卫生部 (2011). 老年人跌倒干预技术指南（「老年人跌倒后的处理」）. Full PDF at 广东省疾病预防控制中心 <http://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/gcdcdfiles/editor/uploadfile/20111027094832117.pdf>, full text of the on-scene handling part as reprinted by 贵南县人民政府 <https://www.guinan.gov.cn/lnb/zc1__zwgk/zc/aztfl/qt/content_27609778>；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十三、一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2023). 人民法院抓实公正与效率践行社会主义核心价值观典型案例（案例十三，齐某某诉孙某某健康权纠纷案，辽宁省康平县人民法院）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/408162.html>；最高人民法院 (2022 年第二次修正，2022 年 4 月 10 日施行). 关于适用《中华人民共和国民事诉讼法》的解释（第九十条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html>；民法典第一千一百九十八条 is in the same 最高检 reprinted full text as above；全国人大常委会 (2023 年修正，2024 年 1 月 1 日施行). 民事诉讼法（第一百五十二、一百六十四条）. Full text reprinted by 上海市发展和改革委员会 <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>；国务院 (2006). 诉讼费用交纳办法（国务院令第 481 号，第六、二十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，2026 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第二十六条第一项、第五十条第五项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>
- Notes: B is assigned because the guideline only says how to handle it, with no number for how many deaths following it avoids. What Article 184 releases you from is harm you cause in the course of helping, and it assumes you acted voluntarily and that the situation was urgent. It does not cover things like "deciding on your own to bundle the person into a private car and drive them to hospital", and waiting for 120 is the safer course. Do not hang up after calling 120; do what the dispatcher on the line tells you. This set of judgements is first of all for older people in your own family and people you know. With someone you do not know on the street, walking away is not against the law, and it is also the least trouble: no law requires you to help. The costs of stopping have to be counted one layer at a time: your time, leaving your number, the family coming after you, being sued, the matter reaching your employer, being filmed and put online. For details see [docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md](../../../docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md). If you do decide to stop, the one thing you must do is **not move them with your hands**, and the reason is medical: forcing someone up may worsen a brain bleed or a spinal injury. There is one exception: if this is a place you run or manage, or the activity is one you organised, walking away may make you liable. The duty to ensure safety under Article 1198 of the Civil Code requires you to carry a share of responsibility for the safety of the people in your venue. For preventing falls in the first place, see §1.13 (training balance and leg strength). For the delayed symptoms after an older person knocks their head, see §13.10 (an older person becomes slow after a knock on the head). For the money after you are hurt helping someone, see §13.39 (hurt or out of pocket after helping someone). For how to keep evidence and report online abuse, see §8.16 (keep evidence then report online abuse).

### 3. If the mouth suddenly turns crooked, one arm goes weak, or speech comes out unclear, call 120 at once; do not wait and do not drive yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Three actions, and you know them after seeing them once: ask the person to smile, to hold both arms out level, and to say a whole sentence.
- In plain words: A crooked mouth, one arm that will not lift, speech that comes out unclear — call 120 at once. If the drug that opens the blocked vessel is given within 3 hours of onset, the chance of recovering to looking after yourself in daily life is about 1.75 times what it is without the drug. Dragging it out to between 3 and 4.5 hours leaves only 1.26 times. Past 4.5 hours no difference can be seen. Write down the exact time the symptoms started. Do not give water or medicine, and do not drive yourself.
- Benefit: Individual data from 16 randomised trials of intravenous thrombolysis, 6756 patients, taken together (a meta-analysis). Intravenous thrombolysis means giving a drug to open the blocked vessel. With alteplase thrombolysis within 3 hours of onset, the odds ratio for recovering to looking after yourself in daily life (modified Rankin score 0–1) was 1.75, 95% CI 1.35–2.27 (the range the figure can credibly lie in). The chance of this result is about 1.75 times what it is without thrombolysis. Dragging it out to between 3 and 4.5 hours gives 1.26 (1.05–1.51). Past 4.5 hours it is 1.15 (0.95–1.40), and no difference can be seen statistically any more
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929-1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>
- Notes: The earlier thrombolysis happens the better it works, and past 4.5 hours it is of basically no use. You call 120 rather than driving yourself because the ambulance takes the person straight to a hospital with a stroke centre, and tells that hospital in advance to get ready. Write down the exact time the symptoms started, because the doctor uses it to decide whether thrombolysis is possible. Do not give water or medicine, and do not wait to "rest a while and see"

### 4. If the room suddenly spins and the person cannot stand, they see double, one eye goes dark, or their finger cannot find the tip of their own nose, call 120 for a stroke as well
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Add two more to the three actions of "face, arms, speech" in §13.3 (sudden crooked mouth and unclear speech). One is to have the person stand straight with their eyes closed and see whether they sway, and then ask whether they see double or whether a piece of their vision is missing. The other, if you suspect it in yourself, is to reach out and touch the tip of your nose, then close your eyes and do it again.
- In plain words: If you only look at the three items "face, arms, speech", you miss more than one stroke in ten, because some strokes do not start with a crooked mouth or a paralysed arm. Count not being able to stand steady, and seeing double or missing a piece of vision, and the share missed drops to 4.4%. If the room suddenly spins so the person cannot stand, if they see double, or if a finger cannot find the tip of their own nose, call 120 all the same, and write down the time all the same.
- Benefit: The stroke centre at the University of Kentucky in the United States reviewed the records of 736 cases of acute ischaemic stroke (a stroke caused by a blocked vessel) in 2014. 14.1% of these patients had none of the three symptoms — crooked face, arm weakness, unclear speech (FAST) — at onset. Adding "unsteady walking, leg weakness, visual symptoms" to the screen brought the share missed down from 14.1% to 4.4%
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>
- Notes: When a vessel in the area of the cerebellum and the brainstem blocks (a posterior circulation infarct), there is often no crooked mouth and no paralysed arm. There is only spinning, veering while walking, double vision (seeing two of everything), and choking on food and drink, which is the easiest kind to put down to neck trouble, low blood sugar or an inner-ear problem and so delay. Missing your nose and not being able to stand with your eyes closed are signs the cerebellum is involved. But no action can rule a stroke out the other way round: if it came on suddenly and does not ease, call 120, and write down the exact time of onset. That study only went through the records of one hospital, and the authors state themselves that it needs further validation

### 5. If one eye suddenly goes dark as though a curtain came down, go to the emergency department the same day and treat it as a stroke, even if it clears up by itself in a few minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The emergency assessment and the vessel scans take half a day to a day.
- In plain words: One eye suddenly going dark as though a curtain came down means a vessel at the back of the eye has blocked, and the longer it stays blocked the less retina can be saved. It is also a form of a lack of blood to the brain, so a stroke easily follows over the next few days. Among people assessed the same day and started on treatment the same day, the share who had another stroke within 90 days went from 10.3% down to 2.1%. Go the same day to an emergency department at a hospital with a stroke centre, rather than waiting to book an eye clinic the next day.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association's 2021 scientific statement on central retinal artery occlusion (the main vessel at the back of the eye being blocked) states plainly: "acute central retinal artery occlusion is an emergency". Care systems should recognise it as an emergency and order it by urgency. The longer the blood is cut off, the less retina can still be saved. The Oxford EXPRESS study looked at two groups of patients: minor stroke, and a lack of blood to the brain whose symptoms eased by themselves (called a transient ischaemic attack in medicine). After these patients were switched to same-day assessment and same-day start of treatment, the share who had another stroke within 90 days went from 10.3% (32/310) down to 2.1% (6/281)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282-e294. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366> ; Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study): a prospective population-based sequential comparison. Lancet, 370(9596), 1432-1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2>
- Notes: B is assigned because EXPRESS was not a randomised trial. It compared two stretches of time in the same population, before and after the way of working changed, and other conditions may have changed between the two stretches as well, which cannot be separated out. A few minutes of blackness in one eye that then clears up by itself is the easiest thing to wave off as "just tiredness" or "low blood sugar". It is a form of transient ischaemic attack, and the next few days are exactly the window in which strokes are common. Losing vision in one eye with no pain, no redness and no watering deserves particular attention. Do not wait to book an eye clinic the next day; go straight to the emergency department of a hospital with a stroke centre.

### 6. If one eye is swollen, painful and red, you see a ring like a rainbow around lights, and you also have a headache and feel sick, go to an eye emergency department the same day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Measuring the pressure in the eye and examining the front of the eye at an eye emergency department takes half a day.
- In plain words: This is an acute attack of glaucoma. The fluid inside the eye cannot drain out, and the pressure in the eye shoots up. The longer it is left, the more of the optic nerve is crushed, and what is destroyed does not come back. Patients during the Wuhan lockdown waited an average of 241 hours before being treated, and 21.87% of them went blind. In the same period the following year, with no lockdown, the average wait was 121 hours and 7.84% went blind.
- Benefit: An eye hospital in Wuhan compared patients with acute primary angle-closure glaucoma over two stretches of time. In the 76 days of lockdown in 2020 there were 54 people and 64 eyes, and in the same period of 2021 there were 46 people and 51 eyes, and the two groups were similar in their population characteristics. From symptoms appearing to receiving treatment, the lockdown group took 241.84±211.95 hours and the comparison group 121.53±96.12 hours (P=0.001). The pressure in the eye at presentation was 52.63±12.45 mmHg against 45.16±9.79 mmHg (P=0.001). The share who went blind was 21.87% against 7.84%. Glaucomatous optic nerve damage was found in 20/64 eyes (31.25%) against 7/51 eyes (13.73%) (P=0.03). A hospital in South Korea reviewed 50 eyes that had had one attack and later had the lens removed. A year later, 25 of them (50%) were left with a visual field defect of some degree. The longer the stretch from symptoms appearing to the pressure being brought down, the more likely a defect was left (P=0.005). In a separate prospective study, 43 people with a shallow front chamber of the eye lay face down in a dark room for 1 hour. The pressure rose clearly in both eyes (P<0.01). In the eye they mainly used day to day the median rise was 3.60 mmHg, and in the other eye 2.70 mmHg (P<0.05)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Zhou L, Wu S, Wang Y, Bao X, Peng T, Luo W, Ortega-Usobiaga J (2022). Clinical presentation of acute primary angle closure during the COVID-19 epidemic lockdown. Frontiers in Medicine, 9, 1078237. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2022.1078237> ; Sung MS, Kim HJ, Park SW (2023). Predictors of long-term visual field outcome after an episode of acute primary angle closure. Clinical & Experimental Ophthalmology, 51(4), 291-299. <https://doi.org/10.1111/ceo.14206> ; Wang J, Wang J, Ng TK, Huang C (2025). Asymmetric intraocular pressure changes in dominant and contralateral eyes: the dark room prone provocative test. Seminars in Ophthalmology, 40(4), 325-331. <https://doi.org/10.1080/08820538.2024.2443972>
- Notes: There are two reasons B is assigned. One is that the two Wuhan groups were compared by going through old records, and the lockdown itself brought other differences with it, which cannot be separated from the delay. The other is that the Korean group was only 50 eyes, and was retrospective as well. What sets it off is not looking at screens; it is two things on top of each other: the pupil widens in the dark, and bending the head down or lying face down for a long time pushes the lens forward. People whose front chamber is already shallow are easily set off this way, so the ones really at high risk are people over 50, people who are far-sighted, and people with a family member who has had angle-closure glaucoma. A young person gaming all night will basically not have an attack like this. The commonest wrong call during an attack is gastroenteritis, migraine or high blood pressure, because the headache and vomiting are more striking than the eye symptoms. Losing vision in one eye with no pain and no redness is a different matter, see §13.5 (one eye suddenly goes dark). On whether blue-light glasses do anything, see §6.16 (blue-light glasses).

### 7. If pressing pain, tightness or constriction in the chest lasts more than 15 minutes without easing, call 120; do not tough it out and do not drive yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing.
- In plain words: Pressing pain, tightness or constriction in the chest that lasts more than 15 minutes without easing — call 120. Among people whose vessel was opened within 55 minutes of reaching hospital, 2.8% died within 30 days. Among those past 55 minutes it was 9.7%. Every stretch of delay means another piece of heart muscle dying. Do not drive yourself, because losing consciousness on the way is a second accident.
- Benefit: Israel ran a national survey of acute coronary syndrome. The patients analysed had ST-elevation myocardial infarction, which is one kind of heart attack. What was measured was the interval from entering the hospital to the blocked vessel being opened with a balloon (balloon dilatation). Where the interval was ≤55 minutes, 5/178 (2.8%) died within 30 days. Where the interval was >55 minutes, 15/155 (9.7%) died within 30 days
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Karkabi B, Meir G, Zafrir B, et al. (2021). Door-to-balloon time and mortality in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction. European Heart Journal - Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjqcco/qcaa037>
- Notes: What that study compared was the delay after reaching hospital, not the stretch from onset to reaching hospital. But the reasoning is the same: every delay means another piece of heart muscle dying. The pain may sit in the upper abdomen, the back or the jaw, and it may be numbness in the left arm. People with diabetes and older people may have no pain at all, only breathlessness. Do not drive yourself, because losing consciousness on the way is a second accident

### 8. If a sudden tearing, ripping pain moves from the chest round to the back and down to the waist, call 120 and say clearly that "the pain is moving"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing.
- In plain words: A tearing pain moving from the chest round to the back and down to the waist — when you call 120, say straight out "it is like being ripped open, and the pain is moving down". Among people with this illness who have no operation and only take drugs, 58% die. Handling it is the opposite of handling a heart attack: only if the doctor thinks of it will they order a contrast CT instead of giving you anticoagulants and thrombolysis. If the blood pressure in the two arms differs a lot, or you cannot feel a pulse on one side, say that too.
- Benefit: The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD) collected 464 cases from 12 centres. Aortic dissection is the illness with that ripping pain in the chest and back. Among the symptoms patients mentioned first, the commonest was sudden severe sharp pain. Overall in-hospital mortality was 27.4%. Among type A dissections that had no operation and only drugs, mortality was 58%
- Evidence: B

- Notes: Dissection and heart attack are handled in exactly opposite ways. Blood pressure that differs a lot between the two arms, and no pulse on one side, are pointers. Again, do not drive yourself, and do not endure it until the next day

### 9. If a sudden "worst headache of my life" reaches its peak within an hour, go to the emergency department at once for a head CT
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Seeing the emergency department itself costs little. A head CT in the emergency department is a few hundred yuan.
- In plain words: What you look at is how fast it reaches its worst. Among severe headaches that peak within an hour, about 6% are a burst vessel on the surface of the brain. Over 40, a painful or stiff neck, someone saw you faint, onset during activity, exploding in an instant, not being able to bend your neck forward — if one of these fits, go to the emergency department for a head CT. Most people who go have nothing wrong, but missing it once and bleeding again means death.
- Benefit: 10 emergency departments in Canada ran a prospective cohort study of 2131 cases of "severe headache reaching its worst within an hour", setting the criteria in advance and then tracking and recording all along. 132 of them (6.2%) were subarachnoid haemorrhage (a burst vessel on the surface of the brain). The Ottawa SAH rule has six items: aged 40 and over, a painful or stiff neck, someone saw the person faint. The other three are onset during activity, an instantaneous exploding onset, and limited forward bending of the neck on the doctor's examination. Meeting any one of them means investigating. The rule picked out every person who had bled, a sensitivity of 100%, 95% CI 97.2%–100.0% (the range the figure can credibly lie in). The price is that only 15.3% of the people who had not bled were ruled out by it (a specificity of 15.3%)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. JAMA, 310(12), 1248-1255. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278018>
- Notes: What you look at is not "how much it hurts" but "how fast it reached its worst", and only a headache that explodes within seconds to a minute is the danger sign, while a migraine building up slowly is not. Vomiting, a stiff neck, a dislike of light, or a brief faint along with it make it more serious still. A specificity of only 15.3% means that the great majority of people who go and get investigated on this rule turn out to have nothing. But missing it once and bleeding again means death, so it is worth investigating. Do not take a painkiller to push it down and go back to sleep

### 10. If an older person becomes unsteady on their feet, slow, sleepy, or weak on one side two or three weeks to a few months after a knock on the head, get a head CT

- Cost: A head CT costs two to three hundred yuan.
- In plain words: After an older person knocks their head, the blood usually does not collect until the third week. They can be completely fine at the time of the knock, and may not even remember it themselves. After surgery for this illness the recurrence rate can reach 33% and the death rate can reach 32%. If you find an older person suddenly muddled, unsteady on their feet, always wanting to sleep, or weak in one hand, first go back and ask whether they have knocked their head in the last two or three months, and then get a CT.

- Notes: The person can be completely fine at the time of the knock, and an older person may not even remember knocking their head. So when the family notices that "lately they have suddenly got muddled, unsteady on their feet, always wanting to sleep, weak in one hand", first go back and ask whether they have fallen or knocked their head in the last two or three months, and then get a CT. Do not simply put it down to "dementia" or "aftereffects of the last stroke", because putting it down to the wrong thing causes delay. Older people taking aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin or one of the newer oral anticoagulants are at higher risk, and so are long-term heavy drinkers. ### 11. If one leg suddenly swells up, feels tight and hurts when pressed, see a doctor as soon as you can; if sudden breathlessness or chest pain is added, call 120 at once

- Cost: An ultrasound of the leg vessels (a lower-limb venous ultrasound) costs one to two hundred yuan.
- In plain words: If one leg suddenly swells up, is a size thicker than the other, and hurts when pressed, go as soon as you can for an ultrasound of the leg veins. If sudden breathlessness, chest pain on a deep breath, a fast heartbeat or dizziness then appear, the clot may already have travelled to the lungs, and you call 120 straight away. Among people with a confirmed pulmonary embolism, 17.4% die within 3 months. Sitting still for more than 4 hours roughly doubles the risk, but spread over one person it is still very low.
- Benefit: The International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER) collected 2454 patients with confirmed acute pulmonary embolism from 52 hospitals in 7 countries. Pulmonary embolism is a clot blocking a pulmonary artery. In this group, death from any cause within 3 months was 17.4% (426/2454). The World Health Organization's WRIGHT project: travel of 4 hours or more at a stretch makes the risk of a clot forming in a vein and travelling to the lungs (venous thromboembolism) "roughly double". But spread over one person the chance is still very low, and for sitting still for more than 4 hours it is "about one in six thousand"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Goldhaber SZ, Visani L, De Rosa M (1999). Acute pulmonary embolism: clinical outcomes in the International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER). - Notes: When both legs swell together, it is usually a problem with the heart, liver, kidneys or the working of the veins. One leg suddenly swelling and being a size thicker than the other is what a deep vein thrombosis typically looks like. The people who run into trouble are those on long coach or plane journeys, in bed after surgery or a fracture, in a plaster cast, pregnant or around childbirth, with a tumour, or taking oral contraceptives. At that point call 120 straight away. 17.4% is the death rate among confirmed pulmonary embolism cases, not "the risk once your leg swells up", so do not frighten yourself with that number.

### 12. In major bleeding press hard on the wound with your hand first, put a tourniquet on a limb you cannot press shut, and call 120 at the same time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A windlass tourniquet costs tens to a bit over a hundred yuan, and you keep one in the car and one in the first-aid kit. Learning once to "put it above the wound, twist until the bleeding stops, note the time" takes a few minutes.
- In plain words: Press hard first with your hand or with clothing, put a tourniquet on a limb you cannot press shut, then call 120, and once it is on you do not loosen it. Among battlefield casualties, nine in ten of those who had a tourniquet put on before shock lived, while only one in ten of those who had it put on after shock lived, and all 5 people who needed one and did not get one died. Put it above the wound, twist until the bleeding stops, and note the time. Do not use a shoelace or a cord instead. On the neck and the trunk you can only press, you cannot tie.
- Benefit: 11 level-one trauma centres in Texas in the United States took in 1,026 cases of limb vessel injury over 6 years, and 181 of them (17.6%) had a tourniquet put on before reaching hospital. Counted straight, with no adjustment at all, 3.9% of those who had a tourniquet died and 5.2% of those who did not. After the other conditions were levelled out (multivariable adjustment), having a tourniquet was still independently linked with surviving. The adjusted OR was 5.86, and the range the figure can credibly lie in is 95% CI 1.41 to 24.47. Of 232 casualties at a field hospital in Baghdad: 90% of those who had a tourniquet put on before shock appeared survived, against only 10% of those who had it put on after shock. All 5 people who needed a tourniquet and did not get one died, and 87% of those who got one survived. The order is: direct pressure, with clothing or a towel, anything will do → a tourniquet for bleeding from a limb you cannot press shut → call 120 → do not loosen it
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Teixeira PGR 等 (2018). Civilian Prehospital Tourniquet Use Is Associated with Improved Survival in Patients with Peripheral Vascular Injury. Journal of the American College of Surgeons. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamcollsurg.2018.01.047> ; Kragh JF 等 (2009). Survival with emergency tourniquet use to stop bleeding in major limb trauma. - Notes: Do not use a thin thing like a cord or a shoelace as a tourniquet, because it will not hold an artery shut and it cuts the skin open as well. Once a tourniquet is on, do not loosen it every so often to "let some blood through". Bleeding on the neck and the trunk can only be pressed, not tied. In that battlefield data the tourniquet itself did not cost anyone a limb, and there were 4 cases of temporary nerve palsy. The Chinese emergency physicians' expert consensus says the same thing: once it is on before hospital, loosening it is not advised unless there is another way to stop the bleeding completely. For bleeding from the upper arm, tie it on the upper middle third of the upper arm, and for bleeding from the thigh, tie it at the top of the thigh. Do not tie it on the forearm, the lower leg, the elbow or the knee. The consensus advises keeping the total time before and after reaching hospital under 2 hours as far as possible, but where you cannot reach hospital and have no other way to stop the bleeding, do not undo it until proper rescue arrives. A tourniquet improvised from a belt, a tie or a sleeve is very hard to stop bleeding with on its own, and needs a stick to twist it tight.

### 16. If someone collapses in convulsions, clear the space around them, lay them on their side, and time it by the clock; do not put anything in their mouth

### 20. If someone swallows cleaning product, pesticide or medicine by mistake, do not make them vomit; take the bottle and go for medical help at once; if it splashes in the eyes or on the skin, rinse with plenty of clean water for at least 15 minutes

### 22. If someone in the heat is dizzy, feels sick, has stopped sweating or is not fully conscious, move them into the shade at once, take their clothes off and pour water on them to cool them; do not give water to someone who is not fully conscious, and call 120
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing.
- In plain words: Skin that is hot and red, slurred speech and confusion mean heatstroke. From dizziness to unconsciousness can be only a few tens of minutes, and the death rate is extremely high. Move the person at once into a cool, airy place, take their clothes off, and cool them with cool water and wet towels, while calling 120. Do not give a person who is not fully conscious a single mouthful of water, because it will go down the wrong way.
- Benefit: The US National Weather Service: heatstroke is a "severe medical emergency". It shows as a body temperature above 103°F (39.4°C), confusion, slurred speech, and hot red skin. What you do is call the emergency services at once, move the person somewhere cool, cool them with cool water and wet cloths, and "do not give fluids". Heat exhaustion shows as heavy sweating, weakness, cold clammy skin, and a fast weak pulse, and what you do is move the person somewhere cool, loosen their clothes, and have them sip water; get medical help if they vomit, get worse, or do not ease within 1 hour. The Yunnan provincial disease control administration advises: move the person at once to a cool, airy place, cool them with a cold wet towel, give a salted drink if they are conscious, and call the emergency number at once if the symptoms are severe; severe heatstroke "has an extremely high death rate". China CDC advises: cool first, move second, and cool within the "golden half hour" after the patient loses consciousness. Where it is possible, a young adult with no underlying illness can be cooled fast by immersion in cold water, while for older people and people with underlying illness you spray water on the skin and let evaporation do the cooling. The Wilderness Medical Society's 2024 update to its heat illness guideline says cold water immersion is the most important way to treat severe heatstroke and cools fastest. ### 23. As long as a person with heatstroke is conscious, give them cool lightly salted water or a sports drink in small amounts often; do not pour it down them and do not give alcohol or sugary drinks
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->

### 24. In a fire crawl along the floor, feel a door before you open it, do not open a door that is hot, take the stairs and not the lift, and once you are out do not go back

Open a door slowly, and if thick smoke comes through, shut it at once. Take the stairs and not the lift, because the moment the power goes you are trapped inside. Once you are out, do not go back for your things. If your clothes catch fire, stop, drop and roll, covering your face with your hands.
- Benefit: The US FEMA escape guidance reads: "get down on the floor and crawl towards the exit underneath the smoke". ### 25. If you see someone drowning, shout for help and call 110/120 first, hold out a pole and throw something that floats, and do not go into the water yourself

### 26. If someone chokes and cannot speak, stand behind them and give 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts; if they go down, do CPR

### 30. In an earthquake, look first at what kind of building you are in: in a block built to the earthquake-resistant standard, take cover where you are, and in an old building that could come down, with the door a few steps away, get out

- Cost: Costs nothing. Look in advance at how many steps it takes to get out of your home, which way you would go, and which table is solid.
- In plain words: Look first at what kind of building you are in. In a block built to the standard after 2001, where getting out means going down stairs and through an entrance hall, take cover where you are: protect your head and neck, get under a solid table, and hold on to a table leg. In a single-storey house, an old brick house, or a self-built house with no earthquake-resistant design, where the door is a few steps away, get out. In every case, do not jump from a window, do not take the lift, and do not go down stairs while it is still shaking.

- Sources: 中国地震局（安徽省地震局制作）. 地震来了，是躲还是跑？(public information video, transcribed from the subtitles). <https://www.cea.gov.cn/cea/dzpd/dzcs/5537260/index.html>；Armenian HK, Noji EK, Oganesian AP (1992). ### 31. If you meet a bear, a wild boar or a pack of feral dogs, do not run; turn side-on and back away slowly

### 33. Go up onto the plateau slowly, by sleeping altitude (how high you sleep that night), and go back down if the symptoms get worse instead of toughing it out

### 34. If a tick is attached to you, grip it against the skin with fine-tipped tweezers and pull it out steadily; do not smear oil on it and do not burn it

### 35. Boil all water you drink in the wild, a full minute at a rolling boil is enough, and you do not have to boil it longer because the altitude is high

### 44. If a baby under 1 is unresponsive and not breathing normally, put the phone on speaker and call 120, and do infant CPR: 30 compressions to 2 breaths

- Cost: Costs nothing. Infant CPR needs one practice session on a course, half a day to a day. The hard parts are breathing into a child's mouth and nose, and not daring to press hard.
- In plain words: If tapping the soles of the feet gets no response and the baby is not breathing normally, put the phone on speaker and call 120, and keep your hands going. Encircle the chest with both hands and press with the two thumbs, or use the heel of one hand if you cannot get your hands around, pressing down about 4 centimetres, about 2 times a second. After every 30 compressions, seal your mouth over the baby's mouth and nose and give 2 breaths. A baby's heart usually stops because of a breathing problem, so more babies survive when the CPR includes breaths.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association and the American Academy of Pediatrics issued their 2025 paediatric basic life support guideline. The guideline says that for cardiac arrest in babies and children, the main cause is still a problem with breathing. Among babies whose heart stopped outside hospital, only 6.6% left hospital alive. An ordinary person who comes across a baby or child who is unresponsive, not breathing normally and showing no signs of life "should begin CPR immediately", rather than feeling for a pulse. The compression depth is at least one third of the front-to-back diameter of the chest, and for a baby "about 1.5 inches (4 centimetres)". The rate is 100 to 120 times a minute. With one rescuer, 30 compressions to 2 breaths. With two rescuers, 15 compressions to 2 breaths. For a baby you press with the heel of one hand, or with both hands encircling the chest and the two thumbs pressing. Where you cannot get your hands around, use the heel of your hand. The two-finger technique has been dropped because it does not press deep enough. The guideline also says that for babies and children in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, adding breaths to the compressions improves survival. A registry in the United States counted 3900 children under 18 in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, 59.4% of them babies. Where the people present did CPR, the adjusted share who left hospital alive was 13.2%, against 9.5% where they did not. The adjusted OR was 1.57 (the odds of surviving were about 57% higher), 95% CI 1.25–1.96 (the range the figure can credibly lie in). Among babies, CPR with breaths was linked with higher survival, and the children who lived also recovered better in the brain. Compression-only CPR gave a result much the same as nobody doing anything. The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' 2025 international first aid guidelines say: to see whether a baby responds, pick the child up or tap the soles of their feet. If you are alone, put the phone on speaker and call the emergency services yourself. If you have no phone to hand that lets you press and call at the same time, do 1 minute of CPR first and then go and call. The Capital Institute of Pediatrics has a public information piece, reprinted by the Beijing municipal health commission. It says: "if there is only 1 person present, do 2 minutes of CPR first and then call for a 120 ambulance". The compression depth is "4cm for a baby". Under 1 year old, the breaths must be "mouth to mouth and nose"

- Notes: B is assigned because the survival figures come from a registry study that only tracked and recorded, and because they are worked out for all children, with no separate figure for babies given in the abstract. The benefit is set to "large" from the figures for all children: leaving hospital alive went from 9.5% to 13.2%, which is about four tenths more in relative terms. Almost all of the benefit goes to your own child. There are three differences from §13.1 (press hard on the chest). One is that a baby needs breaths, because compressions without breaths work poorly in babies. Two is that you use the two thumbs or the heel of one hand and press about 4 centimetres, rather than pressing with both hands stacked. Three is that when you are alone and cannot press and phone at the same time, you press first and then go and call. How long to press first differs between bodies: the single-rescuer procedure from the Capital Institute of Pediatrics and the American guideline is 2 minutes, and the International Red Cross says 1 minute. Putting the phone on speaker removes the question. The European Resuscitation Council teaches 5 breaths first and then compressions, and the American guideline is compressions first and then breaths, and both beat doing nothing. Older tutorials in China teach two-finger compressions, which the 2025 American guideline has dropped. Where choking caused it, look in the mouth before you give breaths, and only take out an object you can see, see §13.43 (a baby chokes).
