# A high-value guide to living

How to live longer and get sick less, and what to do first when an accident happens. How to spend less money for nothing, and which things get you cheated or dragged into a lawsuit. What you can claim when you have no job and no money, and which procedures you need to open a shop, a company or a website. Also dating, marriage, having a child, going abroad, and learning a trade. The parts on law, medical insurance and social insurance follow the rules now in force in mainland China.<br>
672 suggestions. Each one says what you spend, what you get back, and how hard the evidence is. Sources cite journal papers and official documents only. A few cases that officials do not publish cite a signed original news report, with a web archive.

You do not have to do all of them. This is a menu ranked by value for money, not a task list. Taking one or two away already counts. The author has not done most of them either.

**This project has never issued, and will not issue, any token or digital asset.** A coin put out under this project's name has nothing to do with this project. The author does not take, and does not claim, any related proceeds. Treat that kind of coin as a scam. Do not buy it. See [§5.45 (do not spend money on virtual currency)](book/05.md).

Unofficial English translation in this fork. The [Chinese original](../../README.md) is the version that counts. Laws, subsidies and cut-off dates move. If the two differ, follow the Chinese.

## How to read it

- **You do not have to do all of them.** This is a menu ranked by value for money, not a task list. Taking one or two away already counts. Leave the rest. Come back when you need them. "Easy to say, hard to do" is a fair comment. The author has not done most of them either. They are written down so they can be found when you need them. If you want the ones that take the least effort, see the item below on "only the most worth doing".
- **If you want an AI to look it up:** the repository has a Chinese skill ([skills/life-decision-guide](../../skills/life-decision-guide/)). Claude Code and Codex can both install it. After that, ask straight out whether to sign a guarantee for a friend, or whether a two-hour commute each day is worth it. It looks the related entries up in the text first, then ranks the answer the way the book counts, and says which section and which entry. If it cannot find it, it says so. It does not invent numbers. How to install it is in [that folder's note](../../skills/life-decision-guide/README.md).
- **If you want to filter:** open the [online search page](https://eternity4719.github.io/HowToLiveBetter/). You can filter by keyword, section and evidence grade, and also by whether it costs money, how much time it takes, and whether it takes willpower. The conditions stack. The page reads the text in `book/` directly. Change the text and the page follows. This fork's reader, with an English and Vietnamese switch, is [i18n/index.html](../index.html).
- **Entries point at each other** ("see section 8, entry 17"). On the search page that pointer has a dotted line. Click it and the title and the plain-words line of the entry it points at show in place. To actually go there, press "jump". If that entry is hidden by the filter, the page clears the filter. Reading the text on GitHub, the link does not jump, but every pointer says what it points at. You know which entry it is without turning the page.
- **If you want to read in order:** inside each section the entries run from highest value to lowest. Start with the first few of each section.
- **If you want it offline, or to send to someone:** download the [offline single-file HTML](https://github.com/eternity4719/HowToLiveBetter/releases/download/epub-latest/HowToLiveBetter.html). The whole Chinese book, with search and filters, is in that one file. Double-click to open. No server and no network. It can also be sent in WeChat.
- **If you want to print, or to turn pages on a phone:** download the [PDF](https://github.com/eternity4719/HowToLiveBetter/releases/download/epub-latest/HowToLiveBetter.pdf). A4, more than two hundred pages, with a contents, page numbers and bookmarks. Each section starts on a new page.
- **If you want it on a Kindle or another reader:** download the [EPUB](https://github.com/eternity4719/HowToLiveBetter/releases/download/epub-latest/HowToLiveBetter.epub). On a Kindle, send it with Send to Kindle.
- **All three are rebuilt automatically after each update of the text.** The download links stay fixed. A copy you forward does not update with them. The online version is the one that counts.
- **If you cannot read the string of numbers:** every entry has a "in plain words" line. It turns the research wording in the benefit column into an everyday sentence, such as "the chance of dying in the same period is about two tenths lower" or "detention for a few days, and a fine of so much". It uses only what the benefit column already says. It adds no new number. That one line is enough to decide. The benefit column keeps every original figure, for you to check.
- **If you only want the hardest conclusions:** on the search page tick evidence grade A. That leaves the 438 entries that have a concrete number from a pooling of studies or a large trial.
- **If you only want the most worth doing:** tick the value band "extremely high". That gives 114 entries that cost no money, take no time, need no willpower, and whose benefit sits in the largest band. Add one "what you get back", and that is the priority list for that measure.
- **A section title that starts with "do not" is not a warning to tense up.** The section title is the thing that section tries to prevent (do not die early, do not waste time). It does not mean every entry below tells you not to do something. The action is in the entry title. It starts with a verb, and it already says whether to do it or not to do it. One section has both. Section 4 has both "write 'I mean to do it' as 'at what time, where, and when this happens, do that'" and "do not watch television and rolling news". Read the entry title. Do not fit it to the tone of the section title.

An entry looks like this:

```markdown
### 5. Change the salt at home to a low-sodium salt (a potassium salt)
- Cost: One bag costs a few yuan more than ordinary salt. Swap it in while you are buying. It takes no extra time. The taste barely changes.
- In plain words: For someone who has had a stroke, or someone over 60 with high blood pressure, changing the salt at home to a low-sodium salt makes the chance of dying within five years about 12% lower, and stroke about 14% lower.
- Benefit: A trial that split people at random into two groups, done in rural China. 20995 people, all of whom had had a stroke or were over 60 with high blood pressure, followed for 4.74 years. Result: the low-sodium group, compared with the ordinary-salt group, had a risk of death about 12% lower (RR 0.88). Stroke about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events about 13% lower (RR 0.87).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Neal B 等 (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675
- Notes: Disputed. The people in the trial were older people at high risk. A healthy young person who changes salt gets a much smaller benefit. Someone with poor kidney function, or someone taking a potassium-sparing drug, should ask a doctor before changing salt.
```

## Run a copy yourself

```bash
git clone https://github.com/eternity4719/HowToLiveBetter.git
cd HowToLiveBetter
python -m http.server 8000
```

Then open `http://localhost:8000/` in a browser. The search page is purely static. `README.md` and `book/` are its data. There is no backend, no database, and nothing to install. Drop the whole directory on any static server (Nginx, GitHub Pages, object storage) and it works the same. `index.html` has to be opened over http. Double-clicking the local file gives a blank page, because the browser will not let a page read local files. For that, use the offline single-file version above. ## Four resources

What this book tries to help you keep is not only years of life. There are four things:

- **Years of life:** live longer, and die less of things that could have been avoided
- **Time and energy:** do not spend the time you are alive on things that pay nothing, and lose less of each day's attention and strength for nothing
- **Money:** spend less for nothing, and spend money where the return is settled
- **Personal freedom:** do not send yourself into a detention house or a detention centre because you did not know one red line

Every suggestion answers two questions. What do you spend (money / time / energy / willpower)? What can you get back (a change in total mortality / a drop in a particular cause of death / time and energy saved / money saved / protection and personal freedom)? Entries are ranked by value, not by category. The ones that cost almost nothing and give a large benefit sit at the front.

**Who a suggestion's benefit lands on is graded.** By "how likely this benefit is to come back to you later", from high to low: (1) **you yourself**; (2) **your spouse and lineal relatives** (parents, children, grandparents, grandchildren); (3) **friends, colleagues and other relatives** — a reciprocal tie, help that goes out may come back; (4) **strangers** — the lowest grade, but not zero. The chance of a return is small, you do not know their character, and there is also the side of being cheated, bitten back, or retaliated against. Different grades are not added together. When the text reaches grade (4), it writes the benefit and the risk together.

Read the emergency section that way. Of 38,227 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests in China, 79.2% happened at home. Learning to press is first of all so you can press on someone in your own family. Rules such as "if you see someone drowning, do not go in yourself" and "if you come across a fight, do not step in and pull them apart" are self-protection rules. They stop you turning from a bystander into the second injured person. Whether to act for a stranger is your own weighing. The entry writes the exemption, the self-protection move and the risk. It does not count them for you as a reason you must act.

Figures for mortality, for time and energy, for money, and for legal consequences are each counted on their own. They are not converted into each other. These four match the four "what you get back" filters on the search page. They are not ranked against each other.

## Evidence grades

Every suggestion is marked with an evidence grade:

| Grade | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A | A concrete number you can look up. The source is a pooling of many studies, a large cohort that followed many people for many years, or a trial that split people at random (an RCT). It can say how much it fell (HR, RR, a percentage drop) |
| B | Research supports it, but it cannot give one exact number; or only a small sample, or a single study, holds it up |
| C | The author's own experience, or a practice people generally accept, with no direct research paper |

Of the 672 entries in the book, 438 are grade A, 179 are grade B, and 55 are grade C. Another 70 are marked disputed, and 3 places are marked TODO, to be verified. A disputed A or B entry is marked "disputed" and lists the evidence on the other side. Every source cites a primary document only (a journal paper with a DOI or a PubMed link, or a report from an official body such as WHO, CDC or the national statistics office). It does not cite a second-hand retelling. For cases officials do not publish, a few entries cite an original news report with a journalist's byline, used only to show that "this kind of thing happened". The source line has a web archive, and the notes say "media report only". An uncertain number is marked "to be verified".

Grade A only means "there is a concrete number, and the source can be checked". It does not mean the number is certainly causal. Grade A includes both trials that split people at random and studies that only followed and recorded, with no groups. The latter cannot tell whether the thing did the work, or whether the people who do the thing were healthier to begin with. Which one it is sits in the benefit column. "Split at random into two groups" is the first. "Only recorded, no groups" is the second. Grade A on a legal or policy entry means it quotes the text of a statute or an official document.

The percentages in the benefit column and the plain-words line are mostly "a few tenths lower than people who did not do it", not "a few fewer out of every hundred people". The same 12% lower takes far more deaths off older people whose risk was already high than off young people whose risk was already very low. So first see which people the plain-words line names, then look at the percentage.

## Value bands

The evidence grade only answers "can this number be trusted". It does not answer "is this worth doing". So each entry also marks two more things: the size of the benefit (how large the good is) and the measure (which kind of thing you get back). The search page takes those two plus the three costs and builds a value band:

| Dimension | Values | How it is set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measure | years of life / money / time and energy / personal freedom | What this entry mainly gets back. **Different measures are not compared.** "Total mortality down 12%" and "500 yuan saved a year" are not on one ruler. They cannot be ranked against each other |
| Size of benefit | large / medium / small | Fitted, as far as possible, from the entry's own benefit column, on lines set in advance, not by feel. Years of life: the percentage drop (≥20% large, 10–20% medium, <10%, or only an intermediate marker and not the final result, small). Money: the amount (ten-thousand-yuan scale large, hundreds to thousands medium, a few dozen yuan small). Personal freedom: the consequence (avoiding criminal liability large, avoiding detention or an administrative penalty medium, avoiding a civil dispute small). Time and energy: how much is saved (hours a day large, hours a week medium, saved only once small) |
| Value | extremely high / high / ordinary | A large benefit and all three costs at zero = extremely high. A large benefit with a cost that is not high, or a medium benefit with zero cost = high. The rest = ordinary |

Of the 672 entries, 114 (17%) are extremely high, 304 (45%) are high, and 254 (38%) are ordinary. The middle band being large is on purpose. The size of the benefit underneath is only large, medium and small. Cutting it finer would be a precision that was made up.

**This band is the author's own judgement, not evidence.** By this book's standard it itself only counts as grade C. It and the evidence grade are two different things. Neither changes the other. An entry can be grade A and only ordinary in value (a phase 3 RCT of the shingles vaccine with 97.2% efficacy, but two doses cost three to four thousand yuan, and shingles is rarely fatal). It can also be only grade C and extremely high in value (before you leave the country, send the itinerary to the family). "Ordinary" does not mean you should not do it. The entries in the book are all suggested. This band only means you weigh for yourself whether that spend is worth it.

## Reading the numbers (glossary)

The text tries to use everyday wording. Citing a study still brings in a few statistical names. If you cannot read one, look it up here. On the online search page, rest the pointer on a word with a dotted line (on a phone, tap it) and an explanation pops up.

<details>
<summary>Open 41 terms (total mortality, HR, RR, 95% CI, pooling of studies, BMI, LPR, earnest money and a booking deposit…)</summary>

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Total mortality | Over a period, what share of a group of people died, whatever the cause. This book uses it to measure "whether you live long". In the research text it is called all-cause mortality, abbreviated ACM |
| HR | Hazard ratio. Over the same period, how fast the group that did something had the event (death, disease), divided by the group that did not. HR 0.87 is 13% lower than the group that did not. HR 1.21 is 21% higher |
| RR | Relative risk. The ratio of how likely the two groups are to have the event. You read the number the same way as HR |
| OR | Odds ratio. Also a comparison of two groups, but the arithmetic differs a little from RR. When the event is rare, it is close to RR. When the event is common, it states the gap as larger than it really is |
| IRR | Ratio of incidence rates. Incidence is what share newly get this disease over a period. Divide the two groups. Read it like RR |
| RaR | Ratio of how many times it happens. It compares how many times the thing happened (for example how many falls), not how many people it happened to. Read it like RR |
| Standardised mortality ratio | Abbreviated SMR. How many of this group actually died, divided by "how many ordinary people of the same age should have died". 5.86 means the deaths are 5.86 times those of people the same age |
| Risk difference | Subtract the two groups' probabilities of the event. You get directly "how many more out of every thousand". A multiple only says how many times over. The risk difference says how many real extra people |
| 95% CI | 95% confidence interval. A number a study works out always has error. This is the range the true situation is likely to fall in. For a multiple, if the range crosses 1, and for a sum or difference, if the range crosses 0, the difference between the two groups may be only chance. The text then writes "not statistically significant" |
| RCT | Randomised controlled trial. People are split into two groups by something like drawing lots. One group does the thing, one does not. Afterwards you compare how far the results differ. To show "this thing did the work", this design is the most persuasive |
| Pooling of studies | Many studies' results are put together and counted again, to get one overall number. Also called a meta-analysis |
| Cohort | A cohort study. A large group is picked and followed for many years, recording who had the event. It can show that two things often appear together. It is not enough to prove that the first caused the second |
| Observational | An observational study. The researchers only record from the side. They do not assign who does it and who does not. The result is easily pulled off by other factors (see confounding and reverse causation in this table). Read the number with a discount |
| Confounding | A third factor pulls both the supposed cause and the result, so the two things look causal. For example, people who like vegetables often also like exercise more. You cannot tell which one is doing the work |
| Reverse causation | The direction of cause is reversed. It looks as if the first thing caused the second. In fact the second caused the first. It is not that sleeping more makes people die early. It is that people who are seriously ill sleep more |
| d, g | Effect size. How large the gap between two groups is. The gap is turned into "how many times a usual amount of wobble": 0.2 counts as small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large |
| r | Correlation coefficient. How tightly two things move together. It runs from -1 to 1. 0.1 counts as weak, 0.3 medium, 0.5 strong |
| MET | A unit of how hard exercise is. Sitting quietly is 1 MET. A brisk walk is about 3 to 4 MET. Written as MET·h, it is intensity times how many hours, the total amount of movement |
| GRADE | A scoring method used internationally, rating how reliable a piece of evidence is, in four bands: high, moderate, low, very low |
| Intention-to-screen analysis | The effect is counted by "who was told to go and be screened", not by "who actually went". Among those told, some never go, so this method counts the benefit of screening, for the people who really went, as lower |
| Pack-years | A unit of how much someone has smoked in total. Packs a day times years smoked. 30 pack-years is one pack a day for 30 years |
| BMI | Body mass index, a number often used for fat and thin: kilograms of weight divided by height in metres squared |
| LDL | Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, commonly called bad cholesterol |
| eGFR | Estimated glomerular filtration rate, a measure of how well the kidneys filter, in mL/min/1.73 m². A lower number means worse kidney function. The stages of chronic kidney disease are set by it |
| HBsAg | Hepatitis B surface antigen, one item on a lab slip. A positive result means the person is already infected with hepatitis B virus |
| HPV | Human papillomavirus. It has many types. Long infection with some of those types can lead to cervical cancer |
| LDCT | Low-dose chest CT. The radiation is about one fifth to one tenth of an ordinary CT |
| PM2.5 | Fine particles floating in the air, diameter under 2.5 micrometres |
| NOVA | A way of classifying food, by how heavily it is processed, into four classes. "Ultra-processed food" is the fourth |
| LPR | Loan prime rate. The benchmark for loan interest in China, published once on the 20th of each month. Mortgage rates and the interest ceiling on private lending are both counted from it |
| One arbitration, then final | The result of a labour arbitration takes effect directly. The employer cannot take the same matter to court |
| Crude marriage rate, crude divorce rate | Out of every thousand people, how many couples registered a marriage, or a divorce, that year. There is also a number called the divorce-to-marriage ratio, divorces that year divided by marriages that year. It is not the same as these two. Do not mix them |
| AED | Automated external defibrillator. The red or yellow first-aid box often seen in public places. After you switch it on, follow the voice. It decides for itself whether to shock |
| CPR | Cardiopulmonary resuscitation. When the heart has stopped, press hard on the chest so the blood keeps moving |
| 3C certification | China Compulsory Certification. Products listed in the catalogue may not leave the factory, and may not be sold, without this mark |
| ICP filing | Before a website or an app goes live formally, one registration in the system of the industry and information ministry |
| Graded protection | The cybersecurity graded-protection system. A system is split into levels by how important it is. The higher the level, the more security measures are required |
| GPL | A kind of open-source licence. Something built with this kind of code, when it is sent out, usually also has to publish its own code |
| Non-compete | An agreement with the company: for a period after you leave, you do not go and work for a rival in the same trade. During that period the company has to pay you compensation every month, at most 2 years |
| Subscribed capital | The money you promise to put in when you register a company. Once promised, it has to be actually paid within the period the law sets. It is not a number written to look good |
| Earnest money and a booking deposit | The two words differ by one character, and the effect differs a lot. Earnest money has a penalty: if the side that took the money goes back on it, they return double, and the amount is at most 20% of the contract. A booking deposit is only money paid early. Going back on it has no such penalty |
